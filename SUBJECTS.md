# BDS Year 1 — Subjects and Reference Textbooks

> A practical reference guide for the BDS 1st Year past-question repository.

**Corrected subject count: 7, not 6.**

`BDS_1st_year_all_subjects_images_oral_bio.md` is **Oral Biology**, not a duplicate or generic "images" file.

This document defines the seven Year 1 subjects, recommended reference textbooks, must-know areas, and how the books should be used alongside the past-question analysis.

# 1. Anatomy

## Core / Must-Know

- B.D. Chaurasia — *Human Anatomy*
  - Vol. 1: Upper Limb & Thorax
  - Vol. 2: Lower Limb, Abdomen & Pelvis
  - Vol. 3: Head, Neck & Brain
- Inderbir Singh — *Human Embryology*
- Inderbir Singh — *Textbook of Human Histology*

## Practical / Dissection Reference

- Cunningham's *Manual of Practical Anatomy* (Vol. 1–3)

## Quick Revision / MCQ

- B.D. Chaurasia — companion MCQ/review books
- Use these alongside the main textbook rather than as a replacement.

## Must-Know Areas

- General anatomy
- Bones and joints
- Muscles and movements
- Upper limb
- Lower limb
- Thorax
- Abdomen and pelvis
- Head and neck
- Neuroanatomy
- Cardiovascular system
- Respiratory system
- Lymphatic system
- Histology
- Embryology
- Developmental anomalies
- Important anatomical diagrams
- Applied/clinical anatomy

## Study Priority

**Primary:** B.D. Chaurasia

**For embryology:** Inderbir Singh

**For histology:** Inderbir Singh

**For practical/dissection:** Cunningham's

# 2. Physiology

## Core / Must-Know

- Guyton & Hall — *Textbook of Medical Physiology*
- A.K. Jain — *Textbook of Physiology* (Vol. 1–2)

A.K. Jain is particularly useful for undergraduate-level depth and examination-oriented preparation in South Asian curricula.

## Supplementary

- Ganong's *Review of Medical Physiology*

## Practical

- A.K. Jain — *Practical Physiology*
- Ghai's *Practical Physiology*

These are useful for hematology experiments, clinical examination procedures, and practical preparation.

## Must-Know Areas

- General physiology
- Cell physiology
- Blood
- Nerve and muscle physiology
- Cardiovascular system
- Respiratory system
- Gastrointestinal system
- Renal physiology
- Endocrine physiology
- Reproductive physiology
- Central nervous system
- Autonomic nervous system
- Special senses
- Homeostasis
- Acid-base balance
- Important physiological mechanisms
- Important graphs and diagrams

## Study Priority

**Primary:** A.K. Jain + Guyton & Hall

**For deeper concepts:** Ganong

**For practical:** A.K. Jain / Ghai

# 4. Microbiology

## Core / Must-Know

- Ananthanarayan & Paniker — *Textbook of Microbiology*

## Supplementary

- Jawetz, Melnick & Adelberg's *Medical Microbiology*

## Must-Know Areas

- General microbiology
- Bacteriology
- Virology
- Mycology
- Parasitology
- Basic immunology

- Culture media
- Bacterial genetics
- Antibiotic susceptibility testing
- Antimicrobial resistance
- Normal flora
- Important bacterial pathogens
- Important viruses
- Important fungi
- Important parasites
- Laboratory diagnosis
- Hypersensitivity
- Vaccines and immunization
- Oral microorganisms

## BDS-Specific Focus

Microbiology at BDS level is generally more focused than a comprehensive MBBS microbiology curriculum.

Give particular attention to:

- Oral flora
- Dental caries-associated organisms
- Periodontal pathogens
- Sterilization and disinfection
- Infection control
- Medically important organisms
- Basic immunology
- Laboratory diagnosis

There is no need to memorize every organism with exhaustive systemic detail unless it appears in the official syllabus or past questions.

## Study Priority

**Primary:** Ananthanarayan & Paniker

**For additional depth:** Jawetz

# 7. Oral Biology

> **Important:** Oral Biology is a separate Year 1 subject in this repository.
>
> `BDS_1st_year_all_subjects_images_oral_bio.md` contains the Oral Biology question-paper extraction.

## Core / Must-Know

- Orban's — *Oral Histology and Embryology*

## Supplementary / Deeper Reference

- Ten Cate's — *Oral Histology: Development, Structure, and Function*
- Wheeler's — *Dental Anatomy, Physiology, and Occlusion*

## Must-Know Areas

### Tooth Development

- Dental lamina
- Bud stage
- Cap stage
- Bell stage
- Apposition
- Mineralization
- Tooth eruption
- Root formation
- Developmental anomalies
- Transient structures
- Reciprocal induction

### Dental Tissues

- Enamel
- Dentin
- Cementum
- Dental pulp
- Periodontal ligament
- Alveolar bone

### Tooth Morphology

- Primary dentition
- Permanent dentition
- Tooth identification
- Tooth numbering systems
- Maxillary teeth
- Mandibular teeth
- Tooth surfaces
- Line angles
- Point angles
- Occlusal anatomy
- Anatomical landmarks

### Oral Histology

- Oral mucosa
- Salivary glands
- Serous and mucous acini
- Saliva
- Periodontium
- Dental pulp
- Enamel structure
- Dentin structure
- Cementum

## Important Distinction

Oral Biology is **not the same as general Histology/Embryology under Anatomy**.

Anatomy covers general human histology and embryology, while Oral Biology focuses specifically on:

- Tooth development
- Tooth morphology
- Enamel
- Dentin
- Pulp
- Cementum
- Periodontium
- Oral tissues
- Salivary glands
- Dental anatomy

Therefore, **do not substitute the general Anatomy histology textbook for Oral Biology preparation.**

## Study Priority

**Primary:** Orban's

**For deeper oral histology:** Ten Cate's

**For tooth morphology and occlusion:** Wheeler's

# How to Use the Books With This Repository

The purpose of the past-question repository is **not to replace textbooks**.

Instead, use the questions to determine which parts of the textbooks deserve priority.

Recommended workflow:

```text
Past Questions
      ↓
Extracted Questions
      ↓
Topic Identification
      ↓
Frequency Analysis
      ↓
Identify High-Frequency Topics
      ↓
Find Topic in Core Textbook
      ↓
Study the Topic
      ↓
Create Answer / Revision Notes
      ↓
Cheatsheet
````

---

## 1. Use Past Questions to Find High-Yield Topics

Once the analysis files are populated:

```text
analysis/year-1/<subject>_top_topics.md
```

compare the high-frequency topics with the chapter structure of the core textbook.

For example:

```text
Past Questions
     ↓
Brachial Plexus appears repeatedly
     ↓
Anatomy analysis identifies it as high-frequency
     ↓
Read Brachial Plexus from B.D. Chaurasia
     ↓
Prepare structured answer
     ↓
Add to revision material
```

# Official Syllabus Takes Priority

This document is a **practical reference guide**, not an official Kathmandu University syllabus or official prescribed-textbook list.

If the university's official syllabus or faculty explicitly specifies different textbooks:

1. Follow the official syllabus.
2. Treat the officially specified textbooks as authoritative.
3. Update this document if necessary.
4. Document significant discrepancies in `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

Textbook recommendations can vary between universities, curricula, batches, and instructors.

---

# Final Principle

The goal is not to read every book cover to cover.

The goal is:

> **Use the core textbook to build understanding, past questions to identify examination priorities, and analysis to determine what deserves the most revision time.**

Supplementary textbooks should be used when the core textbook does not provide enough conceptual depth or when clarification is needed.
