# BDS 1st Year — Anatomy Quick Revision Cheatsheet
*Based on Kathmandu University Past Questions (2015–2022)*

This cheatsheet distills the most frequently tested concepts, anatomical boundaries, histological structures, and clinical correlations from the extracted past papers. Use it for rapid review before your exams.

---

## 1. Head and Neck Anatomy

### Muscles of Mastication
* **Primary Muscles:** All innervated by the **Mandibular nerve ($V_3$)** (anterior trunk, except medial pterygoid which is from main trunk):
  1. **Masseter:**
     * *Origin:* Zygomatic arch (anterior 2/3 lower border, posterior 1/3 deep surface).
     * *Insertion:* Lateral surface of ramus and coronoid process of mandible.
     * *Action:* Elevates mandible (closes jaw), powerful crushing.
  2. **Temporalis:**
     * *Origin:* Temporal fossa (up to inferior temporal line) and temporal fascia.
     * *Insertion:* Coronoid process (apex and medial surface) and anterior border of mandibular ramus.
     * *Action:* Elevation of mandible; posterior horizontal fibers **retract** mandible.
  3. **Medial Pterygoid:**
     * *Origin:* Deep head from medial surface of lateral pterygoid plate and pyramidal process of palatine bone; superficial head from maxillary tuberosity.
     * *Insertion:* Medial surface of mandibular angle and ramus (mirroring masseter).
     * *Action:* Elevates mandible, assists in protraction and side-to-side chewing movements.
  4. **Lateral Pterygoid:**
     * *Origin:* Upper head from infratemporal surface of greater wing of sphenoid; lower head from lateral surface of lateral pterygoid plate.
     * *Insertion:* Pterygoid fovea on anterior neck of condyle and anterior margin of TMJ articular disc/capsule.
     * *Action:* **Depresses** mandible (initiates mouth opening), **protrudes** mandible (bilateral), and produces side-to-side grinding movements (unilateral). *Only muscle of mastication that opens the mouth.*

### Temporomandibular Joint (TMJ)
* **Type:** Atypical Synovial joint (**Bicondylar** or **Ginglymoarthrodial** joint: hinge + sliding). Articular surfaces are covered by **fibrocartilage** (not hyaline).
* **Articular Surfaces:**
  * Upper: Mandibular fossa (glenoid fossa) and articular eminence of temporal bone.
  * Lower: Condyle (head) of mandible.
* **Articular Disc:** Biconcave fibrous plate dividing the joint cavity into two compartments:
  * *Upper compartment:* Translational / gliding movements (protraction and retraction).
  * *Lower compartment:* Rotational / hinge movements (elevation and depression).
  * *Histological zones:* Anterior thick band, intermediate thin zone (avascular/aneural), posterior thick band, bilaminar retrodiscal pad.
* **Ligaments:**
  * *Lateral (Temporomandibular) ligament:* True intrinsic ligament; prevents posterior and inferior displacement of condyle.
  * *Sphenomandibular ligament:* Accessory; from spine of sphenoid to lingula of mandible (remnant of Meckel's cartilage perichondrium).
  * *Stylomandibular ligament:* Accessory; thickened deep cervical fascia from styloid process to mandibular angle.
* **Movements & Muscles:**
  * *Depression (Opening):* Lateral pterygoid, assisted by digastric, geniohyoid, and mylohyoid (gravity).
  * *Elevation (Closing):* Temporalis, masseter, medial pterygoid.
  * *Protraction:* Lateral and medial pterygoids acting together.
  * *Retraction:* Posterior horizontal fibers of temporalis, deep masseter, digastric.
  * *Lateral / Grinding:* Alternate action of medial and lateral pterygoids of opposite sides.

### Triangles of the Neck
* **Carotid Triangle:**
  * *Boundaries:* Superior: Posterior belly of digastric and stylohyoid; Anteroinferior: Superior belly of omohyoid; Posterior: Anterior border of sternocleidomastoid (SCM).
  * *Floor:* Thyrohyoid, hyoglossus, middle and inferior pharyngeal constrictor muscles.
  * *Carotid Sheath Contents:* Common and internal carotid arteries (medially), Internal jugular vein (laterally), Vagus nerve (CN X, posteriorly in the groove between them).
  * *Other Contents:* External carotid artery and its proximal branches (superior thyroid, lingual, facial, ascending pharyngeal, occipital), Hypoglossal nerve (CN XII crossing superficial to arteries), Ansa cervicalis on anterior sheath wall, Internal jugular deep cervical lymph nodes.
* **Digastric (Submandibular) Triangle:**
  * *Boundaries:* Anteroinferior: Anterior belly of digastric; Posteroinferior: Posterior belly of digastric and stylohyoid; Superior: Base of mandible and line joining angle of mandible to mastoid process.
  * *Contents:* Submandibular gland, facial artery (deep/grooving gland), facial vein (superficial to gland), submandibular lymph nodes, hypoglossal nerve, lingual nerve, submandibular ganglion.

### Deep Cervical Fascia & Scalp
* **Deep Cervical Fascia Layers:**
  1. *Investing layer:* Encircles entire neck; splits to enclose SCM, trapezius, parotid gland, and submandibular gland.
  2. *Pretracheal layer:* Encloses thyroid gland, trachea, and esophagus; attaches to thyroid cartilage; moves with deglutition.
  3. *Prevertebral layer:* Covers cervical vertebrae and prevertebral muscles; extends laterally as axillary sheath.
* **Scalp Layers (SCALP):**
  * **S**kin (thick, contains hair follicles, sebaceous glands).
  * **C**onnective tissue (dense, vascular, bleeding vessels held open by fibrous septa).
  * **A**poneurosis epicranialis (Galea aponeurotica connecting frontalis and occipitalis).
  * **L**oose areolar tissue (**"Dangerous Area of Scalp"**): contains valveless emissary veins that communicate with intracranial dural venous sinuses (superior sagittal sinus), allowing superficial scalp infections to spread into cranial cavity causing meningitis or cavernous sinus thrombosis.
  * **P**ericranium (periosteum over skull bones).

### Infratemporal Fossa & Branchial Apparatus
* **Infratemporal Fossa:**
  * *Boundaries:* Anterior: infratemporal surface of maxilla; Posterior: styloid apparatus, carotid sheath; Medial: lateral pterygoid plate; Lateral: ramus of mandible.
  * *Contents:* Lateral and medial pterygoid muscles, Maxillary artery and branches (Middle meningeal artery entering foramen spinosum, inferior alveolar), Mandibular nerve ($V_3$) and branches (lingual, inferior alveolar, buccal, auriculotemporal), Chorda tympani (CN VII joining lingual nerve), Otic ganglion, Pterygoid venous plexus.
* **Pharyngeal (Branchial) Arches Derivatives:**
  * *1st Arch (Mandibular - Nerve $V_3$):* Meckel's cartilage (malleus, incus, sphenomandibular ligament); Muscles of mastication, mylohyoid, anterior belly of digastric, tensor tympani, tensor veli palatini.
  * *2nd Arch (Hyoid - Nerve VII):* Reichert's cartilage (stapes, styloid process, stylohyoid ligament, lesser horn and upper body of hyoid); Muscles of facial expression, stapedius, stylohyoid, posterior belly of digastric.
  * *3rd Arch (Nerve IX):* Greater horn and lower body of hyoid; Stylopharyngeus muscle.
  * *4th & 6th Arches (Nerve X - Superior and Recurrent laryngeal nerves):* Thyroid, cricoid, arytenoid cartilages; Pharyngeal and laryngeal muscles.
* **Pharyngeal Pouches:**
  * 1st: Eustachian tube, middle ear cavity.
  * 2nd: Palatine tonsillar fossa and crypts.
  * 3rd: Inferior parathyroid glands and thymus.
  * 4th: Superior parathyroid glands and ultimobranchial body (parafollicular C cells of thyroid).

## 2. Thorax & Cardiovascular Anatomy

### Internal Features of the Heart
* **Right Atrium:**
  * *Smooth posterior part (Sinus venarum):* Derived embryologically from sinus venosus. Receives SVC, IVC (guarded by rudimentary Eustachian valve), and coronary sinus (guarded by Thebesian valve).
  * *Rough anterior part (Atrium proper):* Features parallel muscular ridges called **pectinate muscles (musculi pectinati)**.
  * *Crista Terminalis:* Internal muscular ridge separating smooth and rough parts; corresponds externally to the **sulcus terminalis**. SA node lies at its upper end.
  * *Interatrial Septum:* Shows the **fossa ovalis** (floor formed by septum primum) surrounded by the prominent upper margin called **limbus fossa ovalis (annulus ovalis)**, formed by septum secundum.
* **Coronary Circulation:**
  * *Right Coronary Artery (RCA):* Arises from anterior aortic sinus. Runs in coronary sulcus. Branches: Marginal artery, Posterior Interventricular Artery (PIVA - supplies posterior 1/3 of interventricular septum; defines right dominance).
  * *Left Coronary Artery (LCA):* Arises from left posterior aortic sinus. Branches: Anterior Interventricular Artery (LAD - "artery of sudden death", supplies anterior 2/3 of septum and apex), Circumflex artery.

### Respiratory Anatomy
* **Bronchopulmonary Segments:** Anatomically and functionally independent units of lung tissue supplied by a tertiary (segmental) bronchus and a tertiary branch of the pulmonary artery.
  * *Right lung:* 10 segments (Apical, Posterior, Anterior in upper lobe; Lateral, Medial in middle lobe; Superior, Medial basal, Anterior basal, Lateral basal, Posterior basal in lower lobe).
  * *Left lung:* 8–10 segments (Apicoposterior, Anterior, Superior lingular, Inferior lingular in upper lobe; Superior, Anteromedial basal, Lateral basal, Posterior basal in lower lobe).
  * *Surgical importance:* Segmental artery is central/intrasegmental; pulmonary veins are **intersegmental** (lie in the connective tissue septa between segments). A segment can be surgically excised without damaging adjacent tissue.
* **Pleural Recesses:**
  * *Costodiaphragmatic recess:* Slit-like space between costal and diaphragmatic pleura; lowest part of pleural cavity (fluid collects here in pleural effusion).
  * *Costomediastinal recess:* Located anteriorly between costal and mediastinal pleura, overlying the cardiac notch.

## 3. Upper Limb & Locomotor Anatomy

### Axilla
* **Boundaries:**
  * *Apex (Cervicoaxillary canal):* Clavicle (anterior), superior border of scapula (posterior), outer border of 1st rib (medial).
  * *Base:* Skin, subcutaneous tissue, axillary fascia.
  * *Anterior wall:* Pectoralis major, pectoralis minor, subclavius, clavipectoral fascia.
  * *Posterior wall:* Subscapularis, teres major, latissimus dorsi.
  * *Medial wall:* Upper 4–5 ribs with intercostal muscles, upper digitations of serratus anterior.
  * *Lateral wall:* Coracobrachialis and biceps brachii in the intertubercular groove of humerus.
* **Contents:** Axillary artery and branches (6 branches), Axillary vein and tributaries, Cords and branches of **Brachial Plexus**, Axillary lymph nodes (anterior, posterior, lateral, central, apical groups), axillary fat and loose areolar tissue.

### Brachial Plexus & Nerve Injuries
* **Formation:** Formed by anterior primary rami of **C5, C6, C7, C8, T1**.
  * *Roots (C5–T1) → Trunks (Upper: C5-C6, Middle: C7, Lower: C8-T1) → Divisions (3 Anterior, 3 Posterior) → Cords (Lateral, Medial, Posterior) → Terminal branches.*
* **Clinical Injuries:**
  * **Erb's Palsy (Upper Trunk Injury - C5, C6):** Caused by birth trauma (excessive traction on head) or fall on shoulder. Affects suprascapular, axillary, and musculocutaneous nerves. Position: **"Policeman's tip" / "Waiter's tip" deformity** — arm adducted, internally rotated; forearm extended and pronated. Loss of abduction, lateral rotation, and elbow flexion.
  * **Klumpke's Palsy (Lower Trunk Injury - C8, T1):** Caused by upward traction on arm during breech delivery or clutching an object while falling from a height. Ulnar and median nerve intrinsic hand branches affected. Result: **True Claw Hand** (hyperextension at MCP joints, flexion at IP joints due to paralysis of lumbricals and interossei). May present with Horner's syndrome if T1 sympathetic fibers are damaged.
  * **Radial Nerve Injury:** Damaged in radial (spiral) groove by fracture of humerus shaft. Result: **Wrist drop** (loss of wrist and finger extensors) and sensory loss on dorsum of 1st web space.

## 4. General Anatomy & Histology

### Bone & Joint Classification
* **Bone Classification by Shape:** Long (humerus, femur), Short (carpals, tarsals), Flat (skull vault, ribs, sternum), Irregular (vertebrae), **Sesamoid** (develop inside tendons, lack periosteum and Haversian system; e.g. Patella, Pisiform).
* **Blood Supply of Long Bone:**
  1. *Nutrient artery:* Enters nutrient foramen directed away from the growing end; supplies inner 2/3 of cortex and bone marrow.
  2. *Metaphyseal arteries:* Arise from periarticular anastomosis; supply metaphysis (most vascular zone; common site of acute hematogenous osteomyelitis).
  3. *Epiphyseal arteries:* Supply the epiphysis.
  4. *Periosteal arteries:* Supply outer 1/3 of cortex.
* **Endochondral (Intracartilaginous) Ossification Stages:**
  1. Formation of hyaline cartilage model.
  2. Periosteal bone collar formation around diaphysis.
  3. Chondrocyte hypertrophy, matrix calcification, and chondrocyte death.
  4. Periosteal bud (blood vessels, osteoprogenitor cells) invades calcified cartilage.
  5. Primary ossification center establishes in diaphysis; secondary ossification centers appear in epiphyses after birth.
  6. Epiphyseal plate zones (resting → proliferation → hypertrophy → calcification → ossification) allow longitudinal bone growth.
* **Synovial Joint Classification:**
  * *Plane:* Intercarpal, intertarsal (gliding).
  * *Hinge (Ginglymus):* Elbow, ankle, interphalangeal (flexion/extension).
  * *Pivot (Trochoid):* Superior radioulnar, atlantoaxial (rotation).
  * *Condylar / Bicondylar:* Knee, TMJ (flexion/extension with limited rotation).
  * *Ellipsoid:* Wrist (radiocarpal), MCP joints (flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, circumduction).
  * *Saddle:* 1st Carpometacarpal joint of thumb.
  * *Ball and Socket (Spheroidal):* Shoulder, hip (multiaxial movements).

### Histology of Lymphoid Organs & Tissues
* **Spleen:**
  * Fibromuscular capsule with trabeculae extending into parenchyma. No cortex and medulla.
  * *White pulp:* Lymphoid nodules (Malpighian corpuscles) with germinal centers, traversed eccentrically by a **central arteriole** surrounded by Periarteriolar Lymphoid Sheath (PALS, T-cell area).
  * *Red pulp:* Splenic cords of Billroth (reticular fibers, macrophages, plasma cells, RBCs) and splenic sinusoids lined by elongated endothelial stave cells.
* **Lymph Node:**
  * Bean-shaped with capsule, subcapsular sinus, afferent lymphatics on convex surface, efferent at hilum.
  * *Outer Cortex:* Lymphoid follicles (primary without germinal center, secondary with germinal center containing B-lymphocytes).
  * *Paracortex:* Interfollicular region containing T-lymphocytes and high endothelial venules (HEVs).
  * *Medulla:* Medullary cords (plasma cells, macrophages) and medullary sinuses.
* **Palatine Tonsil:**
  * Lined on luminal surface by **stratified squamous non-keratinized epithelium**.
  * Epithelium invaginates to form deep, branching **tonsillar crypts** (10–15 in number).
  * Subepithelial lymphoid follicles with germinal centers; partial connective tissue hemicapsule on deep surface; **no afferent lymphatics**.
* **Thymus:**
  * Bilobed; capsule sends septa dividing parenchyma into incomplete lobules.
  * *Cortex:* Densely packed T-lymphocytes (thymocytes).
  * *Medulla:* Lighter staining; contains **Hassall's corpuscles** (concentric layers of degenerated, keratinized epithelial reticular cells; diagnostic hallmark).
* **Skin & Muscle Histology:**
  * *Thick skin (palms/soles):* Thick stratum corneum, clear **stratum lucidum** present, no hair follicles, no sebaceous glands, prominent dermal papillae.
  * *Thin skin:* Thin stratum corneum, lacks stratum lucidum, contains hair follicles, sebaceous glands, and arrector pili muscles.
  * *Skeletal vs Cardiac muscle:* Skeletal has long unbranched fibers, peripheral multinucleation, and no intercalated discs; Cardiac has branched fibers, single central nucleus, and **intercalated discs** with gap junctions.

## 5. General Embryology

### Notochord
* **Formation:**
  * Formed in the 3rd week of development from the primitive node (Hensen's node) of the primitive streak.
  * Epiblast cells invaginate and migrate cranially in the midline between ectoderm and endoderm to form the solid **notochordal process**.
  * The process canalizes into the notochordal canal, fuses with underlying endoderm (neurenteric canal formation), and eventually rounds off into the definitive solid rod called the **notochord**.
* **Fate / Functions:**
  * Defines the primordial longitudinal axis of the embryo and provides structural rigidity.
  * Induces overlying ectoderm to thicken and fold, forming the **neural tube** (neuroectoderm).
  * Forms the primary axis around which vertebral bodies develop.
  * **Adult Remnant:** Persists as the **nucleus pulposus** inside the intervertebral discs.
