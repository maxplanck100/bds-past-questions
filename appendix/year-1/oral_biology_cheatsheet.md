# BDS 1st Year — Oral Biology Quick Revision Cheatsheet
*Based on Kathmandu University Past Questions (2014–2024)*

This cheatsheet distills the most frequently tested concepts, tooth morphological criteria, histological structures, and craniofacial embryological stages from the extracted past papers. Use it for rapid review before your exams.

---

## 1. Dental Anatomy & Tooth Morphology

### Permanent Maxillary Canine ("Cornerstone of the Arch")
* **General Significance:** Longest tooth in the human dentition (crown + root average ~27 mm); largest, longest single root supporting the **canine eminence** of the alveolar ridge.
* **Labial Aspect:**
  * Pentagonal outline.
  * Prominent **labial ridge** extending from cusp tip to cervical line, dividing the labial surface into shallow mesiolabial and distolabial developmental depressions.
  * *Cusp Slopes:* **Mesial cusp slope is shorter than the distal cusp slope** in unworn teeth (the contact area is more incisal on the mesial than the distal).
* **Lingual Aspect:**
  * Well-developed, large **cingulum** centered in the cervical third.
  * Prominent **lingual ridge** extending from cusp tip toward the cingulum, dividing the lingual fossa into two distinct fossae: **mesiolingual fossa** and **distolingual fossa**.
  * Prominent mesial and distal marginal ridges.
* **Mesial & Distal Aspects:** Triangular outline with base at cervix; labial and lingual crests of curvature in cervical third.
* **Root:** Single, exceptionally long, conical root with developmental grooves on both mesial and distal root surfaces (distal groove is deeper).

### Permanent Mandibular First Molar
* **General Significance:** Largest tooth in the mandibular arch; earliest permanent tooth to erupt (at ~6 years of age, behind primary 2nd molar).
* **Occlusal Aspect:**
  * Trapezoidal or pentagonal outline; wider mesiodistally than buccolingually.
  * **Five Cusps (3 Buccal, 2 Lingual):**
    1. *Mesiobuccal (MB):* Second largest cusp.
    2. *Distobuccal (DB):* Third largest cusp.
    3. *Distal (D):* Smallest cusp; located on the distobuccal line angle.
    4. *Mesiolingual (ML):* Largest, highest, and sharpest cusp.
    5. *Distolingual (DL):* Second highest cusp.
  * *Groove Pattern:* Central developmental groove with **Mesiobuccal**, **Distobuccal**, and **Lingual developmental grooves** intersecting in the central fossa, forming a classic **"Y" or "W" occlusal groove pattern**.
* **Buccal Aspect:** Trapezoidal; shows two developmental grooves: **Mesiobuccal groove** (terminates in buccal pit) and **Distobuccal groove**, dividing the three buccal cusps.
* **Roots:** **Two distinct roots (Mesial and Distal):**
  * *Mesial Root:* Broad, flattened mesiodistally, grooved on both surfaces; contains **two pulp canals** (Mesiobuccal and Mesiolingual).
  * *Distal Root:* Rounder, less curved; contains **one or two canals** (Distal / Distobuccal and Distolingual).

### Permanent Maxillary First Molar
* **General Significance:** Largest tooth in the maxillary arch.
* **Occlusal Aspect:**
  * Rhomboidal outline with two acute angles (mesiobuccal and distopalatal) and two obtuse angles (mesiopalatal and distobuccal).
  * **Four Major Cusps + One Supplementary Cusp:**
    1. *Mesiopalatal / Mesiolingual (MP/ML):* **Largest cusp**.
    2. *Mesiobuccal (MB):* Second largest.
    3. *Distobuccal (DB):* Third largest.
    4. *Distopalatal / Distolingual (DP/DL):* Smallest of the four major cusps.
    5. **Cusp of Carabelli (Tubercle of Carabelli):** Non-functional supplementary 5th cusp located on the lingual surface of the mesiopalatal cusp.
  * **Oblique Ridge (Prominent Characteristic Landmark):** A prominent ridge running diagonally across the occlusal surface, formed by the union of the **triangular ridge of the distobuccal cusp** and the **distal ridge of the mesiopalatal cusp**.
* **Roots:** **Three widely divergent roots:**
  * *Palatal (Lingual) Root:* Largest, longest, and strongest; banana-shaped curve.
  * *Mesiobuccal (MB) Root:* Broad, flattened; often contains **two root canals (MB1 and MB2)**.
  * *Distobuccal (DB) Root:* Smallest, conical, single canal.

### Primary (Deciduous) vs. Permanent Teeth
| Feature | Primary (Deciduous) Teeth | Permanent Teeth |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Total Number** | 20 teeth (no premolars) | 32 teeth |
| **Crown Dimensions** | Smaller overall; crowns wider mesiodistally relative to crown height | Larger overall; crowns taller cervico-incisally |
| **Cervical Constriction** | **Marked cervical constriction** with prominent buccal cervical ridges | Less pronounced cervical constriction |
| **Enamel & Dentin Thickness** | Enamel and dentin layers are **much thinner** (~1 mm uniform thickness) | Enamel and dentin layers are thicker (~2–2.5 mm) |
| **Pulp Cavity** | **Relatively much larger pulp chamber**; pulp horns extend much closer to occlusal surface (especially mesial pulp horns) | Relatively smaller pulp chamber; pulp horns farther from surface |
| **Roots** | Roots are **slender, longer, and widely flared** beyond crown width to accommodate developing permanent succedaneous tooth buds | Roots are sturdier, less flared |
| **Color** | More opaque, chalky white or bluish-white (lower mineral content, higher organic matrix) | Yellowish-white or grayish-white |

## 2. Odontogenesis (Development of Teeth)

### Morphological Stages of Tooth Development
1. **Dental Lamina Stage:** Localized proliferation of primary oral ectoderm forming a C-shaped band (dental lamina) along future alveolar ridges (6th week IUL).
2. **Bud Stage:** Localized proliferations of dental lamina cells invaginate into the underlying ectomesenchyme, forming 10 rounded, knob-like epithelial buds in each jaw.
3. **Cap Stage:** Unequal growth of the epithelial bud leads to invagination, producing a three-layered **Enamel Organ**:
   * *Outer Enamel Epithelium (OEE):* Cuboidal cells covering the outer convex surface.
   * *Inner Enamel Epithelium (IEE):* Columnar cells lining the inner concavity; differentiate into ameloblasts.
   * *Stellate Reticulum:* Star-shaped cells with central nuclei and branching processes in a loose glycosaminoglycan-rich matrix (acts as a mechanical cushion).
   * *Condensation of Ectomesenchyme:*
     * **Dental Papilla:** Ectomesenchyme condensed within the concavity; forms **Dentin and Pulp**.
     * **Dental Follicle (Dental Sac):** Fibrous condensation enclosing the enamel organ and papilla; forms **Cementum, Periodontal Ligament, and Alveolar Bone Proper**.
4. **Bell Stage (Early and Late):**
   * *Enamel Organ Layers (4 distinct layers):* OEE, Stellate Reticulum, **Stratum Intermedium** (2–3 layers of flat squamous cells rich in **alkaline phosphatase**, essential for enamel mineralization), and IEE.
   * *Histodifferentiation:* IEE cells elongate into tall columnar preameloblasts, inducing peripheral dental papilla cells to differentiate into **odontoblasts**.
   * *Morphodifferentiation:* Folding of the IEE establishes the future crown form and cusp pattern.
   * *Late Bell Stage (Appositional Stage):* Deposition of hard tissues begins: **Dentinogenesis precedes Amelogenesis**. Odontoblasts secrete predentin, which calcifies; contact with mineralized dentin triggers ameloblasts to secrete enamel matrix.
5. **Transient Embryonic Structures:**
   * **Enamel Knot:** Localized thickening of non-dividing epithelial cells in the center of IEE during cap/early bell stage. Acts as the **primary signaling center** expressing morphogenetic factors (**FGF-4, BMP-2, BMP-4, Shh, Wnt**); dictates crown morphogenesis and determines cusp locations. Secondary enamel knots appear at future cusp tips.
   * **Enamel Cord:** A vertical strand of cells extending from the enamel knot toward the OEE.
   * **Enamel Niche:** Artifactual appearance of mesenchyme pocketed between dental lamina folds.

### Root Development & Hertwig's Epithelial Root Sheath (HERS)
* **Formation:** At the completion of crown formation, the cervical loop (where OEE meets IEE without intervening stellate reticulum) proliferates apically as a bilayered epithelial skirt called **Hertwig's Epithelial Root Sheath (HERS)**.
* **Epithelial Diaphragm:** The apical horizontal rim of HERS turns inward at a $45^\circ$ angle, circumscribing the future primary apical foramen and guiding the number of roots.
* **Sequence of Events:**
  1. Inner layer of HERS induces adjacent peripheral dental papilla cells to differentiate into **radicular odontoblasts**, which form root dentin.
  2. Once the first layer of root dentin is deposited, HERS fragments and disintegrates.
  3. Ectomesenchymal cells of the dental follicle migrate through gaps in the degenerated sheath, contact root dentin, and differentiate into **cementoblasts**, which lay down cementum.
* **Epithelial Rests of Malassez (ERM):**
  * Persistent cellular remnants of fragmented HERS that reside permanently within the **Periodontal Ligament (PDL)**.
  * *Pathological Significance:* Under inflammatory stimuli (e.g. chronic periapical periodontitis from infected necrotic pulp), rests of Malassez proliferate to line the lumen of **Radicular (Periapical) Cysts**.

## 3. Hard Tissues of the Tooth

### Enamel
* **Composition:** The most highly mineralized and hardest tissue in the human body: **96% inorganic material** (crystalline calcium hydroxyapatite: $Ca_{10}(PO_4)_6(OH)_2$), **1% organic matrix** (non-collagenous proteins: **amelogenins** [90% in developing enamel], **enamelins**, ameloblastin), and **3% water** by weight.
* **Amelogenesis (Life Cycle of Ameloblast):**
  1. *Morphogenetic Stage:* IEE cells are short columnar.
  2. *Organizing / Differentiation Stage:* Cells elongate, reverse nuclear polarity (nuclei move away from basement membrane toward stratum intermedium); Golgi apparatus faces dentin.
  3. *Formative / Secretory Stage:* Ameloblasts develop a conical apical projection called **Tomes' Process**, which secretes enamel matrix proteins in a rhythmic, picket-fence pattern, forming **Enamel Rods (Prisms)** and interrod substance.
  4. *Maturation Stage:* Tomes' process disappears (ruffle-ended and smooth-ended ameloblasts); massive resorption of water and organic amelogenins coupled with influx of calcium and phosphate ions into hydroxyapatite crystals.
  5. *Protective Stage:* Ameloblasts flatten, combine with remnants of stratum intermedium and OEE to form the **Reduced Enamel Epithelium (REE)**, which covers unerupted enamel and later forms the primary junctional epithelium.
* **Microscopic Hallmarks of Enamel:**
  * **Enamel Rods (Prisms):** Basic structural units (4–5 $\mu$m diameter) running from DEJ to surface; keyhole or fish-scale cross-sectional shape.
  * **Striae of Retzius:** Incremental growth lines reflecting rhythmic apposition of enamel matrix.
  * **Neonatal Line:** An exaggerated, prominent Stria of Retzius present in all primary teeth and permanent first molars, reflecting metabolic and physiological stress at birth.
  * **Hunter-Schreger Bands (HSB):** Optical alternating dark (diazones) and light (parazones) bands seen under reflected light, caused by periodic shifts in the orientation of adjacent rod groups.
  * **Enamel Tufts:** Hypomineralized, brush-like structures extending from the DEJ into the inner 1/3 of enamel.
  * **Enamel Lamellae:** Thin, leaf-like vertical faults or cracks containing organic material, extending from outer enamel surface deep into or across the DEJ.
  * **Enamel Spindles:** Club-shaped ends of **trapped odontoblastic processes** that cross the DEJ into the enamel matrix before mineralization.

### Dentin & Dentinogenesis
* **Composition:** **70% inorganic** (hydroxyapatite crystals), **20% organic** (predominantly **Type I Collagen** [90%], with non-collagenous proteins: phosphophoryn [DPP], dentin sialoprotein [DSP]), and **10% water** by weight. Resilient, elastic foundation supporting brittle enamel.
* **Microscopic Structure:**
  * **Dentinal Tubules:** Closely packed microscopic channels extending throughout the full thickness of dentin from the pulp to the DEJ/CDJ. Tubules follow an **S-shaped curvature** in the crown and a straighter course in the root.
  * *Contents of Tubules:* Cytoplasmic process of odontoblast (**Tomes' fiber**), dentinal fluid (extracellular fluid transudate from pulp), and unmyelinated nerve terminals in inner 1/3.
  * **Peritubular (Intratubular) Dentin:** Highly mineralized collar lining the inner wall of tubules; poor in collagen.
  * **Intertubular Dentin:** Located between tubules; forms the bulk of dentin; dense collagen meshwork.
* **Classification of Dentin:**
  * **Primary Dentin:** Forms the bulk of the tooth; deposited during tooth development until complete root formation. Subdivided into:
    * *Mantle Dentin:* First-formed layer (~150 $\mu$m thick) immediately underlying DEJ; less mineralized; thick Von Korff's collagen fibers.
    * *Circumpulpal Dentin:* Forms remaining mass of primary dentin; uniform mineralization.
  * **Secondary Dentin:** Physiological dentin formed slowly throughout life **after root completion**. Formed unevenly (more on roof and floor of pulp chamber), causing gradual, progressive reduction in pulp chamber volume with advancing age.
  * **Tertiary (Reactive / Reparative) Dentin:** Deposited focal and rapidly in response to **localized pathological or operative stimuli** (caries, attrition, erosion, cavity preparation).
    * *Reactionary Dentin:* Deposited by surviving original odontoblasts in response to mild stimuli.
    * *Reparative Dentin:* Deposited by newly differentiated odontoblast-like cells (from pulpal stem cells) following death of original odontoblasts from severe insults.
* **Theories of Dentin Hypersensitivity:**
  1. *Direct Neural Innervation Theory:* Free nerve endings in tubules directly stimulated (*invalidated: nerves penetrate only inner 1/3*).
  2. *Odontoblast Receptor / Transducer Theory:* Odontoblast acts as a sensory receptor synaptic with pulpal nerves (*lacks synaptic machinery*).
  3. **Hydrodynamic Theory of Brännström (Widely Accepted Standard):**
     * Thermal, osmotic, evaporative, tactile, or chemical stimuli applied to exposed dentin cause rapid **displacement/movement of dentinal fluid** within dentinal tubules (outward or inward).
     * Fluid movement exerts mechanical shear stress and deforms the subodontoblastic nerve plexus (**Plexus of Raschkow**) and mechanoreceptive A$\delta$ nerve terminals at the pulp-dentin border, triggering action potentials perceived by the patient as sharp, acute pain.

### Cementum
* **Composition:** **45–50% inorganic** hydroxyapatite, **50–55% organic matrix** (Type I collagen) and water. Avascular, non-innervated, dynamic mineralized tissue covering root dentin.
* **Types of Cementum:**
  * **Acellular Extrinsic Fiber Cementum (AEFC / Primary Cementum):**
    * Covers the **cervical 2/3 of the root**.
    * Lacks embedded cementocytes (acellular).
    * Contains dense, fully mineralized **extrinsic Sharpey's fibers** (derived from PDL fibroblasts); primary function is **tooth anchorage**.
  * **Cellular Intrinsic Fiber Cementum (CIFC / Secondary Cementum):**
    * Covers the **apical 1/3 of the root and furcation areas**.
    * Contains entrapped **cementocytes** within lacunae, connected by canaliculi oriented toward the PDL.
    * Formed rapidly; plays major role in **compensatory eruption and repair/regeneration** of resorbed root surfaces.
* **Cementoenamel Junction (CEJ) Relationships (Choquet's Classification):**
  1. **Cementum overlaps enamel:** ~**60%** of teeth.
  2. **Cementum meets enamel end-to-end:** ~**30%** of teeth.
  3. **Gap between cementum and enamel (Dentin exposed):** ~**10%** of teeth (leaves exposed dentinal tubules; high clinical predisposition to **cervical dentin hypersensitivity and root caries**).

## 4. Soft Tissues: Pulp, Periodontal Ligament & Oral Mucosa

### Dental Pulp
* Specialized loose, richly vascularized and innervated connective tissue derived from the dental papilla.
* **Histological Zones (from Dentin Inward toward Center):**
  1. **Odontoblastic Layer:** Outermost zone; continuous single layer of columnar odontoblast cell bodies lining the pulpal surface of predentin.
  2. **Cell-Free Zone of Weil (Subodontoblastic Layer):** Slit-like zone containing extensive unmyelinated nerve plexus (**Plexus of Raschkow**) and capillary loop network.
  3. **Cell-Rich Zone:** High density of fibroblasts, undifferentiated mesenchymal stem cells (pericytes), and macrophages.
  4. **Pulp Core / Central Zone:** Loose connective tissue stroma containing major arterioles, venules, lymphatic channels, and nerve trunks.
* **Pulp Innervation:**
  * **$A\delta$ Fibers (Myelinated):** High conduction velocity (5–30 m/s); located primarily at pulp-dentin border; transmit **sharp, piercing, well-localized acute pain** (stimulated by hydrodynamic fluid movement in dentinal tubules; tests positive with cold pulp sensitivity testing).
  * **C Fibers (Unmyelinated):** Slow conduction velocity (0.5–2 m/s); located throughout pulp core; transmit **dull, aching, burning, throbbing, poorly localized lingering pain** (stimulated by inflammatory mediators and tissue necrosis; characteristic of irreversible pulpitis).
* **Functions of Pulp:** Formative (dentinogenesis), Nutritive (supplies nutrients to avascular dentin), Sensory (pain perception), Defensive/Reparative (tertiary dentin formation).
* **Age Changes in Pulp:** Decreased pulp chamber volume, reduced cellularity, decreased vascularity and nerve fibers, marked increase in fibrous collagen fibers, and presence of **Pulp Stones (Denticles)**:
  * *True Denticles:* Composed of dentin with dentinal tubules; formed by entrapped odontoblasts.
  * *False Denticles:* Concentric calcified laminations of dead cells/collagen; no tubules.

### Periodontal Ligament (PDL)
* Specialized, highly cellular fibrous connective tissue connecting the cementum of the tooth root to the alveolar bone proper (**Gomphosis joint**). Hourglass shape (average thickness: **0.15–0.38 mm**; narrowest at middle third fulcrum ~0.2 mm).
* **Principal Collagen Fiber Groups (Dentoalveolar Group):**
  1. **Alveolar Crest Group:** Runs obliquely downward and outward from cervical cementum just below CEJ to the alveolar bone crest. *Resists lateral and extrusive forces.*
  2. **Horizontal Group:** Runs at right angles to the long axis from cementum to alveolar bone immediately below crest. *Resists tipping and lateral horizontal forces.*
  3. **Oblique Group:** **The most numerous and predominant fiber group** (occupies middle 2/3 of root). Runs obliquely upward and outward from cementum to alveolar bone. *Bears the primary brunt of masticatory vertical compressive loads, converting intrusive pressure into tensile stress on bone.*
  4. **Apical Group:** Radiates from root apex to fundus of socket. *Resists tipping, rotational, and extrusive forces.*
  5. **Interradicular Group:** Runs from furcation cementum to interradicular crest in multirooted teeth.
* **Gingival Group of Fibers:** Dentogingival, Alveologingival, Dentoperiosteal, Circular (encircles tooth collar), and **Transseptal Group** (crosses above interdental alveolar bone to connect cementum of adjacent teeth; maintains tooth-to-tooth proximal contact).
* **Sharpey's Fibers:** The terminal embedded ends of PDL collagen fibers inserted into cementum on one side and alveolar bone proper (cribriform plate / bundle bone) on the other.

### Oral Mucosa & Gingiva
* **Types of Oral Mucosa:**
  * *Masticatory Mucosa:* Keratinized or parakeratinized stratified squamous epithelium bound tightly to underlying periosteum (**mucoperiosteum**); subjected to abrasive masticatory forces (Gingiva, Hard Palate).
  * *Lining Mucosa:* Non-keratinized, flexible, distensible stratified squamous epithelium with loose submucosa (Buccal, Labial mucosa, Floor of mouth, Ventral tongue, Soft palate).
  * *Specialized Mucosa:* Sensory mucosa bearing taste papillae on the dorsum of the tongue.
* **Gingival Histology:**
  * *Free (Marginal) Gingiva:* Unattached cuff surrounding tooth collar; boundary is free gingival groove.
  * *Attached Gingiva:* Firm, resilient, tightly bound to periosteum; exhibits a stippled ("orange-peel") surface texture caused by alternating epithelial rete pegs and connective tissue papillae.
  * *Junctional Epithelium (JE):* Non-keratinized collar of flattened cells attached directly to the tooth surface via **hemidesmosomes and internal basal lamina**. High cellular turnover rate (**4–6 days**); highly permeable to allow outward flow of Gingival Crevicular Fluid (GCF) and defensive PMN migration into the sulcus.
* **Biological Width:** The combined vertical dimension of the supracrestal soft tissue attachment:
  $$\text{Biological Width} = \text{Junctional Epithelium } (\sim 0.97\ \text{mm}) + \text{Supracrestal Connective Tissue Attachment } (\sim 1.07\ \text{mm}) \approx \mathbf{2.04\ mm}$$
  *(Must never be violated by subgingival restoration margins to prevent chronic inflammation and bone loss).*

### Salivary Glands: Serous vs. Mucous Acini
* **Serous Acini:**
  * Spherical shape with small central lumen.
  * Round, spherical nuclei located in the basal third.
  * Dark basophilic cytoplasm on H&E (abundant rough ER); apical cytoplasm packed with acidophilic **zymogen granules** containing **$\alpha$-Amylase (Ptyalin)** and lysozyme.
  * Watery, thin, enzyme-rich secretion.
  * *Dominant in:* **Parotid Gland (100% serous)**.
* **Mucous Acini:**
  * Tubular or rounded shape with larger, visible central lumen.
  * **Compressed, flattened nuclei pushed against the basal cell membrane**.
  * Pale, vacuolated, foamy-appearing cytoplasm on H&E (mucin droplets extract during tissue processing).
  * Viscous, thick, lubricating mucin secretion rich in high-molecular-weight glycoproteins.
  * *Dominant in:* **Sublingual Gland (predominantly mucous)** and minor palatine/lingual glands.
* **Submandibular Gland:** Mixed gland (80% serous, 20% mucous); contains **Serous Demilunes (Crescents of Gianuzzi)**: cap-like serous cells fitted over the tubular ends of mucous acini.
* **Duct System:** Intercalated ducts (simple cuboidal) → **Striated ducts** (simple columnar cells with prominent basal infoldings packed with vertically oriented mitochondria; actively reabsorb $Na^+$ and $Cl^-$ and secrete $K^+$ and $HCO_3^-$, converting isotonic primary saliva into **hypotonic final saliva**) → Excretory ducts (pseudostratified to stratified columnar).

## 5. Craniofacial Embryology & Eruption

### Development of the Mandible & Meckel's Cartilage
* **Embryological Origin:** Develops from the mandibular prominence of the **1st Pharyngeal (Branchial) Arch**.
* **Type of Ossification:** Primarily **Intramembranous Ossification**.
* **Sequence of Mandibular Development:**
  1. Ossification begins at the 6th–7th week of intrauterine life at a single primary ossification center located in the angle between the mental and incisive branches of the inferior alveolar nerve (near future **mental foramen**).
  2. Bone formation spreads rapidly anteriorly to the midline and posteriorly toward the ramus, forming a trough around the developing neurovascular bundle and tooth germs.
* **Role and Fate of Meckel's Cartilage:**
  * *Nature:* The cartilaginous bar of the 1st arch; acts as a **morphogenetic guide and structural template**, but **does NOT directly contribute to the formation of the mandibular bony ramus or body** (the bone forms *lateral* to the cartilage by intramembranous ossification).
  * *Fate of Meckel's Cartilage:*
    * *Dorsal End:* Undergoes endochondral ossification to form the **Malleus** and **Incus** of the middle ear.
    * *Intermediate Portion (Perichondrium):* Fibrous perichondrium persists as the **Sphenomandibular Ligament** and the **Anterior Ligament of Malleus**.
    * *Ventral Portion:* Regresses and is resorbed by chondroclasts, with minor contributions to mental ossicles at the symphysis menti.
* **Secondary Cartilages of the Mandible:**
  * Distinct from Meckel's cartilage; develop independently to assist rapid mandibular expansion:
    1. **Condylar Cartilage:** The largest and most crucial; develops as a cone-shaped fibrocartilage mass capping the condylar process. Acts as an active endochondral growth center until adulthood (**20–21 years of age**), contributing to mandibular length, height, and ramus growth.
    2. **Coronoid Cartilage:** Transient; disappears before birth.
    3. **Symphyseal Cartilages:** Two cartilaginous nodules at the mandibular symphysis; fuse during the first year of life.
