# BDS 1st Year — Biochemistry Quick Revision Cheatsheet
*Based on Kathmandu University Past Questions (2015–2021)*

This cheatsheet distills the most frequently tested concepts, pathways, and clinical correlations from the extracted past papers. Use it for rapid review before your exams.

---

## 1. Carbohydrate Metabolism

### Glycolysis
* **Irreversible Steps (Key Regulatory Enzymes):**
  1. Glucose → Glucose-6-P (*Hexokinase / Glucokinase*)
  2. Fructose-6-P → Fructose-1,6-bisP (*Phosphofructokinase-1 / PFK-1*) — **Rate-limiting step**
  3. Phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP) → Pyruvate (*Pyruvate kinase*)
* **Rapoport-Luebering Cycle:** Unique to RBCs. Bypasses the pyruvate kinase step to produce **2,3-BPG**, which decreases hemoglobin's affinity for O₂, facilitating O₂ release to tissues.
* **Glycolysis in RBCs:** RBCs lack mitochondria and rely entirely on anaerobic glycolysis for ATP. End products are lactate and 2,3-BPG.

### HMP Shunt (Pentose Phosphate Pathway)
* **Significance:** Generates **NADPH** (for reductive biosynthesis & maintaining reduced glutathione against oxidative stress) and **Ribose-5-phosphate** (for nucleotide synthesis).

### TCA Cycle (Krebs Cycle)
* **Amphibolic Pathway:** Serves both catabolic (oxidation of carbs, fats, proteins to CO₂ & H₂O) and anabolic (providing precursors for heme, amino acids, glucose via gluconeogenesis) pathways.
* **Key yield per Acetyl-CoA:** 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 GTP, 2 CO₂.

## 2. Lipid Metabolism

### Fatty Acid Oxidation (β-Oxidation)
* **Activation:** Fatty acid + CoA + ATP → Acyl-CoA (occurs in cytosol).
* **Transport:** Carnitine shuttle transports Acyl-CoA into mitochondria.
* **Cycle Steps:** Oxidation → Hydration → Oxidation → Thiolysis. Yields Acetyl-CoA, NADH, FADH₂.
* **Fate of Acetyl-CoA:** Enters TCA cycle or forms Ketone bodies (in the liver).

### Ketone Bodies
* **Types:** Acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate, Acetone.
* **Synthesis:** Liver mitochondria (from Acetyl-CoA).
* **Utilization:** Extrahepatic tissues (brain, heart, muscle).
* **Increased Synthesis in:** Starvation, uncontrolled Diabetes Mellitus, high-fat/low-carb diets.

### Lipoproteins & Cholesterol
* **Chylomicrons:** Transport dietary (exogenous) triglycerides from intestine to tissues.
* **VLDL:** Transport endogenous triglycerides from liver to tissues.
* **LDL:** Delivers cholesterol to peripheral tissues (**Atherogenic**).
* **HDL:** Mediates **Reverse Cholesterol Transport** (peripheral tissues → liver). Protective against atherosclerosis.
* **Cholesterol Synthesis:** **HMG-CoA reductase** is the rate-limiting enzyme. Regulated by feedback inhibition.

### Phospholipids & Eicosanoids
* **Phospholipids:** Major components of cell membranes, lung surfactant (Dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine), and emulsifying agents in bile.
* **Eicosanoids:** Derived from Arachidonic acid. Include Prostaglandins, Thromboxanes, Leukotrienes. Functions: inflammation, blood clotting, smooth muscle contraction.

## 3. Proteins & Amino Acids

### Protein Structure & Forces
* **Primary:** Amino acid sequence (*Peptide bonds*).
* **Secondary:** α-helix, β-pleated sheet (*Hydrogen bonds*).
* **Tertiary:** 3D folding (*Hydrophobic interactions, disulfide bonds, ionic bonds, Van der Waals*).
* **Quaternary:** Multiple polypeptide chains (e.g., Hemoglobin).
* **Sickle Cell Anemia:** Point mutation in β-globin (Glutamate → Valine at position 6). Causes HbS polymerization under low O₂, leading to RBC sickling.

### Enzymology
* **Competitive Inhibition:** Inhibitor resembles substrate and binds the active site. **Vmax unchanged, Km increases**. (Examples: Statins, Methotrexate, Sulfa drugs).
* **Non-competitive Inhibition:** Binds allosteric site. **Vmax decreases, Km unchanged**.
* **Isoenzymes:** Different molecular forms of the same enzyme (e.g., LDH, CK). Useful in tissue-specific diagnosis.
* **Coenzymes:** Organic non-protein helpers (e.g., NAD⁺, FAD, CoA).

### Heme Synthesis & Porphyrias
* **Synthesis:** Mitochondria → Cytosol → Mitochondria. Rate-limiting enzyme: **ALA synthase** (requires Vitamin B6).
* **Porphyrias:** Defects in heme synthesis pathway leading to porphyrin accumulation.
  * *Acute Intermittent Porphyria:* PBG deaminase deficiency. Presents with neurological/abdominal symptoms.

### Special Amino Acids & Neurotransmitters
* **Catecholamines:** Tyrosine → DOPA → Dopamine → Norepinephrine → Epinephrine.
* **Acetylcholine:** Synthesized from Choline + Acetyl-CoA (*Choline acetyltransferase*). Degraded by Acetylcholinesterase.
* **Myasthenia Gravis:** Autoantibodies destroy postsynaptic ACh receptors at the neuromuscular junction.

## 4. Bioenergetics & Electron Transport Chain (ETC)

### ETC & Chemiosmotic Theory
* **Location:** Inner mitochondrial membrane.
* **Complexes:** I (NADH dehydrogenase), II (Succinate dehydrogenase), III (Cytochrome bc1), IV (Cytochrome c oxidase).
* **Chemiosmotic Theory (Peter Mitchell):** Electron flow pumps protons (H⁺) from the matrix to the intermembrane space, creating an electrochemical gradient. Protons flow back through **ATP synthase (Complex V)**, driving ATP synthesis.
* **Uncouplers:** (e.g., 2,4-Dinitrophenol, high-dose Aspirin). Dissipate the proton gradient as heat instead of making ATP.

### Free Radicals & Antioxidants
* **ROS (Reactive Oxygen Species):** Superoxide, Hydroxyl radical, H₂O₂. Cause lipid peroxidation and DNA damage.
* **Antioxidants:** SOD, Catalase, Glutathione peroxidase, Vitamins C & E.
* **Respiratory Burst:** Phagocytes generate ROS via NADPH oxidase to kill engulfed bacteria.

## 5. Vitamins, Minerals & Clinical Biochemistry

### Iron Metabolism
* **Absorption:** Duodenum/Jejunum. Absorbed as Fe²⁺ (reduced by Vitamin C).
* **Transport:** Bound to **Transferrin** in blood.
* **Storage:** Bound to **Ferritin** (intracellular) and **Hemosiderin**.
* **Regulation:** **Hepcidin** (liver peptide) inhibits ferroportin, decreasing iron absorption and release from macrophages.
* **Anemias:**
  * *Iron Deficiency:* Microcytic hypochromic. Low ferritin, high TIBC.
  * *Pernicious:* Vit B12 deficiency (lack of Intrinsic Factor). Megaloblastic.
  * *Thalassemia:* Genetic defect in globin chain synthesis (α or β).

### Key Vitamins
* **Vitamin B12:** Required for methionine synthase and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase. Deficiency causes megaloblastic anemia & neurological deficits.
* **Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid):** Required for hydroxylation of collagen (Proline/Lysine). Deficiency = Scurvy. Also keeps Iron in the absorbable Fe²⁺ state.
* **Folate Trap:** B12 deficiency traps folate as 5-methyl-THF, causing a functional folate deficiency.
* **Organophosphorus Poisoning:** Irreversibly inhibits Acetylcholinesterase → ACh accumulation → cholinergic crisis (SLUDGE syndrome). Treatment: Atropine + Pralidoxime.

### Cardiac Markers (Myocardial Infarction)
* **Troponin I/T:** Most sensitive & specific. Rises in 3-6 hrs, peaks at 24 hrs, stays elevated for 7-10 days.
* **CK-MB:** Rises in 4-6 hrs, peaks at 24 hrs, normalizes in 48-72 hrs (ideal for detecting re-infarction).
* **Myoglobin:** Early marker (rises in 1-4 hrs) but not cardiac-specific.
* **LDH:** LDH1 > LDH2 ("flipped pattern") indicates MI.

### Atherosclerosis
* **LDL Role:** Oxidized LDL is taken up by macrophages via scavenger receptors → Foam cells → Fatty streaks → Atherosclerotic plaque.
* **Lowering Cholesterol:** Statins (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors), Bile acid sequestrants, Niacin.

## 6. Molecular Biology

### DNA Structure (Watson-Crick Model)
* Double helix, antiparallel strands.
* Sugar-phosphate backbone on the outside, Nitrogenous bases paired inside.
* **Base pairing:** A=T (2 Hydrogen bonds), G≡C (3 Hydrogen bonds).

### DNA vs RNA
* **DNA:** Deoxyribose sugar, Thymine base, Double-stranded, stores genetic info.
* **RNA:** Ribose sugar, Uracil base, Single-stranded, involved in protein synthesis.
