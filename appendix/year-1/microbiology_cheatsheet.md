## 1. General Bacteriology & Infection Control

### Sterilization vs. Disinfection
* **Sterilization:** Absolute process that destroys or eliminates **all forms of microbial life**, including bacterial spores, viruses, and fungi. Essential for critical dental instruments that penetrate soft tissue or bone.
* **Disinfection:** Process that eliminates many or all pathogenic microorganisms on inanimate objects, **with the exception of bacterial spores**.

### Physical Agents: Moist Heat vs. Dry Heat
* **Autoclave (Moist Heat Under Pressure):**
  * *Principle:* Water boils when vapor pressure equals atmospheric pressure. In a closed vessel, increasing pressure raises boiling temperature. Steam under pressure transfers **latent heat of condensation** (540 cal/g) directly upon contacting cooler surfaces, penetrating porous materials rapidly and killing microbes by **coagulation and denaturation of essential proteins**.
  * *Standard Parameters:* **$121^\circ\text{C}$ at 15 pounds per square inch (psi) pressure for 15 minutes** (or $134^\circ\text{C}$ at 30 psi for 3–5 minutes for flash cycles).
  * *Uses in Dentistry:* Sterilization of surgical extraction forceps, scalers, dental burs, handpieces, surgical drapes, and culture media.
  * *Sterilization Controls (Monitors):*
    * *Biological Indicator:* Spores of **$Geobacillus\ stearothermophilus$** (incubated at $55–60^\circ\text{C}$; failure to grow confirms sterilization).
    * *Chemical Indicator:* Bowie-Dick test pack (tests vacuum leak and steam penetration), Browne's tubes (color change from red to green), Autoclave heat-sensitive indicator tape (dark diagonal stripes).
    * *Physical Indicator:* Pressure gauge, internal temperature probe, cycle timer printouts.
* **Hot Air Oven (Dry Heat):**
  * *Principle:* Kills microorganisms primarily by **oxidative destruction of cellular constituents**, protein denaturation, and toxic electrolyte concentration effects.
  * *Standard Parameters:* **$160^\circ\text{C}$ for 2 hours** (or $170^\circ\text{C}$ for 1 hour).
  * *Uses:* Dry glassware (Petri dishes, test tubes, pipettes), dry powders, anhydrous fats, oils, and petroleum jelly. *Not suitable for rubber, fabrics, or aqueous solutions.*
  * *Biological Indicator:* Spores of **$Bacillus\ atrophaeus$** (formerly *Bacillus subtilis* subsp. *niger*; incubated at $37^\circ\text{C}$).

### Bacterial Structure & Gram Staining
* **Gram-Positive vs. Gram-Negative Cell Wall:**
  * *Gram-Positive:* Thick, multilayered **peptidoglycan mesh (20–80 nm)**, contains **teichoic and lipoteichoic acids**; no outer membrane, no periplasmic space, no endotoxin.
  * *Gram-Negative:* Thin single peptidoglycan layer (2–7 nm) surrounded by an **outer membrane** containing **Lipopolysaccharide (LPS / Endotoxin)** with Lipid A (toxic moiety), core oligosaccharide, and O-antigen. Contains a distinct periplasmic space with $\beta$-lactamases.
* **Gram Stain Procedure:**
  1. *Primary Stain:* Crystal violet (1 minute) → all cells purple.
  2. *Mordant:* Gram's Iodine (1 minute) → forms insoluble Crystal Violet-Iodine (CV-I) complex within cell wall.
  3. *Decolorizer:* Acetone or 95% Ethyl alcohol (10–30 seconds) → dehydrates thick Gram-positive wall trapping CV-I (remains purple); dissolves lipid-rich outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria, leaching out CV-I (cells become colorless).
  4. *Counterstain:* Dilute carbol fuchsin or Safranin (30–60 seconds) → Gram-negative cells take up counterstain and stain **pink / red**.

### Bacterial Growth Curve
* Observed in a closed batch culture system; plotted as $\log_{10}$ of viable bacterial count vs. time:
  1. **Lag Phase:** Period of cellular adaptation to new environment. Bacteria increase in size and synthesize enzymes, RNA, and structural components. **No increase in bacterial cell count** ($N_t = N_0$).
  2. **Log (Exponential) Phase:** Cells divide at a constant, maximal rate by binary fission. Population doubles per generation time ($2^n$). Highest metabolic activity; **bacteria are most susceptible to antimicrobials and physical agents**.
  3. **Stationary Phase:** Rate of bacterial multiplication equals rate of bacterial death. Exhaustion of essential nutrients, accumulation of toxic metabolic by-products, and drop in pH. Spore formation occurs here in spore-formers.
  4. **Phase of Decline (Death Phase):** Viable cell count declines exponentially due to toxic accumulation, nutrient exhaustion, and release of autolytic enzymes.

### Culture Media Classification
* **Classification by Function:**
  * *Basal / Simple Media:* Support growth of non-fastidious bacteria (e.g. Nutrient Agar, Nutrient Broth, Peptone Water).
  * *Enriched Media:* Basal media supplemented with blood, serum, or egg for fastidious pathogens (e.g. **Blood Agar** for streptococcal hemolysis; **Chocolate Agar** containing lysed RBCs for *Haemophilus influenzae* and *Neisseria*).
  * *Selective Media:* Contain inhibitors (bile salts, dyes, antibiotics) that suppress unwanted commensals while allowing selective growth of the target pathogen (e.g. **MacConkey Agar** with crystal violet and bile salts for Gram-negative enteric bacilli; **Lowenstein-Jensen (LJ) Medium** containing malachite green for *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*; **TCBS Agar** for *Vibrio cholerae*).
  * *Differential / Indicator Media:* Incorporate substrates and pH indicators to distinguish between bacterial species by colonial appearance (e.g. **MacConkey Agar** distinguishes **Lactose Fermenters** [pink colonies, e.g. *E. coli*, *Klebsiella*] from **Non-Lactose Fermenters** [pale/colorless colonies, e.g. *Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *Pseudomonas*]).
  * *Transport Media:* Non-nutrient media maintaining pathogen viability during transport without allowing overgrowth (e.g. Stuart's medium, Cary-Blair medium).
  * *Anaerobic Media:* Contain reducing agents that lower redox potential (e.g. **Robertson's Cooked Meat (RCM) broth** with meat particles absorbing oxygen; Thioglycollate broth).

### Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (AST)
* **Kirby-Bauer Disk Diffusion Method:**
  * Standard medium: **Mueller-Hinton Agar (MHA)** poured to a uniform depth of **4 mm** at pH 7.2–7.4.
  * Inoculum preparation: Bacterial suspension adjusted to **0.5 McFarland turbidity standard** (~$1.5 \times 10^8$ CFU/mL).
  * Inoculation: Lawn culture spread evenly in three planes using a sterile cotton swab.
  * Disk placement: Filter paper antibiotic disks applied within 15 minutes of inoculation; incubated inverted at **$35–37^\circ\text{C}$ for 16–18 hours**.
  * Reading & Interpretation: Diameter of **zone of inhibition** (including disk diameter) measured in millimeters using a caliper/ruler and interpreted as **Susceptible (S), Intermediate (I), or Resistant (R)** against standard CLSI / EUCAST interpretive breakpoint charts.

## 2. Immunology

### Hypersensitivity Reactions (Gell and Coombs Classification)
* **Type I (Immediate / Anaphylactic):**
  * *Mediator:* **IgE antibodies** bound to high-affinity $Fc\varepsilon RI$ receptors on tissue **mast cells and basophils**.
  * *Mechanism:* On re-exposure, multivalent allergen cross-links surface IgE → activates intracellular tyrosine kinases and $Ca^{2+}$ influx → rapid degranulation releasing preformed primary mediators (**Histamine**, ECF-A, NCF) followed by synthesized secondary lipid mediators (**Leukotrienes $LTC_4, LTD_4, LTE_4$ [SRS-A]**, Prostaglandin $PGD_2$, PAF).
  * *Clinical Examples:* Anaphylactic shock from Penicillin or local anaesthetics, bronchial asthma, allergic rhinitis, acute urticaria, angioedema.
  * *Emergency Dental Management of Anaphylaxis:* Stop dental procedure, lay patient supine with feet elevated, maintain patent airway, administer **Intramuscular Adrenaline (Epinephrine 1:1,000 solution): 0.5 mL (0.5 mg) IM into anterolateral thigh**, repeat every 5–15 minutes as needed; supplemental high-flow O₂, IV fluids, and secondary antihistamines/corticosteroids.
* **Type II (Cytotoxic / Antibody-Mediated):**
  * *Mediator:* **IgG or IgM** directed against cell-surface or tissue antigens.
  * *Mechanism:* Opsonization, membrane attack complex (MAC) complement lysis, and Antibody-Dependent Cellular Cytotoxicity (ADCC) via NK cells.
  * *Examples:* ABO incompatible blood transfusion reactions, Erythroblastosis fetalis (Rh incompatibility), Autoimmune hemolytic anemia, Goodpasture syndrome, Myasthenia gravis.
* **Type III (Immune Complex-Mediated):**
  * *Mediator:* Soluble **Antigen-Antibody (Ag-Ab) complexes** circulating in blood.
  * *Mechanism:* Immune complexes deposit in small blood vessel walls (especially kidneys, joints, skin), fix complement, and attract neutrophils via C5a → release of lysosomal enzymes causes necrotizing vasculitis.
  * *Examples:* Serum sickness, Arthus reaction, Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis (PSGN), Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE).
* **Type IV (Delayed-Type / Cell-Mediated):**
  * *Mediator:* **Sensitized T-lymphocytes** ($CD4^+$ Th1 and $CD8^+$ cytotoxic T cells); **no antibodies involved**.
  * *Mechanism:* Peak onset at **48–72 hours**. APCs present antigen to sensitized Th1 cells → secretion of cytokines (**IFN-$\gamma$, TNF-$\alpha$, IL-2**) → massive recruitment and activation of macrophages into epithelioid cells and giant cells.
  * *Examples:* **Mantoux Tuberculin Skin Test**, contact dermatitis (nickel allergy from orthodontic appliances, poison ivy, latex allergy), graft rejection.

## 3. Medical Parasitology & Mycology

### Malaria (*Plasmodium* Species)
* **Etiology:** Four major human species: *Plasmodium vivax, P. falciparum, P. malariae, P. ovale*.
* **Life Cycle:**
  * *Definitive Host:* Female *Anopheles* mosquito (undergoes sexual reproduction / **sporogony** resulting in infective sporozoites in salivary glands).
  * *Intermediate Host:* Humans (undergo asexual reproduction / **schizogony**).
  * *Human stages:* Exoerythrocytic (pre-erythrocytic) schizogony in hepatocytes → releases merozoites → Erythrocytic schizogony in RBCs (Ring form / early trophozoite → Ameboid trophozoite → Schizont containing merozoites and hemozoin pigment). RBC rupture releases pyrogens causing paroxysms of chills and fever. Hypnozoites (dormant liver stages) in *P. vivax* and *P. ovale* cause clinical **relapses**.
* **Laboratory Diagnosis:**
  * **Peripheral Blood Smear (Gold Standard):** Collected at peak of fever; stained with **Giemsa or Leishman stain**:
    * **Thick Smear:** Concentrates RBCs; high sensitivity for **screening and detecting presence** of malarial parasites (even low parasitemia).
    * **Thin Smear:** Preserves RBC morphology; used for **species identification and calculating % parasitemia**.
    * *Microscopic Features of P. falciparum:* Multiple delicate tiny ring forms per RBC, appliqué/accolé forms on RBC margin, no trophozoites/schizonts in peripheral blood (sequestered in deep capillaries), and characteristic **crescent / banana-shaped gametocytes**.
  * **Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs):** Immunochromatographic detection of *P. falciparum* **Histidine-Rich Protein 2 (HRP-2)** or pan-malarial **Parasite Lactate Dehydrogenase (pLDH)**.

### Lymphatic Filariasis (*Wuchereria bancrofti*)
* **Etiology & Habitat:** *Wuchereria bancrofti* (nematode). Adult worms reside inside human lymphatic vessels and lymph nodes (especially inguinal, scrotal, and abdominal).
* **Vector:** Female **$Culex\ quinquefasciatus$** mosquito.
* **Nocturnal Periodicity:** Microfilariae circulate in highest numbers in peripheral blood at night (**between 10:00 PM and 2:00 AM**), receding to pulmonary vasculature during the day, matching the nocturnal feeding habits of the vector mosquito.
* **Laboratory Diagnosis:**
  * *Night Blood Smear:* Thick blood film collected between 10 PM and 2 AM; stained with Giemsa or Leishman stain.
  * *Morphology:* Sheathed microfilaria (250–300 $\mu$m long), smooth graceful curves, column of distinct somatic cell nuclei that **does not extend to the tip of the tail (tail tip is pointed and free of nuclei)**.
  * *DEC Provocation Test:* Diethylcarbamazine (100 mg oral) mobilizes microfilariae into blood within 15–45 minutes for daytime collection.
  * *Circulating Filarial Antigen (CFA):* Card agglutination tests detect adult worm antigens in daytime blood.

### Medical Mycology: Dimorphism & *Candida albicans*
* **Thermal Dimorphism:** Fungi that exhibit two distinct temperature-dependent morphological forms:
  * *At room temperature ($25^\circ\text{C}$):* Grow as saprophytic, branching filamentous **molds** with hyphae.
  * *At body temperature ($37^\circ\text{C}$ / in host tissues):* Grow as pathogenic, budding **yeasts**.
  * *Mnemonic:* **"Mold in the Cold ($25^\circ\text{C}$), Yeast in the Beast ($37^\circ\text{C}$)"**.
  * *Examples:* **$Histoplasma\ capsulatum$** (intracellular yeast inside macrophages), *Blastomyces dermatitidis*, *Coccidioides immitis*, *Sporothrix schenckii*.
* **$Candida\ albicans$:**
  * Commensal yeast of the oral cavity, gastrointestinal tract, and vagina that causes opportunistic fungal infections (**Candidiasis / Thrush**) upon immune suppression, broad-spectrum antibiotic therapy, xerostomia, or corticosteroid use.
  * *Forms of Oral Candidiasis:* Pseudomembranous (curd-like white plaques that wipe off leaving a bleeding base), Erythematous (red painful patches, e.g. denture stomatitis), Hyperplastic (leukoplakia-like, cannot be wiped off), Angular cheilitis.
  * *Laboratory Diagnosis:*
    * *Direct Microscopy:* Gram stain shows **Gram-positive, oval budding yeast cells (blastospores)** and elongated **pseudohyphae**. 10% KOH wet mount reveals fungal elements.
    * *Culture:* Sabouraud's Dextrose Agar (SDA) incubated at $37^\circ\text{C}$ yields creamy white, pasty colonies with a characteristic yeast odor.
    * **Germ Tube Test (Reynolds-Braude Phenomenon):** Diagnostic rapid test. Yeast cells incubated in human serum at $37^\circ\text{C}$ for 2 hours produce cylindrical germ tube outgrowths **without constriction at the point of origin**.
    * *Cornmeal Agar:* Stimulates formation of thick-walled, spherical **chlamydospores**.

## 4. Virology & Oral Microbiology

### Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) & Dental Practice
* **Structure & Virology:** Member of *Hepadnaviridae*. Enveloped, partially double-stranded circular DNA virus (3.2 kb) containing an endogenous DNA polymerase with reverse transcriptase activity.
  * *Dane Particle:* The complete infectious virion (42 nm spherical structure).
  * **HBsAg (Surface Antigen):** Located on envelope. First serological marker to appear in blood (1–10 weeks post-exposure); persistence beyond 6 months indicates **chronic HBV infection**. Used as the antigen in the recombinant HBV vaccine.
  * **HBcAg (Core Antigen):** Intracellular nucleocapsid antigen; not detected free in serum.
  * **HBeAg (Envelope Antigen):** Soluble degradation product of nucleocapsid. Marker of active viral replication, high infectivity, and high transmission risk.
* **Serological Profile Interpretation:**
  * *Acute Infection:* **HBsAg (+), IgM anti-HBc (+)**, HBeAg (+).
  * *Window Period:* HBsAg becomes negative, anti-HBs not yet detectable; **IgM anti-HBc is the sole positive marker**.
  * *Recovery and Immunity from Natural Infection:* **Anti-HBs (+), IgG anti-HBc (+)**, HBsAg (-).
  * *Immunity from Vaccination:* **Anti-HBs (+) alone**; IgG anti-HBc is (-).
  * *Chronic Infection (High Infectivity):* HBsAg (+) > 6 months, IgG anti-HBc (+), **HBeAg (+)**.
* **Relevance to Dentistry & Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP):**
  * Transmitted via percutaneous inoculation (needle-stick injuries), blood, and saliva.
  * *Unvaccinated exposed health-worker:* Administer **Hepatitis B Immune Globulin (HBIG)** within 24–72 hours + initiate full 3-dose recombinant HBV vaccine series (0, 1, 6 months).

### Normal Oral Microflora & Dental Caries Microbiology
* **Normal Flora of Oral Cavity:**
  * *Pioneers & Predominant Organisms:* **$Streptococcus\ salivarius$** (dorsum of tongue and saliva), **$Streptococcus\ mitis$** and **$Streptococcus\ sanguinis$** (buccal mucosa and teeth), *Actinomyces viscosus* (subgingival and root surfaces).
  * *Beneficial Roles:* **Colonization Resistance** (blocks attachment of exogenous pathogens, competes for nutrients, produces lactic acid and inhibitory bacteriocins), stimulates maturation of local mucosal immune system (secretory IgA).
* **Microbiology of Dental Caries:**
  * Multifactorial infectious disease involving host susceptibility, cariogenic plaque microflora, substrate (fermentable carbohydrates), and time (Keyes' triad).
  * **$Streptococcus\ mutans$ (Primary Etiological Agent of Enamel Caries):**
    1. *Adherence:* Synthesizes extracellular, sticky, water-insoluble **glucans (dextrans)** from dietary sucrose using the enzyme **glucosyltransferase (GTF)**, mediating strong adherence to the acquired pellicle and promoting dental plaque biofilm accumulation.
    2. *Acidogenicity:* Rapidly ferments sucrose, glucose, and fructose via glycolysis into lactic acid.
    3. *Aciduricity:* Possesses a membrane-bound ATPase proton pump that allows it to survive and continue metabolic activity in low pH environments.
    4. *Critical pH:* When plaque pH falls below **5.5**, hydroxyapatite in dental enamel begins to dissolve (**demineralization**).
  * **$Lactobacillus$ Species (*L. acidophilus*, *L. casei*):** Secondary invaders; highly aciduric; responsible for the **progression of established cavitated dentinal caries**.
  * **Periodontal "Red Complex" Pathogens:** Strongly associated with severe chronic periodontitis: **$Porphyromonas\ gingivalis$**, **$Treponema\ denticola$**, and **$Tannerella\ forsythia$**.
