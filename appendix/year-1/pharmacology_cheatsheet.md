## 1. General Pharmacology & Pharmacokinetics

### Routes of Drug Administration
* **Oral Route:**
  * *Advantages:* Most convenient, safe, painless, economical, non-invasive; self-administration possible; overdose can be managed by gastric lavage or activated charcoal.
  * *Disadvantages:* Slow onset of action (unsuitable in acute emergencies); subject to **first-pass hepatic metabolism**; erratic absorption (food interactions, emesis, diarrhea); unpalatable/irritant drugs cause nausea; cannot be used in unconscious or uncooperative patients.
* **Intravenous (IV) Route:**
  * *Advantages:* **100% Bioavailability ($F=1.0$)**; immediate onset of action (ideal for dental/medical emergencies: anaphylaxis, status epilepticus); precise titration of dosage; allows administration of large volumes and irritating hypertonic solutions.
  * *Disadvantages:* Injected drug **cannot be recalled** (high toxicity risk); requires strict asepsis; risk of extravasation, thrombophlebitis, and air embolism; requires trained personnel.
* **Sublingual Route:**
  * Drug tablet placed beneath the tongue dissolves rapidly in oral fluids and is absorbed through the non-keratinized, highly vascular oral mucosa directly into the systemic venous circulation (superior vena cava).
  * *Key Advantage:* **Completely bypasses the portal circulation and first-pass hepatic metabolism**, yielding a rapid therapeutic onset within 1–3 minutes (e.g. **Nitroglycerin / Glyceryl Trinitrate** in acute angina pectoris; Sublingual Buprenorphine).

### Pharmacokinetic Principles
* **First-Pass (Presystemic) Metabolism:**
  * The enzymatic degradation and metabolism of an orally administered drug during its passage from the gastrointestinal lumen across the gut wall and through the liver via the portal vein **before reaching the systemic arterial circulation**.
  * *Clinical Significance:* Drastically reduces oral bioavailability, necessitating higher oral doses compared to IV doses, or alternate routes of administration (sublingual, transdermal, parenteral).
  * *Drugs with extensive first-pass metabolism:* **Lignocaine / Lidocaine** (ineffective orally), **Propranolol**, **Nitroglycerin**, **Morphine**, **Salbutamol**.
* **Bioavailability ($F$):**
  * The fraction or percentage of an administered dose of unchanged drug that reaches the systemic circulation in active form:
    $$\text{Bioavailability } (F) = \frac{\text{AUC}_{\text{oral}}}{\text{AUC}_{\text{IV}}} \times \frac{\text{Dose}_{\text{IV}}}{\text{Dose}_{\text{oral}}} \times 100$$
* **Plasma Half-Life ($t_{1/2}$):**
  * The time required for the plasma concentration of a drug to decline by 50%:
    $$t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693 \times V_d}{\text{Clearance (CL)}}$$
  * *Rule of Steady State:* During continuous administration or constant-interval dosing, it takes **4 to 5 half-lives ($4–5 \times t_{1/2}$)** for plasma drug concentration to reach **steady state ($C_{ss}$)**, and similarly 4 to 5 half-lives to eliminate >95% of the drug once administration ceases.
* **Prodrug:**
  * An inactive or weakly active pharmacological compound that requires *in vivo* metabolic biotransformation (usually enzymatic) to yield the biologically active therapeutic drug.
  * *Advantages:* Improves oral bioavailability and absorption, reduces local GI irritation, prolongs drug half-life, or achieves site-specific tissue targeting.
  * *Examples:* **Enalapril** → Enalaprilat; **Levodopa** → Dopamine; **Acyclovir** → Acyclovir triphosphate; **Omeprazole** → Sulfenamide; **Aspirin** → Salicylic acid.

### Drug Metabolism (Biotransformation)
* Primarily occurs in hepatocytes to convert lipophilic, non-polar compounds into polar, hydrophilic, water-soluble metabolites for renal or biliary excretion:
  1. **Phase I Reactions (Non-Synthetic / Functionalization):**
     * Introduce or unmask a polar functional group ($-OH, -NH_2, -SH, -COOH$).
     * *Reactions:* **Oxidation** (catalyzed primarily by microsomal **Cytochrome P450 / CYP450** monooxygenases), **Reduction**, and **Hydrolysis**.
     * Result: Drug is converted into a more polar metabolite, which may be active, inactive, or toxic.
  2. **Phase II Reactions (Synthetic / Conjugation):**
     * An endogenous polar substrate is covalently attached to the functional group of the parent drug or Phase I metabolite.
     * *Reactions:* **Glucuronidation** (catalyzed by UDP-glucuronosyltransferases; most common Phase II pathway), **Sulfation**, **Acetylation** (NAT2 enzyme), **Glutathione (GSH) conjugation**, and Glycine conjugation.
     * Result: Almost invariably yields **pharmacologically inactive, highly polar, water-soluble excretable compounds**.

## 2. Autonomic Nervous System & Toxicology

### Cholinergic Drugs & Anticholinergics
* **Organophosphorus (OP) Poisoning:**
  * *Mechanism:* Irreversibly binds and phosphorylates the active esteratic site of the enzyme **Acetylcholinesterase (AChE)**, inactivating it and causing massive, toxic accumulation of Acetylcholine (ACh) at all muscarinic and nicotinic synapses throughout the peripheral and central nervous systems.
  * *Clinical Manifestations (SLUDGE / DUMBELS):*
    * *Muscarinic effects:* **S**alivation, **L**acrimation, **U**rination, **D**efecation, **G**astrointestinal hypermotility/cramps, **E**mesis, **M**iosis (pinpoint pupils), **B**radycardia, **B**ronchospasm, and profuse bronchorrhea.
    * *Nicotinic effects:* Muscle twitching, fasciculations, muscle weakness, and flaccid paralysis (including respiratory diaphragm paralysis).
    * *CNS effects:* Restlessness, confusion, convulsions, coma, and respiratory depression.
  * *Treatment Protocol:*
    1. *Resuscitation & Decontamination:* Remove contaminated clothing, wash skin thoroughly with soap and water, secure airway, administer supplemental oxygen.
    2. **Atropine Sulfate (Specific Competitive Muscarinic Antagonist):**
       * Administer **2 mg IV immediately**, repeated every 5–10 minutes until signs of full **atropinization** appear: drying of bronchial/pulmonary secretions, dry mouth, clearing of rhonchi, heart rate > 80–100 bpm, and pupillary dilation. *Atropine reverses muscarinic and central effects, but has NO effect on nicotinic neuromuscular paralysis.*
    3. **Pralidoxime / 2-PAM (Cholinesterase Reactivator):**
       * Specific chemical oxime that binds the organophosphate-enzyme complex, dislodging the phosphate group and **reactivating free Acetylcholinesterase**.
       * *Dose:* 1–2 g IV slow infusion over 15–30 minutes. Reverses nicotinic muscle paralysis and fasciculations.
       * *Crucial Timing:* **Must be administered early (within 24–36 hours)** before chemical conformational stabilization ("aging") of the phosphorylated enzyme occurs.
* **Atropine (Anticholinergic / Muscarinic Blocker):**
  * Competitive antagonist of all muscarinic receptor subtypes ($M_1, M_2, M_3$).
  * *Therapeutic Uses:*
    1. **Pre-anesthetic medication:** Reduces salivary, pharyngeal, and respiratory secretions to prevent intraoperative airway obstruction and laryngospasm.
    2. **Sinus Bradycardia & AV Block:** Blocks vagal tone on the SA node to restore normal sinus heart rate.
    3. **Organophosphorus and Carbamate Poisoning:** Life-saving antidote to reverse muscarinic hypersecretion.
    4. **Ophthalmology:** Produces prolonged mydriasis and cycloplegia for diagnostic refraction testing and iridocyclitis.
    5. **Antispasmodic:** Relieves smooth muscle spasm in intestinal and renal colic.
  * *Contraindications:* Narrow-angle glaucoma (precipitates acute angle closure), Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (triggers acute urinary retention).

### Adrenergic Drugs & Blockers
* **Adrenaline (Epinephrine):**
  * Potent non-selective direct agonist at $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \beta_1, \beta_2$ adrenergic receptors.
  * *Therapeutic Uses:*
    1. **Drug of Choice for Anaphylactic Shock:** Administer **0.5 mL (0.5 mg) of 1:1,000 solution Intramuscularly (IM)** into the anterolateral thigh. Reverses bronchospasm ($\beta_2$), counteracts systemic vasodilation and restores blood pressure ($\alpha_1$), and inhibits mast cell mediator release ($\beta_2$).
    2. **Vasoconstrictor additive in Local Anesthetics (1:80,000 to 1:200,000):** Reduces operative bleeding, prolongs anesthesia duration, and reduces systemic toxicity.
    3. **Cardiac Arrest:** Restores rhythm during cardiopulmonary resuscitation (1 mg IV of 1:10,000 solution).
* **Adrenergic Antagonists:**
  * **Prazosin:** Highly selective competitive **$\alpha_1$-adrenergic receptor antagonist**. Causes arteriolar and venous vasodilation without reflex tachycardia (preserves inhibitory presynaptic $\alpha_2$ autoreceptors).
    * *Uses:* Essential hypertension; symptomatic relief of **Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)** (relaxes prostatic smooth muscle and bladder neck).
    * *Adverse Effect:* **First-dose syncope** (severe postural/orthostatic hypotension occurring within 30–90 minutes of the first dose; prevent by starting at low dose [0.5 mg] at bedtime).
  * **Propranolol:** Non-selective competitive **$\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$ adrenergic receptor blocker**.
    * *Effects:* Decreases heart rate, myocardial contractility, and cardiac output ($\beta_1$ blockade); causes bronchoconstriction and inhibits glycogenolysis ($\beta_2$ blockade).
    * *Uses:* Hypertension, Angina pectoris, post-Myocardial Infarction cardioprotection, supraventricular tachyarrhythmias, essential tremor, somatic anxiety/performance tremor, migraine prophylaxis.
    * *Contraindications:* **Bronchial asthma / COPD** (triggers fatal bronchospasm), severe bradycardia, 2nd- or 3rd-degree AV block, peripheral vascular disease.

## 3. Analgesics, NSAIDs & Dental Pain Management

### NSAIDs Classification & Mechanisms
* **Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs):**
  * *Non-Selective COX-1 / COX-2 Inhibitors:* Aspirin, Ibuprofen, Diclofenac, Naproxen, Indomethacin, Ketorolac.
  * *Preferential COX-2 Inhibitors:* Meloxicam, Nimesulide.
  * *Selective COX-2 Inhibitors (Coxibs):* Celecoxib, Etoricoxib, Parecoxib.
  * *Analgesic-Antipyretic with Poor Anti-Inflammatory Action:* **Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)**.
* **Mechanism of Action:**
  * Inhibit the enzyme **Cyclooxygenase (COX / Prostaglandin Endoperoxide Synthase)**, blocking the conversion of membrane-derived Arachidonic acid into prostaglandin $PGG_2$ and $PGH_2$, thereby preventing the downstream synthesis of **Prostaglandins ($PGE_2, PGF_{2\alpha}, PGI_2$)** and **Thromboxane $A_2$ ($TXA_2$)**.

### Aspirin (Acetylsalicylic Acid)
* **Mechanism:** **Irreversibly acetylates** and inactivates both COX-1 (at Ser-529) and COX-2 (at Ser-516). Platelets lack nuclei and cannot synthesize new enzyme; thus, platelet COX-1 inhibition lasts for the entire **lifespan of the platelet (7–10 days)**.
* **Dose-Dependent Actions:**
  1. *Low Dose (75–150 mg/day - Antiplatelet):* Selectively blocks platelet $TXA_2$ synthesis without significantly suppressing endothelial prostacyclin ($PGI_2$) production. Used for primary and secondary prevention of myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, and post-stent thrombosis.
  2. *Intermediate Dose (300–600 mg every 4–6 hours - Analgesic & Antipyretic):* Relieves mild to moderate pain (headache, dental pain, dysmenorrhea) and reduces fever by resetting hypothalamic thermoregulatory set-point.
  3. *High Dose (3–5 g/day - Anti-Inflammatory):* Suppresses chronic joint inflammation in acute rheumatic fever and rheumatoid arthritis.
* **Adverse Effects:**
  * **Gastric Mucosal Ulceration & GI Bleeding:** COX-1 inhibition suppresses protective prostaglandins ($PGE_2, PGI_2$) that stimulate gastric mucus and bicarbonate secretion and maintain mucosal blood flow.
  * **Prolonged Bleeding Time:** Increases surgical hemorrhage risk; **must be stopped 5–7 days before major dental extraction or periodontal surgery**.
  * **Aspirin-Induced Asthma (Samter's Triad):** Diverting arachidonic acid metabolism exclusively into the 5-lipoxygenase (5-LOX) pathway causes overproduction of bronchoconstrictor leukotrienes ($LTC_4, LTD_4, LTE_4$).
  * **Reye's Syndrome:** Fatal hepatic encephalopathy and fatty liver occurring when aspirin is administered to children or adolescents with viral infections (varicella/influenza). *Aspirin is strictly contraindicated in children; use Paracetamol instead.*
  * **Salicylism (Toxicity):** Tinnitus, hearing impairment, vertigo, hyperventilation (respiratory alkalosis followed by metabolic acidosis), and confusion.

### Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)
* Potent central analgesic and antipyretic with negligible peripheral anti-inflammatory activity. Does not cause gastric mucosal damage or affect platelet aggregation or bleeding time. First-line analgesic in patients with peptic ulcers, bleeding diathesis, asthma, and in children.
* **Overdose Hepatotoxicity Mechanism:**
  * At normal therapeutic doses, 95% of paracetamol undergoes harmless hepatic Phase II conjugation via **glucuronidation and sulfation**, while ~5% is metabolized by microsomal **CYP2E1** into the highly toxic, electrophilic reactive intermediate **NAPQI (N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine)**.
  * Toxic NAPQI is immediately neutralized and detoxified by conjugation with endogenous hepatic **Glutathione (GSH)** into non-toxic mercapturic acid conjugates.
  * In acute overdose (>10–15 g in adults), normal glucuronide/sulfate pathways are saturated, shunting large amounts through CYP2E1. Once hepatic glutathione stores are depleted by >70%, free reactive NAPQI covalently binds sulfhydryl groups of vital hepatocyte mitochondrial and cytoplasmic proteins, triggering **massive centrilobular hepatic necrosis and acute liver failure**.
* **Specific Antidote:** **N-Acetylcysteine (NAC):**
  * *Mechanism:* Replenishes intracellular glutathione stores by serving as a cysteine precursor, and directly binds and detoxifies free circulating NAPQI. Most effective when administered intravenously within **8–10 hours** of ingestion.

### Selective COX-2 Inhibitors (Coxibs)
* Selectively inhibit inducible COX-2 (upregulated at sites of inflammation) while sparing constitutive COX-1 (present in gastric mucosa, platelets, and renal vasculature).
* *Advantage:* Markedly reduced incidence of gastric mucosal erosion, peptic ulceration, and GI bleeding compared to non-selective NSAIDs.
* *Cardiovascular Risk:* Selectively inhibit endothelial **Prostacyclin ($PGI_2$)** (a vasodilator and inhibitor of platelet aggregation) without inhibiting platelet COX-1-mediated **Thromboxane $A_2$ ($TXA_2$)** (a potent vasoconstrictor and platelet aggregator). This creates an unbuffered prothrombotic imbalance, significantly **increasing the risk of myocardial infarction, stroke, and thrombotic cardiovascular events**.

## 4. Local Anaesthetics (LA)

### Chemical Classification
* **Esters:** Procaine, Benzocaine, Tetracaine, Chloroprocaine, Cocaine.
  * Metabolized rapidly in plasma by **plasma pseudocholinesterases / butyrylcholinesterase**.
  * Form the degradation product **para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA)**, associated with a higher incidence of true allergic reactions.
* **Amides:** **Lignocaine (Lidocaine)**, **Bupivacaine**, **Mepivacaine**, **Prilocaine**, **Articaine**.
  * Metabolized predominantly by hepatic microsomal **Cytochrome P450 enzymes**.
  * True allergic reactions are extremely rare.
  * *Nodal Mnemonic Rule:* All amide local anesthetics contain the letter **"i" TWICE** in their generic name (L**i**gnoca**i**ne, Bup**i**vaca**i**ne, Pr**i**loca**i**ne, Art**i**ca**i**ne), whereas esters contain the letter "i" only once (Procane, Benzoca**i**ne, Tetracane).

### Mechanism of Action of Lignocaine (Lidocaine)
1. Local anesthetics are weak bases ($pK_a$ 7.7–8.5). In the alkaline/neutral extracellular fluid (pH 7.4), a fraction exists in the **un-ionized, lipophilic, base form**, which readily diffuses across the axonal phospholipid bilayer membrane into the axoplasm.
2. Inside the axoplasm (pH ~7.1), the molecule accepts a proton and converts into the **ionized, cationic form**.
3. The ionized cationic molecule binds with high affinity to a specific receptor binding site within the **intracellular inner vestibule of voltage-gated $Na^+$ channels**.
4. This stabilizes the $Na^+$ channel in the **inactivated state**, physically occluding the channel pore and preventing $Na^+$ influx.
5. Halts depolarization: threshold potential is never reached; generation and propagation of the nerve action potential is completely blocked (**conduction block**).
* **Differential Nerve Block:** Small-diameter unmyelinated **C fibers** and small myelinated **A$\delta$ fibers** (transmitting pain and temperature sensations) have short critical lengths and high firing frequencies, so they are **blocked first**, followed sequentially by fibers transmitting touch, pressure, and lastly large myelinated **A$\alpha$ motor fibers**.

### Rationale for Combining Adrenaline (Epinephrine) with Lignocaine
* Typically formulated as **2% Lignocaine with 1:80,000 or 1:200,000 Adrenaline**:
  1. **Produces Local Vasoconstriction:** Counteracts the intrinsic vasodilator action of lignocaine on local blood vessels via vascular $\alpha_1$-adrenergic receptor stimulation.
  2. **Decreases Systemic Absorption:** Slows down the rate of removal of lignocaine from the injection site into the systemic circulation.
  3. **Prolongs Anesthesia Duration:** Increases the duration of effective dental pulpal and soft tissue anesthesia by 2- to 3-fold (from ~30–45 minutes to **2–4 hours**).
  4. **Reduces Systemic Toxicity:** Lower peak plasma drug concentrations decrease the risk of CNS toxicity (drowsiness, seizures) and cardiac toxicity (arrhythmias).
  5. **Provides Local Hemostasis:** Vasoconstriction reduces capillary bleeding in the operative field during dental extractions, periodontal surgery, and minor oral surgical procedures.
  6. **Increases Maximum Safe Dose:** Increases the maximum permissible safe dose of lignocaine in adults from **4.5 mg/kg (max 300 mg) without adrenaline** to **7.0 mg/kg (max 500 mg) with adrenaline**.
* **Contraindications to Adrenaline in Dental LA:**
  * Severe uncontrolled hypertension, recent Myocardial Infarction (< 6 months), unstable angina, uncontrolled hyperthyroidism/thyrotoxicosis, pheochromocytoma, and patients taking non-selective $\beta$-blockers (propranolol: triggers unopposed $\alpha_1$ vasoconstrictive hypertensive crisis).
  * Infiltration around anatomical **end-arteries** (fingers, toes, tip of nose, pinna, penis) to prevent ischemic necrosis and gangrene.

## 5. Antimicrobial Chemotherapy & Anticoagulants

### Anticoagulant Therapy: Heparin vs. Warfarin
| Feature | Heparin (Unfractionated) | Warfarin (Coumarin Derivative) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Route & Onset** | Parenteral (IV / SC); **Immediate onset of action** (minutes) | Oral; **Delayed onset of action (36–72 hours)** (requires clearance of pre-existing circulating clotting factors) |
| **Mechanism of Action** | Binds to and induces a conformational change in **Antithrombin III (AT-III)**, accelerating its enzymatic inactivation of **Thrombin (Factor IIa)** and **Factor Xa** by 1,000-fold | Competitively inhibits the enzyme **Vitamin K Epoxide Reductase (VKORC1)**, blocking the regeneration of reduced Vitamin KH₂ and preventing post-translational **$\gamma$-carboxylation of glutamic acid residues on Factors II, VII, IX, X, and Proteins C & S** |
| **Site of Action** | Blood *in vivo* and *in vitro* (effective in test tubes) | In liver *in vivo* only (ineffective *in vitro*) |
| **Laboratory Monitoring** | **Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (APTT)** (Target: 1.5–2.5 $\times$ control) | **Prothrombin Time (PT)** expressed as **International Normalized Ratio (INR)** (Target: **2.0–3.0** for DVT/AF; 2.5–3.5 for mechanical prosthetic heart valves) |
| **Antidote / Reversal** | **Protamine Sulfate** (basic peptide that neutralizes acidic heparin by chemical electrostatic binding) | **Vitamin K1 (Phytonadione)** (requires hours) and **Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP)** / Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC) for immediate reversal |
| **Pregnancy Safety** | Safe (large charged polymer; does not cross placenta) | **Teratogenic (Contraindicated in pregnancy)**: crosses placenta causing warfarin embryopathy (chondrodysplasia punctata, nasal hypoplasia) and fetal hemorrhage |

### Dental Antibiotic Prophylaxis (Infective Endocarditis Prevention)
* Indicated in patients with high-risk cardiac conditions (prosthetic heart valves, previous infective endocarditis, unrepaired cyanotic congenital heart disease) undergoing invasive dental procedures involving **gingival manipulation, periapical manipulation, or perforation of oral mucosa** (e.g. extractions, scaling, subgingival band placement):
  * **Standard Regimen:** **Amoxicillin 2.0 g orally** in adults (50 mg/kg in children) administered as a single dose **30 to 60 minutes prior to the procedure**.
  * **Allergic to Penicillin:** **Clindamycin 600 mg orally** (20 mg/kg in children) OR **Azithromycin / Clarithromycin 500 mg orally** 30–60 minutes prior to the procedure.

### Antimalarial: Chloroquine
* **Mechanism of Action:** Chloroquine is a weak diprotic base that passively diffuses across membranes and concentrates heavily within the highly acidic intraerythrocytic food vacuole of the intraerythrocytic malarial parasite (*Plasmodium*). Inside the acidic vacuole, it becomes protonated and trapped. It inhibits the biocrystallization enzyme **heme polymerase**, preventing the conversion of toxic, free reactive heme (ferriprotoporphyrin IX, released during hemoglobin digestion) into non-toxic, insoluble crystalline **hemozoin pigment**. The free reactive heme accumulates, poisoning parasite food vacuoles, causing lipid peroxidation, membrane lysis, and parasite death.
* **Therapeutic Uses:** Clinical cure and suppressive prophylaxis of sensitive malaria (*P. vivax, P. malariae, P. ovale*, and chloroquine-sensitive *P. falciparum*); Extraintestinal amebiasis (hepatic amebic abscess); Anti-inflammatory agent in Rheumatoid Arthritis and Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (discoid lupus).
* **Adverse Effects:** Pruritus (severe and distressing in dark-skinned individuals), nausea, vomiting, epigastric distress, headache, reversible corneal deposits, and **irreversible retinopathy ("Bull's-Eye" Maculopathy)** with prolonged high cumulative dosages; QT prolongation on ECG.
