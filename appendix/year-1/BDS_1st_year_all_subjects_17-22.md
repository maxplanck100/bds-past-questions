*Cross-Disciplinary Rapid Review Guide for Kathmandu University BDS I Examinations*

This consolidated cheatsheet distills the high-frequency intersections, cross-subject themes, and universal examination staples across all seven basic sciences tested in the 2017–2022 Kathmandu University examination collection (`extracted/year-1/BDS_1st_year_all_subjects_17-22.md`).

## 1. The Head, Neck & Craniofacial Axis

### Muscles of Mastication, TMJ & Mandibular Development
* **Anatomy — Muscles of Mastication:**
  * All four muscles innervated by **Mandibular nerve ($V_3$)**.
  * *Masseter, Temporalis, Medial Pterygoid:* Elevators of the mandible (close the mouth).
  * *Lateral Pterygoid:* **Depresses (initiates mouth opening)**, protrudes (bilateral), and performs side-to-side grinding (unilateral). *Only muscle of mastication that opens the mouth.*
* **Anatomy — Temporomandibular Joint (TMJ):**
  * Atypical synovial **bicondylar / ginglymoarthrodial** joint lined with **fibrocartilage**.
  * Biconcave articular disc divides joint into: upper compartment (gliding/translation) and lower compartment (hinge/rotation).
* **Oral Biology — Mandibular Development & Meckel's Cartilage:**
  * Mandible develops in the 1st pharyngeal arch by **intramembranous ossification lateral to Meckel's cartilage** (begins at mental foramen at 6th–7th week IUL).
  * *Fate of Meckel's Cartilage:* Does **not** directly form the mandibular ramus or body. Dorsal end becomes **Malleus** and **Incus**; perichondrium becomes **Sphenomandibular Ligament**; remaining ventral cartilage is resorbed.
  * *Condylar Cartilage:* Major secondary endochondral growth center active until adulthood (**20–21 years**).
* **Pharmacology — Local Anesthesia for Mandibular Nerve Block:**
  * 2% Lignocaine with 1:80,000 Adrenaline binds intracellularly to **voltage-gated $Na^+$ channels**, blocking depolarization. Adrenaline provides local vasoconstriction, slows absorption, prolongs pulpal anesthesia to 2–4 hours, and reduces peak toxicity.

### Dental Anatomy & Tooth Morphology
* **Permanent Maxillary Canine:** Longest tooth in dentition; prominent labial ridge; **mesial cusp slope is shorter than distal cusp slope**; lingual ridge divides lingual fossa into mesiolingual and distolingual fossae.
* **Permanent Mandibular First Molar:** 5 cusps (3 buccal [MB, DB, D], 2 lingual [ML, DL]); pentagonal occlusal outline; "Y" or "W" occlusal groove pattern; **two roots** (mesial root has 2 canals).
* **Permanent Maxillary First Molar:** Rhomboidal occlusal outline; 4 major cusps + **Cusp of Carabelli** on mesiopalatal cusp; **Oblique Ridge** connects distobuccal cusp to mesiopalatal cusp; 3 divergent roots (palatal, MB, DB).

## 2. Blood, Hemostasis & Circulation Axis

### Red Blood Cells & Anemia
* **Physiology — Erythropoiesis & Blood Groups:**
  * Regulated by **Erythropoietin (EPO)** in response to hypoxia. Hemoglobin synthesis begins in the intermediate normoblast; nucleus extruded in late normoblast.
  * *ABO System:* Landsteiner's Law (universal donor = O; universal recipient = AB).
  * *Rh System & Erythroblastosis Fetalis:* Rh- mother sensitized by Rh+ fetus during delivery produces anti-D IgG; subsequent Rh+ fetus attacked → hemolysis, kernicterus, hydrops fetalis. **Prevention: RhoGAM at 28 weeks and within 72 hours post-partum**.
* **Biochemistry — Iron Metabolism:**
  * Absorbed in duodenum as $Fe^{2+}$ (facilitated by gastric acid and Vitamin C); transported in blood bound to **Transferrin**; stored as **Ferritin** and hemosiderin. Regulated negatively by **Hepcidin** (blocks ferroportin).
* **Pathology — Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA):**
  * Microcytic, hypochromic anemia (MCV < 80 fL, MCH < 27 pg, high RDW > 15%).
  * *Biochemical profile:* **Serum Ferritin markedly low (< 15 $\mu$g/L)**, Serum Iron low, **TIBC elevated**, Transferrin saturation < 15%. Bone marrow shows absence of hemosiderin on Prussian blue.

### Hemostasis & Anticoagulant Pharmacology
* **Physiology — Hemostatic Cascade:**
  * Vascular spasm → Platelet plug formation (vWF adhesion, ADP and $TXA_2$ release, GP IIb/IIIa aggregation) → Coagulation cascade (Factor Xa + Va + $Ca^{2+} +$ PL form prothrombinase → converts Prothrombin to Thrombin → converts Fibrinogen to Fibrin meshwork crosslinked by Factor XIIIa).
* **Pharmacology — Anticoagulants (Heparin vs. Warfarin):**
  * *Heparin:* Parenteral, rapid onset, activates Antithrombin III, monitored by **APTT**, antidote = **Protamine Sulfate**.
  * *Warfarin:* Oral, delayed onset (36–72 hrs), inhibits Vitamin K epoxide reductase (VKORC1) blocking Factors II, VII, IX, X, monitored by **PT/INR (target 2.0–3.0)**, antidote = **Vitamin K1** and FFP. Teratogenic!
* **Pathology — Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC):**
  * Widespread microvascular thrombosis consuming platelets and coagulation factors → severe bleeding. Laboratory: thrombocytopenia, prolonged PT/APTT, decreased fibrinogen, elevated **D-dimer**, and **schistocytes** on blood smear.

## 3. Infection Control, Inflammation & Antimicrobial Therapy

### Sterilization & Infection Control
* **Microbiology — Autoclave (Moist Heat Under Pressure):**
  * Kills by **protein denaturation and coagulation**.
  * Standard parameters: **$121^\circ\text{C}$ at 15 psi for 15 minutes**.
  * Controls: Biological indicator = **$Geobacillus\ stearothermophilus$** spores; Chemical = Bowie-Dick tape and Browne's tubes.
* **Microbiology — Kirby-Bauer Disk Diffusion Method:**
  * Mueller-Hinton agar (4 mm depth), 0.5 McFarland bacterial suspension, incubated at $35–37^\circ\text{C}$ for 16–18 hours; zone of inhibition measured in mm.

### Inflammation & Wound Healing
* **Pathology — Acute Inflammation:**
  * *Vascular:* Vasodilation (histamine, NO) + increased vascular permeability (inter-endothelial gaps in post-capillary venules) → edema and stasis.
  * *Cellular:* Margination → Rolling (**Selectins**) → Adhesion (**Integrins / ICAM-1**) → Transmigration (**PECAM-1 / CD31**) → Chemotaxis (C5a, $LTB_4$, IL-8) → Phagocytosis (opsonins: IgG, C3b) → Intracellular killing via **NADPH oxidase and MPO-halide system**.
* **Pathology — Wound Healing:**
  * *Primary Intention:* Clean surgical incised wound, margins approximated, minimal granulation tissue and scarring.
  * *Secondary Intention:* Large tissue loss (extraction socket), open margins, abundant granulation tissue, wound contraction by **myofibroblasts**, large scar. *Infection is the #1 local delaying factor; Vitamin C deficiency delays collagen synthesis.*

### Dental Antimicrobial & Analgesic Pharmacology
* **Pharmacology — NSAIDs:**
  * *Aspirin:* Irreversible acetylation of COX-1 and COX-2. Antiplatelet cardioprotection at 75–150 mg/day ($TXA_2$ inhibition). Adverse effects: peptic ulcers, prolonged bleeding, Reye's syndrome in children, salicylism.
  * *Paracetamol:* Central COX inhibition; safe in ulcers/bleeding. Overdose toxic metabolite **NAPQI** causes centrilobular liver necrosis; antidote = **N-Acetylcysteine (NAC)**.
* **Pharmacology & Microbiology — Infective Endocarditis Prophylaxis:**
  * Indicated for high-risk cardiac patients undergoing invasive dental extractions/scaling: **Amoxicillin 2.0 g orally** (or Clindamycin 600 mg orally in penicillin-allergic patients) **30–60 minutes prior to procedure**.

## 4. Cellular Biology, Oncology & Energy Metabolism

### Cell Injury, Apoptosis & Neoplasia
* **Pathology — Apoptosis Pathways:**
  * *Intrinsic (Mitochondrial):* DNA damage/p53 → Bax/Bak pore formation → **Cytochrome c** release → Apaf-1 apoptosome → initiator **Caspase-9**.
  * *Extrinsic (Death Receptor):* FasL/Fas or TNF/TNFR1 → FADD → initiator **Caspase-8**.
  * Both activate executioner **Caspases 3, 6, 7** → internucleosomal DNA fragmentation (180–200 bp ladder) and apoptotic bodies with phosphatidylserine "eat-me" signal.
* **Pathology — Neoplasia & Metastasis:**
  * Benign (well-differentiated, slow, encapsulated, never metastasizes) vs. Malignant (anaplasia, pleomorphism, high N:C ratio, invasive, metastasizes).
  * *Routes of spread:* Lymphatic (carcinomas → regional lymph nodes), Hematogenous (sarcomas → liver, lungs), Seeding of cavities (Krukenberg tumor).
* **Pathology — Hematologic Neoplasms:**
  * *AML:* $\ge 20\%$ myeloblasts, **Auer rods** (pathognomonic), MPO positive.
  * *Hodgkin Lymphoma:* **Reed-Sternberg (RS) cells** (large, binucleated "owl-eye" nuclei with prominent eosinophilic nucleoli; CD15+, CD30+).

### Bioenergetics & Metabolic Pathways
* **Biochemistry — Oxidative Phosphorylation & ETC:**
  * Complexes I, III, IV pump protons ($H^+$) into the mitochondrial intermembrane space, creating an electrochemical proton gradient. Protons flow back through **ATP Synthase (Complex V)** driving ATP phosphorylation (Chemiosmotic Theory).
* **Biochemistry — Lipoproteins:**
  * *Chylomicrons:* Dietary TG transport.
  * *VLDL:* Endogenous hepatic TG transport.
  * *LDL:* Delivers cholesterol to tissues (**Atherogenic**).
  * *HDL:* Mediates **Reverse Cholesterol Transport** from tissues to liver (**Cardioprotective**).

## 5. Master Kathmandu University BDS Exam Scoring Formula

1. **Section Breakdown (50-Mark Format):**
   * Section B (25 marks) + Section C (25 marks).
   * Typically 1 Long Question (6 marks), 2 Short Answer Questions (5 marks each), and 1 Short Notes Question (3 sub-questions $\times$ 3 marks = 9 marks).
2. **Diagram Rules for Full Marks:**
   * In **Anatomy and Oral Biology**, diagrams carry 2 to 3 marks out of every 5-to-6 mark question. Always draw clean, labeled line diagrams.
   * In **Physiology and Biochemistry**, draw sequential flowcharts and reaction schemes.
3. **Tabular Contrasts:**
   * Whenever asked to "differentiate", "compare and contrast", or "distinguish", always use a **multi-column Markdown-style table** specifying clear parameters of comparison (e.g. Definition, Site, Mechanism, Clinical Examples).
