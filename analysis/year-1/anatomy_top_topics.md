# BDS 1st Year — Anatomy: Topic Frequency & Exam Prediction Analysis

Based on Kathmandu University BDS 1st Year Anatomy examination papers (2015–2022), extracted from `extracted/year-1/anatomy.md` and `extracted/year-1/BDS_1st_year_all_subjects_17-22.md` (spanning 10 unique exam sessions). Counting method: each **topic** is counted once per **paper** in which it appears. Confidence percentages for future-exam predictions combine historical frequency with recency weighting (heavier weight given to 2019–2022 sessions).

## Sample Question Type per Top Topic

**1. Muscles of Mastication**
> "Name muscles of mastication with their origin, insertion, action and nerve supply." *(2020)*

**2. Lymphoid Histology**
> "Describe the histological structures of spleen with well labeled diagram." *(2019, 2022)*

**3. Synovial Joints & TMJ**
> "Enlist the type of synovial joint with one example of each." *(2022)* / "Describe temporomandibular joint under: articulating bones, ligaments and relations." *(2018)*

**4. Carotid Triangle**
> "Describe the boundaries, contents and applied anatomy of carotid triangle." *(2017, 2018, 2021)*

**5. Brachial Plexus**
> "Write about the formation of Brachial Plexus with suitable diagram. Mention any two applied anatomy." *(2022)*

**6. Right Atrium & Heart**
> "Discuss the interior of right atrium with a well labelled diagram." *(2022)*

**7. Ossification**

**8. General Histology (Skin & Muscles)**
> "Mention the histological differences between skeletal muscle and cardiac muscle." *(2022)*

**9. Axilla**
> "Write down the boundaries, contents and applied anatomy of axilla." *(2018, 2022)*

**10. Pharyngeal Apparatus**
> "Mention the derivatives of branchial apparatus." *(2022)*

## Notes and Limitations

- Dataset includes 10 papers from 2015 to 2022 from both BDS I separate anatomy papers and consolidated BDS 1st year sets.
- BDS anatomy places disproportionately heavy emphasis on Head and Neck (Muscles of mastication, TMJ, Carotid triangle, Infratemporal fossa, Branchial arches) compared to general MBBS anatomy.
