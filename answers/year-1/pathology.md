## September 3, 2021 (New Course, 50 Marks)

### Section B

**1.** **Neoplasia:** Abnormal, uncontrolled, purposeless proliferation of cells with heritable changes, persisting after removal of the stimulus that initiated it.

**Differences between benign and malignant tumours:**

| Feature | Benign | Malignant |
|---------|--------|----------|
| Growth rate | Slow | Rapid |
| Growth pattern | Expansile | Infiltrative/invasive |
| Differentiation | Well-differentiated; resembles parent tissue | Poorly differentiated; anaplastic |
| Capsule | Often encapsulated | No capsule |
| Mitoses | Rare, normal | Frequent, atypical |
| Metastasis | **Never** | **Yes** (hallmark of malignancy) |
| Necrosis | Rare | Common |
| Local invasion | No | Yes |
| Recurrence after excision | Rare | Common |
| Effect on host | Usually local; may compress/obstruct | Destructive; systemic effects (cachexia) |

**Routes of metastasis:**
1. **Lymphatic spread:** Most common for carcinomas; cancer cells enter lymphatics → regional lymph nodes → distant nodes (sentinel node concept).
2. **Haematogenous spread:** Common for sarcomas; via veins → liver (from GI via portal vein), lungs (from all sites via systemic veins), bone, brain.
3. **Direct (contiguous) spread:** Local invasion into adjacent structures.
4. **Transcoelomic spread:** Across body cavities (peritoneum → Krukenberg tumour; pleura); seeding of serosal surfaces.
5. **Perineural spread:** Along nerve sheaths (especially head/neck, pancreatic tumours).
6. **Implantation/iatrogenic:** Tumour cells seeded during surgical manipulation.

**2.** **Pathogenesis of emphysema:**

Emphysema = abnormal permanent enlargement of airspaces **distal to the terminal bronchioles** accompanied by destruction of their walls **without significant fibrosis**.

**Pathogenesis (protease-antiprotease hypothesis):**
1. Smoking → recruits neutrophils and macrophages to the lung → release **elastase** (neutrophil elastase, MMP-12 from macrophages).
2. Normally, **α₁-antitrypsin (AAT)** inhibits elastase.
3. Tobacco smoke → oxidises methionine in AAT → inactivates AAT → **imbalance** between proteases and antiproteases.
4. Unchecked elastase degrades elastin in alveolar walls → loss of elastic recoil → alveolar destruction → hyperinflation.

**AAT (α₁-antitrypsin) deficiency:** Inherited (PiZZ phenotype) → low plasma AAT → panacinar emphysema at a young age, predominantly in lower lobes.

**Types of emphysema:**
- **Centriacinar (centrilobular):** Central part of acinus (respiratory bronchioles) affected; upper lobe predominance; smoking-related.
- **Panacinar (panlobular):** Entire acinus affected; lower lobe predominance; AAT deficiency.
- **Distal acinar (paraseptal):** Peripheral part, subpleural; spontaneous pneumothorax in young adults.
- **Irregular (paracicatricial):** Adjacent to scars.

**3.** **Causes of cell injury:**
- **Hypoxia/ischaemia** (most common) — ↓ ATP → Na⁺-K⁺ pump fails → cellular swelling.
- **Infectious agents** — bacteria, viruses, parasites, fungi.
- **Physical agents** — trauma, heat, cold, radiation.
- **Chemical agents/drugs** — poisons, carbon tetrachloride, drugs.
- **Immunological reactions** — Type II/III hypersensitivity, autoimmunity.
- **Nutritional deficiency or excess.**
- **Genetic defects** — enzyme/protein dysfunction.
- **Ageing.**

**Necrosis vs. Apoptosis:**

| Feature | Necrosis | Apoptosis |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Type of death | Pathological | Physiological (and pathological) |
| Stimulus | External insult (ischaemia, toxins) | Programmed signals |
| Cell size | Swells | Shrinks |
| Nucleus | Karyolysis, karyorrhexis, pyknosis | Condensed, fragmented |
| Membrane | Disrupted | Intact (initially) |
| Inflammation | Yes (DAMPs released) | No |
| Organelles | Disrupted | Intact initially |
| Mechanism | Passive denaturation | Active, energy-dependent (caspases) |
| Outcome | Cell lysis | Apoptotic bodies (phagocytosed) |

**4. Short notes:**

**4a. Causes of pleural effusion**

**Transudates** (protein <30 g/L, LDH low; due to imbalance of hydrostatic/oncotic forces):
- Congestive cardiac failure (most common), hepatic cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome, hypoalbuminaemia, Meigs syndrome.

**Exudates** (protein >30 g/L, LDH high; due to inflammation/tumour):
- Pneumonia (parapneumonic effusion), TB (lymphocyte-predominant exudate), malignancy (pleural metastases), pulmonary embolism, autoimmune (SLE, RA), post-cardiac injury.

**Haemothorax:** Blood (trauma, aortic dissection). **Chylothorax:** Lymph (thoracic duct damage).

**4b. Pathologic calcification**

**Dystrophic calcification:** Ca²⁺ deposition in dead/necrotic tissue; serum calcium normal. Examples: TB caseation (Ghon focus), atherosclerotic plaques, calcified tumours, heart valves (endocarditis healed). Mechanism: Ca²⁺ binds to cell membrane phosphatidylserine of dead cells; pH rises locally; Ca²⁺ precipitates.

**Metastatic calcification:** Ca²⁺ deposits in normal tissue due to **hypercalcaemia**. Examples: Hyperparathyroidism, hypervitaminosis D, milk-alkali syndrome, widespread bone metastases. Sites: Gastric mucosa, kidneys, lungs, systemic arteries.

**4c. Bronchiectasis**

Permanent, abnormal dilatation of bronchi with destruction of the muscular and elastic components of bronchial walls.

**Causes:** Post-infectious (childhood pertussis, measles, TB), cystic fibrosis (most common in West), Kartagener syndrome (primary ciliary dyskinesia), bronchial obstruction (foreign body, tumour), immunodeficiency.

**Pathogenesis:** Infection → inflammation → bronchial wall destruction → dilatation → pooling of secretions → recurrent infection (vicious cycle).

**Morphology:** Cylndrical, saccular, or varicose dilatation, predominantly in lower lobes; mucus and pus pooling; chronic inflammation, scarring.

**Clinical:** Chronic productive cough with copious purulent sputum ("three-layered" — mucous, sero-mucous, and greenish bottom layer), haemoptysis, recurrent pneumonia. CT chest (HRCT) is the gold standard for diagnosis.

### Section C

**5.** **Anaemia:** Reduction in the oxygen-carrying capacity of blood, usually defined as haemoglobin below normal levels (Males <13 g/dL; Females <12 g/dL).

**Causes of iron deficiency anaemia (IDA):**
1. Chronic blood loss (most common in adults — GI: peptic ulcer, colon cancer, haemorrhoids; menorrhagia in women).
2. Increased demand (pregnancy, infancy, adolescence).
3. Decreased intake (poor diet).
4. Decreased absorption (coeliac disease, post-gastrectomy, H. pylori infection).

**Laboratory diagnosis of IDA:**
- **CBC:** Microcytic (low MCV) hypochromic (low MCH/MCHC) anaemia; reduced Hb; thrombocytosis common.
- **Peripheral smear:** Small pale RBCs, pencil cells (elongated), target cells, anisocytosis, poikilocytosis.
- **Iron studies:** Serum iron ↓, TIBC (total iron binding capacity) ↑, serum ferritin ↓ (most sensitive marker — reflects iron stores), transferrin saturation ↓ (<15%).
- **Bone marrow biopsy** (rarely needed): Absent iron stores (Prussian blue stain negative).
- **Reticulocyte count:** Low.
- **Serum soluble transferrin receptor (sTfR):** Elevated in IDA (distinguishes from anaemia of chronic disease).

**6.** **Risk factors for atherosclerosis:**
*Major (modifiable):* Hypertension, hyperlipidaemia (elevated LDL), smoking (most important modifiable risk factor), diabetes mellitus, obesity.
*Major (non-modifiable):* Age (men >45, women >55), male sex, family history.
*Other:* Physical inactivity, metabolic syndrome, homocysteinaemia, low HDL.

**Pathogenesis of atherosclerosis (response-to-injury hypothesis):**
1. **Endothelial injury/dysfunction** (by hypertension, oxidised LDL, smoking, hyperglycaemia) → ↑ permeability, adhesion molecule expression (ICAM-1, VCAM-1).
2. **Monocyte recruitment:** LDL enters intima → oxidised LDL (oxLDL) → monocytes attracted → differentiate into macrophages → phagocytose oxLDL via scavenger receptors → **foam cells** → **fatty streak** (earliest lesion, reversible).
3. **Smooth muscle cell (SMC) proliferation:** Platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), TGF-β → SMC migrate from media to intima → proliferate → form fibrous cap.
4. **Advanced/complicated plaque:** Core of lipid, necrotic debris, foam cells; covered by fibrous cap (SMCs + collagen). **Calcification, ulceration, haemorrhage, thrombosis** → acute coronary syndrome.

**7.** **Leukemia definition and classification:**

**Definition:** Malignant neoplasm of haematopoietic stem cells, characterised by proliferation of abnormal leucocytes (or their precursors) in the bone marrow and peripheral blood.

**Classification:**
- **By cell type:** Myeloid (AML, CML) or Lymphoid (ALL, CLL).
- **By course:** Acute (blast crisis, rapidly progressive) or Chronic (more mature cells, indolent).

**Etiological factors:**
1. **Radiation:** Ionising radiation (atomic bomb survivors, therapeutic radiation).
2. **Chemicals/drugs:** Benzene, alkylating agents (secondary leukaemia), topoisomerase II inhibitors.
3. **Viruses:** HTLV-1 (adult T-cell leukaemia/lymphoma), EBV.
4. **Genetic:** Down syndrome (21-trisomy → 20× risk of ALL), constitutional chromosomal abnormalities.
5. **Prior haematological disorders:** Myelodysplastic syndrome, aplastic anaemia → AML.
6. **Chromosomal translocations:** Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22) in CML; t(15;17) in APL; t(8;21), t(12;21) in AML/ALL.

**8. Short notes:**

**8a. Congestive cardiac failure (CCF)**
A clinical syndrome where the heart cannot maintain adequate output to meet metabolic demands or can do so only at elevated filling pressures.
- **Left heart failure:** ↓ CO → systemic hypoperfusion (fatigue, ↓renal perfusion → fluid retention); ↑ pulmonary venous pressure → pulmonary oedema (dyspnoea, orthopnoea, PND). Causes: IHD, hypertension, valvular disease, cardiomyopathy.
- **Right heart failure:** ↑ systemic venous pressure → JVP elevated, hepatomegaly (nutmeg liver — centrilobular congestion), ascites, peripheral pitting oedema. Often secondary to LHF or pulmonary hypertension.
- **Morphology:** Cardiac hypertrophy/dilatation; pulmonary congestion with haemosiderin-laden macrophages ("heart failure cells") in right heart failure from long-standing LHF; congested nutmeg liver (centrilobular necrosis in severe cases).

**8b. Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)**
Acquired disorder characterised by **widespread intravascular fibrin formation** and consumption of coagulation factors and platelets, often with secondary fibrinolysis.

**Causes:** Sepsis (Gram-negative endotoxin — most common), obstetric complications (placental abruption, amniotic fluid embolism, retained dead foetus), tissue trauma/burns, malignancy (acute promyelocytic leukaemia — t(15;17), adenocarcinomas), snake bites, transfusion reactions.

**Pathogenesis:** Triggering event → widespread **tissue factor** (TF) exposure → systemic thrombin generation → microvascular fibrin thrombi (organ ischaemia) + consumption of clotting factors and platelets (V, VIII, fibrinogen, platelets) → paradoxical **bleeding** (from depletion). Secondary fibrinolysis → FDPs/D-dimers ↑.

**Lab:** Prolonged PT, aPTT; low fibrinogen; low platelets; elevated D-dimers and FDPs; fragmented RBCs (schistocytes) on peripheral smear.

**8c. Aetiopathogenesis of infective endocarditis (Pathology perspective)**

Predisposing cardiac lesions (rheumatic, congenital) → endothelial damage → non-bacterial thrombotic endocarditis (NBTE) — sterile platelet-fibrin thrombus. Transient bacteraemia (dental, surgical, IV drug use) → bacteria seed NBTE → vegetation (fibrin + bacteria + inflammatory cells). Vegetations on:
- Mitral valve (most common), Aortic valve; RV valves in IVDU.
- **Acute IE** (*S. aureus*): Aggressive, destroys normal valves rapidly; large, friable vegetations.
- **Subacute IE** (*S. viridans*): Indolent course, usually abnormal valves; smaller vegetations.

Complications: Emboli (septic — stroke, renal infarcts), valve destruction (regurgitation), mycotic aneurysm, immune complex glomerulonephritis (Osler nodes, Janeway lesions, Roth spots), heart failure.

### Section B

**1.** **Thrombosis:** Formation of a solid mass of blood (thrombus) within the cardiovascular system **during life** from constituents of blood.

**Types of thrombus:**
1. **Red (coagulation) thrombus:** Predominantly RBCs + fibrin; forms in slow-flowing/static blood (veins, aneurysms). "Lines of Zahn" in arterial thrombi (alternating pale platelet/fibrin and dark RBC layers).
2. **White (platelet/mural) thrombus:** Predominantly platelets + fibrin; on injured arterial walls or heart chambers.
3. **Mixed thrombus:** Most common; head (white, attached), tail (red, propagated).
4. **Hyaline thrombus:** Microthrombi in capillaries (DIC).

**Fates of thrombus:**
1. **Resolution** (fibrinolysis, ideal outcome).
2. **Organisation and recanalisation:** Fibroblasts and new capillaries grow into thrombus → partial restoration of flow.
3. **Calcification** (phleboliths in veins).
4. **Propagation** (clot extends).
5. **Embolism** — most dangerous fate; thrombus fragments break off → embolus travels downstream.

**2.** **Mechanism of wound healing:**

**Primary intention (clean surgical wound):** Day 0–1: Haemostasis (clot formation); Days 1–3: Inflammation (neutrophils, then macrophages); Days 3–7: Proliferation — fibroblasts lay down Type III collagen, angiogenesis, myofibroblasts cause wound contraction; Week 2+: Remodelling — Type III collagen replaced by Type I, scar matures. Wound strength: 70% of normal by 6 weeks; 80% by 3 months; never reaches 100%.

**Growth factors:** PDGF, TGF-β (fibroblast activation, collagen synthesis), VEGF (angiogenesis), EGF, bFGF.

**Factors impairing healing:** Infection, poor blood supply (ischaemia), vitamin C deficiency (↓ prolyl/lysyl hydroxylase → ↓ collagen cross-linking — scurvy), zinc deficiency, diabetes mellitus (microangiopathy, impaired WBC function), steroids (↓ inflammation).

**3.** **Aetiopathogenesis of pulmonary tuberculosis:** (See Microbiology section, Sep 1, 2021 Q5.)

**4. Short notes:**

**4a. Chemical mediators of acute inflammation**

| Mediator | Source | Actions |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Histamine | Mast cells, basophils, platelets | Immediate vasodilation, ↑ vascular permeability |
| Serotonin | Platelets | Vasodilation, ↑ vascular permeability |
| Prostaglandins (PGE₂, PGI₂) | Arachidonic acid (COX pathway) | Vasodilation, pain, fever (↑ set point) |
| Leukotrienes (LTC4, LTD4, LTB4) | Arachidonic acid (LOX pathway) | Bronchoconstriction (LTC4/D4); chemotaxis (LTB4) |
| Platelet Activating Factor (PAF) | Mast cells, platelets, endothelium | Platelet aggregation, ↑ permeability, chemotaxis |
| Complement (C3a, C5a) | Complement cascade | Mast cell degranulation (C3a, C5a), chemotaxis (C5a), opsonisation (C3b) |
| Bradykinin | Plasma (kallikrein-kinin system) | Vasodilation, ↑ permeability, pain, oedema |
| IL-1, TNF-α | Macrophages | Fever (pyrogens), acute phase proteins, endothelial adhesion molecules |
| IL-8 | Macrophages, endothelium | Chemotaxis (neutrophils) |
| Nitric Oxide (NO) | Endothelium | Vasodilation, antimicrobial |

**4b. Pathogenesis of atopic asthma**

Type I (IgE-mediated) hypersensitivity reaction in the airways. Initial sensitisation: Allergen (dust mite, pollen) → APCs → Th2 differentiation → IL-4 drives B-cell class switching to **IgE** → IgE binds FcεRI on mast cells (sensitisation). Re-exposure: Allergen cross-links IgE-FcεRI → mast cell degranulation → **early phase reaction** (minutes): histamine, LTC4/D4, PGD2 → bronchoconstriction, oedema, mucus secretion. **Late phase reaction** (4–8 hours): IL-4, IL-5, IL-13 → eosinophil/neutrophil recruitment → airway inflammation, hyperresponsiveness. Chronic: Airway remodelling (goblet cell hyperplasia, subepithelial fibrosis, smooth muscle hypertrophy, ↑ basement membrane thickness).

**4c. Stages of lobar pneumonia**

Caused predominantly by *Streptococcus pneumoniae*; entire lobe affected uniformly:
1. **Congestion (Day 1–2):** Vascular engorgement, oedema fluid, few bacteria in alveoli; lobe heavy, red.
2. **Red hepatisation (Day 2–4):** Alveoli filled with RBCs + fibrin + bacteria + neutrophils; lobe airless, solid, liver-like ("hepatisation"), red colour.
3. **Grey hepatisation (Day 4–8):** RBCs lyse; alveoli contain fibrin + neutrophils + macrophages; lobe becomes grey/yellow; bacteria cleared.
4. **Resolution (Day 8–9 onwards):** Macrophages digest and clear fibrin and cellular debris; lung returns to normal architecture. Fibrinous exudate may organise (carnification) if resolution fails.

### Section C

**5.** **Aetiopathogenesis of megaloblastic anaemia:**

**Cause:** Deficiency of **vitamin B12** or **folic acid** (or both), causing impaired DNA synthesis → megaloblastic change.

**B12 deficiency causes:** Dietary (rare; strict vegans), pernicious anaemia (autoimmune destruction of gastric parietal cells → ↓ intrinsic factor → ↓ B12 absorption in terminal ileum), gastrectomy, terminal ileum disease/resection (Crohn's), parasites (*Diphyllobothrium latum*), blind loop syndrome.

**Folate deficiency causes:** Poor diet (alcoholics, elderly), ↑ demand (pregnancy), malabsorption (coeliac disease), drugs (methotrexate — DHFR inhibitor; phenytoin; sulphasalazine).

**Pathogenesis:** B12/folate needed for conversion of dUMP → dTMP (thymidylate) → DNA synthesis. Deficiency → impaired DNA replication → cells enlarge (RNA/protein synthesis intact) but cannot divide → **megaloblastic** change. **B12 also required for myelin synthesis** (methionine synthesis via methylmalonyl-CoA pathway) → subacute combined degeneration (B12 deficiency only — posterior column + lateral corticospinal tract demyelination).

**Lab:** Macrocytic anaemia (↑ MCV); peripheral smear — macro-ovalocytes, **hypersegmented neutrophils** (≥5 lobes — pathognomonic); bone marrow — megaloblasts (giant, early nuclear maturation, nuclear-cytoplasmic asynchrony).

**6.** **CML — clinical presentation and laboratory diagnosis:**

CML (Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22) → BCR-ABL fusion gene → constitutively active tyrosine kinase → uncontrolled myeloid proliferation).

**Clinical:** Insidious onset; fatigue, weight loss, sweats; **massive splenomegaly** (main feature); hepatomegaly. Blast crisis (transformation to acute leukaemia).

**Lab:** Leukocytosis (WBC 50,000–500,000/μL) with the entire myeloid spectrum (blasts, promyelocytes, myelocytes, neutrophils, basophilia, eosinophilia); low/absent **Leukocyte Alkaline Phosphatase (LAP) score** (vs. leukemoid reaction where LAP is high); normocytic normochromic anaemia; thrombocytosis initially. Bone marrow hypercellular. Cytogenetics: Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22) in >95%. Molecular: BCR-ABL by RT-PCR or FISH. Treatment: **Imatinib** (first tyrosine kinase inhibitor — targeted therapy).

**7.** **Aetiopathogenesis of myocardial infarction (MI):**

**Aetiology:** Most commonly from **atherosclerotic plaque rupture** → thrombosis → sudden occlusion of coronary artery. Risk factors (same as atherosclerosis). Rarely: coronary artery spasm, embolism.

**Pathogenesis:** Vulnerable plaque (thin fibrous cap, large lipid core, inflammation) → rupture → exposure of subendothelial collagen and TF → platelet activation/aggregation → fibrin clot → **complete occlusion** → transmural ischaemia → 20–30 min: irreversible cell injury → **coagulative necrosis** (within 6h, not visible grossly; 12–24h: yellow-brown pallor; 3–5 days: maximal softening, yellow with hyperaemic border; 3 weeks: granulation tissue; 7 weeks: fibrous scar).

**Complications:** Arrhythmias (most common early cause of death), pump failure/cardiogenic shock, ventricular wall rupture (3–5 days; cardiac tamponade), papillary muscle rupture (acute MR), ventricular aneurysm (late), mural thrombus (→ embolism), Dressler syndrome (autoimmune pericarditis weeks later).

**8. Short notes:**

**8a. Leukopenia**
Decreased total WBC count (<4,000/μL). Causes: Agranulocytosis (↓ neutrophils) — drugs (chemotherapy, clozapine, propylthiouracil), aplastic anaemia, megaloblastic anaemia; viral infections (HIV, EBV, CMV); SLE; overwhelming bacterial infection (late). Lymphopenia: HIV/AIDS, malnutrition, radiation, steroids, SLE. **Neutropenia** (<1,500/μL) → increased risk of bacterial/fungal infection; <500/μL → severe risk.

**8b. Complications of hypertension**
- **Heart:** Left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) → HF with preserved EF; accelerates coronary artery disease → MI; atrial fibrillation.
- **Brain:** Hypertensive encephalopathy (raised ICP), stroke (haemorrhagic → Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysm rupture; ischaemic → atherosclerosis), lacunar infarcts.
- **Kidneys:** Hypertensive nephrosclerosis (afferent arteriolar thickening → ischaemic nephropathy → CKD).
- **Aorta:** Aortic dissection (medial degeneration + hypertension); aortic aneurysm.
- **Eyes:** Hypertensive retinopathy (arteriovenous nipping, flame haemorrhages, exudates, papilloedema in severe cases).
- **Peripheral arteries:** Accelerated atherosclerosis → PAD.

**8c. Clinical criteria for diagnosis of rheumatic heart disease (Jones Criteria, revised 2015):**

Diagnosis: **2 major** or **1 major + 2 minor** criteria + evidence of preceding GAS infection (positive throat culture, rapid strep test, or rising streptococcal antibody titer — ASO, anti-DNase B).

**Major criteria:** Carditis (clinical or subclinical/echo), Arthritis (migratory polyarthritis), Chorea (Sydenham's), Erythema marginatum, Subcutaneous nodules.

**Minor criteria:** Fever (≥38.5°C), Elevated ESR (≥60 mm/hr) or CRP (≥3 mg/dL), Prolonged PR interval on ECG.

**4. Short notes:**

**4a. Differences between necrosis and apoptosis:** (See Sep 3, 2021 Q3 above.)

**4b. Vascular events in acute inflammation**

Following tissue injury:
1. **Transient vasoconstriction** (seconds) → reflex of smooth muscle.
2. **Arteriolar vasodilation** (→ ↑ blood flow → heat and redness — calor and rubor).
3. **↑ vascular permeability** (mediators: histamine, serotonin, bradykinin, leukotrienes, PAF act on venular endothelium) → protein-rich exudate into interstitium (→ oedema/tumour; ↓ plasma oncotic pressure; ↑ ESR).
4. **Slowing of blood flow (stasis):** ↑ viscosity from exudate loss + packing of RBCs → **margination** of WBCs (axial shift → rolling on endothelium).
5. **Leukocyte adhesion and emigration:** Rolling (selectins: E-selectin, P-selectin bind PSGL-1/sialyl-Lewis X on neutrophils) → Firm adhesion (integrins: β₂-integrins bind ICAM-1/VCAM-1 on endothelium) → Diapedesis (transmigration between endothelial cells) → Chemotaxis (C5a, IL-8, LTB4, fMLP).

**4c. Primary tuberculosis**

Primary TB (Ghon complex) occurs in previously uninfected individuals:
1. Inhaled *M. tuberculosis* → alveoli → macrophages → initial focus in the **mid-zone of the lung** (Ghon focus — subpleural lesion).
2. Bacilli drain to hilar/mediastinal lymph nodes → lymph node enlargement.
3. Ghon focus + enlarged lymph node = **Ghon complex (primary complex)**.
4. Usually self-limiting (heals, calcifies) in immunocompetent.
5. Minority progress to progressive primary TB (young children, immunocompromised).
6. Haematogenous spread → miliary TB, meningitis, bone/joint TB, renal TB.

### Section C

**5.** **Aetiopathogenesis of rheumatic heart disease:** (See Sep 1, 2021 Q5 in Microbiology, and here:)

**Pathogenesis (molecular mimicry):** Group A Strep (GAS) pharyngeal infection → M protein (especially M1, M3) → immune response → Anti-M antibodies also recognise cardiac proteins (myosin heavy chain, cardiac alpha-myosin, laminin — Group A Strep antigen shares antigenic similarity/molecular mimicry) → autoimmune damage to heart. **Aschoff body:** Pathognomonic lesion — granulomatous lesion in myocardium with central fibrinoid necrosis surrounded by **Anitschkow cells** (caterpillar cells — macrophages with characteristic linear nuclear chromatin) and Aschoff giant cells.

**Morphological features:** Pancarditis — Pericarditis (fibrinous "bread-and-butter" pericarditis), Myocarditis (Aschoff bodies), Endocarditis (verrucous vegetation on valve leaflet margins, especially mitral → long-term → valve thickening, fusion of commissures → stenosis/regurgitation).

**6.** IDA definition and lab diagnosis: (See Sep 3, 2021 Q5 above.)

**7.** Classification and peripheral blood smear of IDA:

**Aetiological classification:** Haemorrhagic, deficiency (iron, B12, folate), haemolytic (inherited/acquired), aplastic/hypoplastic, chronic disease.

**Morphological classification:**
- Microcytic hypochromic (MCV <80 fL): IDA, thalassaemia, sideroblastic anaemia, ACD.
- Normocytic normochromic (MCV 80–100 fL): Acute blood loss, ACD, haemolytic anaemia, aplastic anaemia.
- Macrocytic (MCV >100 fL): B12/folate deficiency, liver disease, hypothyroidism, drugs (hydroxyurea, azathioprine).

**PBS in IDA:** Microcytic (small RBCs), hypochromic (pale centre >1/3 of diameter), pencil/cigar cells, target cells, anisocytosis, poikilocytosis. Reticulocytes low.

**8. Short notes:**

**8a. Haemophilia**
- **Haemophilia A:** X-linked recessive; deficiency of **Factor VIII**. Most common severe congenital coagulopathy (1:5,000 males).
- **Haemophilia B (Christmas disease):** X-linked recessive; deficiency of **Factor IX**; clinically identical but less common.
- Clinical: Deep tissue bleeds (haematomas, haemarthroses — joint deformities), prolonged bleeding after surgery/trauma; spontaneous bleed in severe (<1% factor).
- Lab: Prolonged **aPTT** (intrinsic pathway defect); normal PT, platelet count, bleeding time. Specific factor assay confirms.
- Treatment: Factor VIII/IX concentrate (recombinant); desmopressin (DDAVP) for mild haemophilia A (↑endothelial vWF/VIII release).

**8b. Hypertension**
Persistent elevation of systolic BP ≥140 mmHg and/or diastolic BP ≥90 mmHg (JNC 8 / WHO criteria). **Primary (essential) hypertension** (~95%): Multifactorial — genetic, increased salt intake, renin-angiotensin-aldosterone overactivity, autonomic dysfunction, obesity, sedentary lifestyle. **Secondary hypertension** (~5%): Renal disease (most common — renal artery stenosis, CKD), endocrine (primary hyperaldosteronism, Cushing, phaeochromocytoma), coarctation of aorta. Complications: (See Mar 6, 2020 Q8b.)

**8c. Fallot's tetralogy:**
(See Anatomy section — Oct 17, 2016 old course Q10d above. Pathology perspective: Cyanotic CHD; pathogenesis — right-to-left shunt → deoxygenated blood bypasses pulmonary circulation → cyanosis. RVH is a compensatory response to high RV afterload from outflow obstruction. Histologically, pulmonary arterial changes of pulmonary hypertension if survival prolonged without correction.)

## January 11, 2019 (December 2018/January 2019 Session, 50 Marks)

### Section B

**1.** Thrombus definition and aetiopathogenesis:

**Virchow's Triad** (three factors predisposing to thrombosis):
1. **Endothelial injury/dysfunction** — atherosclerosis, hypertension, vasculitis → exposes collagen (platelet adhesion) and TF (coagulation cascade activation).
2. **Abnormal (turbulent) blood flow** — turbulence (atherosclerosis, valvular disease) or stasis (AF, DVT, varicose veins, prolonged immobilisation) → platelet-endothelium interaction, ↓ natural anticoagulant washout.
3. **Hypercoagulability** — inherited (Factor V Leiden, prothrombin gene mutation, protein C/S deficiency, antithrombin deficiency) or acquired (pregnancy, OCP, malignancy, nephrotic syndrome, DIC).

**Morphology:** Lines of Zahn (alternating pale platelet/fibrin layers and darker RBC layers in arterial thrombi/cardiac thrombi). Venous thrombi: red thrombi (RBC dominant).

**Fate of thrombus:** (See Mar 6, 2020 Q1 above.)

**2.** **Classification of lung tumours:**

**Primary malignant:**
- Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC, ~85%): Adenocarcinoma (most common overall, peripheral, TTF-1+), Squamous cell carcinoma (central, hilar, keratin pearls), Large cell carcinoma.
- Small cell lung cancer (SCLC, ~15%): Central, aggressive, strongly associated with smoking, neuroendocrine origin.

**Primary benign:** Hamartoma (most common benign pulmonary tumour — cartilage/fat/muscle/epithelium "popcorn" pattern on CT).

**Metastatic (secondary):** Most common lung tumours; from breast, colon, kidney, prostate, sarcomas.

**Morphology of squamous cell carcinoma:** Arises from bronchial epithelium (metaplasia → dysplasia → carcinoma in situ → invasive). Gross: Hilar, large central mass, may cavitate. Microscopy: Sheets of squamous cells with **keratin pearls** (concentric whorls of keratinised cells — pathognomonic), individual cell keratinisation, intercellular bridges (desmosomes), SCC pattern.

**4. Short notes:**

**4a. Wound healing** (See Mar 6, 2020 Q2 above.)

**4b. Granulomatous disease**

Granuloma: Organised aggregate of **epithelioid macrophages** (activated macrophages with abundant pink cytoplasm) + lymphocytes ± Langerhans-type giant cells (peripheral nuclei — horseshoe/ring arrangement) ± central necrosis (caseating — TB; non-caseating — sarcoidosis).

Causes of granulomas (BLIND categories): **B**erylliosis, **L**eprosy, **I**nflammatory (sarcoidosis, Crohn's disease), **N**ecrotising (TB, histoplasmosis), **D**ermatological (foreign body reaction).

**Caseating granuloma (TB):** Central caseous necrosis (cheese-like, acellular eosinophilic material — coagulative necrosis without structural preservation) + epithelioid macrophages + Langerhans giant cells + lymphocytic rim.

**Non-caseating granuloma (sarcoidosis):** Epithelioid macrophages + Langerhans giant cells **without central necrosis**; asteroid bodies/Schaumann bodies may be present.

**4c. Necrosis:** (See Sep 3, 2021 Q3 — necrosis column in the table; plus types:)

Types of necrosis:
- **Coagulative:** Most common; cell architecture preserved (ghost outlines); ischaemia of heart, kidney, spleen.
- **Liquefactive:** Cell architecture destroyed; liquefied debris; brain infarcts, abscesses.
- **Caseous:** Cheese-like; TB and other fungal infections; characteristic of granulomas.
- **Fat necrosis:** Saponification; acute pancreatitis (lipase release), traumatic fat necrosis (breast).
- **Fibrinoid:** Immune complexes in vessel walls; malignant hypertension, polyarteritis nodosa.
- **Gangrenous:** Dry (ischaemia, coagulative → mummification), Wet (superinfection → liquefaction).

**8a. Hodgkin lymphoma (HL)**
Malignant lymphoma with characteristic **Reed-Sternberg (RS) cells** (large cells with bilobed/"owl-eye" nuclei and prominent nucleoli; CD15+, CD30+, usually CD45−, EBV+). WHO classifies HL into:
- **Classical HL** (95%): Nodular sclerosis (most common, young women, mediastinum), Mixed cellularity (EBV-assoc., older patients), Lymphocyte-rich, Lymphocyte-depleted.
- **Nodular lymphocyte predominant HL** (5%): Popcorn cells (CD20+, CD45+).

Clinical: Painless cervical/supraclavicular lymphadenopathy; B symptoms (fever, night sweats, weight loss >10% over 6 months); alcohol-induced pain in nodes. Staging: Ann Arbor/Lugano. Treatment: ABVD chemotherapy ± radiation.

**8b. Immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP)**
Autoimmune destruction of platelets: Anti-platelet IgG antibodies (anti-GPIIb/IIIa) coat platelets → phagocytosis by splenic macrophages → thrombocytopenia (<150,000/μL). Cause: Autoimmune (primary, no cause); secondary: SLE, CLL, post-viral (children), drug-induced.

Clinical: Petechiae, purpura, ecchymoses, mucosal bleeding; no splenomegaly (distinguishes from hypersplenism). Lab: ↓ platelet count; normal PT, aPTT; peripheral smear shows large platelets (young, released early); bone marrow — megakaryocytes increased (normal or ↑). Treatment: Prednisolone → IVIG → splenectomy → TPO agonists (eltrombopag, romiplostim).

**4a. Septic shock**

Distant effects of a tumour not due to direct invasion or metastasis but to: (i) **Hormone/peptide secretion** by tumour cells — SIADH (SCLC), PTHrP (squamous cell Ca → hypercalcaemia), ectopic ACTH (SCLC → Cushing's). (iii) Clubbing/hypertrophic osteoarthropathy (lung tumours). (iv) Venous thrombosis (Trousseau sign — migrating thrombophlebitis in mucin-secreting adenocarcinomas). **Definition and cardinal signs of inflammation:**
**Inflammation:** Vascular and cellular response to tissue injury designed to eliminate the cause and repair damaged tissue. **Cardinal signs** (Celsus + Virchow): Rubor (redness — vasodilation), Calor (heat — ↑ blood flow), Tumour (swelling — oedema from exudate), Dolor (pain — bradykinin, PGE₂, serotonin), Functio laesa (loss of function — Virchow's addition).

**Shock:** Circulatory failure with inadequate tissue perfusion → organ dysfunction.

**Classification:**
| Type | Mechanism | Examples |
|------|----------|---------|
| Hypovolaemic | ↓ blood/fluid volume | Haemorrhage, severe dehydration, burns |
| Cardiogenic | ↓ cardiac output | MI, cardiac tamponade, massive PE |
| Distributive | Vasodilatation/maldistribution | Septic, anaphylactic, neurogenic |
| Obstructive | Mechanical obstruction to flow | PE, cardiac tamponade, tension pneumothorax |

**Miliary tuberculosis:** Haematogenous dissemination of *M. tuberculosis* producing many small (<2 mm) granulomas (millet seed-like) throughout the lungs and other organs (liver, spleen, bone marrow, meninges). Occurs in primary progressive TB or reactivation in immunocompromised. CXR: Bilateral diffuse fine nodular shadowing. Lab: Sputum often negative; bone marrow biopsy or bronchoscopy for diagnosis; IGRA/tuberculin may be negative (immunosuppression). Mortality high without treatment.

**Sickle cell anaemia:** (See Microbiology — Biochemistry reference.) RBCs sickle under low O₂ → vaso-occlusion → organ infarcts (painful crises, AVN, splenic sequestration, dactylitis, stroke, acute chest syndrome) + haemolysis → anaemia. Peripheral smear: Sickle cells, target cells, Howell-Jolly bodies (functional asplenia). Lab: Hb electrophoresis → HbS band. Diagnosis: Newborn screen (HPLC).

**Leukocytosis:** WBC >11,000/μL. Neutrophilia (bacterial infection, tissue necrosis, drugs), Lymphocytosis (viral infection, CLL, pertussis), Eosinophilia (allergies, parasites, drugs), Basophilia (rare; CML, mast cell disorders), Monocytosis (chronic infections, TB, monocytic leukaemia).

**Complications of chronic RHD:** Mitral stenosis (most common valve lesion in RHD) → Left atrial dilatation → atrial fibrillation → left atrial thrombus → systemic emboli (stroke). Pulmonary hypertension → right heart failure. Repeated attacks → progressive valve destruction → combined stenosis and regurgitation. Infective endocarditis on damaged valves. Arrhythmias.
