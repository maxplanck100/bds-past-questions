## October/November 2021

**1.** Non-heme iron (Fe³⁺) is reduced to Fe²⁺ by a ferric reductase (duodenal cytochrome b) aided by dietary vitamin C, then transported into the enterocyte by DMT1. Inside the cell it is either stored as ferritin or exported into plasma via ferroportin, re-oxidised to Fe³⁺ by hephaestin, and bound to transferrin for transport. **Hepcidin** (liver-derived peptide hormone) regulates this by binding ferroportin and triggering its internalisation and degradation — reducing iron release from enterocytes/macrophages when iron stores are high or during inflammation, and falling when iron is needed.

**2.** DNA is a right-handed double helix of two antiparallel strands with a sugar-phosphate backbone on the outside and nitrogenous bases paired on the inside (A=T via 2 H-bonds, G≡C via 3 H-bonds). Salient features: ~10 base pairs per turn, major and minor grooves, complementary base pairing allows semi-conservative replication, uniform diameter (2 nm) maintained by purine-pyrimidine pairing.

**3.** Irreversible (regulated) steps of glycolysis: (i) Glucose → Glucose-6-P (hexokinase), (ii) Fructose-6-P → Fructose-1,6-bisP (PFK-1, main control point), (iii) Phosphoenolpyruvate → Pyruvate (pyruvate kinase). **Rapoport-Luebering cycle**: RBC-specific shunt diverting 1,3-BPG to 2,3-BPG (via bisphosphoglycerate mutase) instead of continuing glycolysis, bypassing one ATP. Significance: 2,3-BPG binds deoxyhemoglobin, lowering O₂ affinity and shifting the oxygen dissociation curve right, promoting O₂ unloading to tissues.

4a. **Glycosaminoglycans (GAGs)** — long, unbranched heteropolysaccharide chains of repeating disaccharide units (amino sugar + uronic acid), heavily sulfated/carboxylated giving strong negative charge and water retention. Examples: hyaluronic acid (synovial fluid), chondroitin sulfate (cartilage), heparin (anticoagulant).

4b. **Glycolytic products in RBCs** — RBCs lack mitochondria and rely entirely on anaerobic glycolysis: glucose → 2 pyruvate → 2 lactate (via LDH, regenerating NAD⁺), net 2 ATP; a fraction is diverted through the Rapoport-Luebering shunt to form 2,3-BPG.

4c. **Sickle-cell haemoglobin** — point mutation in the β-globin gene (GAG→GTG) substitutes valine for glutamate at position 6, producing HbS, which polymerises when deoxygenated, deforming RBCs into a sickle shape. **Lab diagnosis**: peripheral smear (sickle-shaped cells), sickling test (sodium metabisulfite), haemoglobin electrophoresis/HPLC showing an HbS band.

**5.** TCA cycle: Acetyl-CoA + oxaloacetate → citrate (citrate synthase) → isocitrate → α-ketoglutarate (+NADH, CO₂) → succinyl-CoA (+NADH, CO₂) → succinate (+GTP) → fumarate (+FADH₂) → malate → oxaloacetate (+NADH). Net per turn: 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 GTP, 2 CO₂. **Amphibolic** because it is both catabolic (oxidises acetyl-CoA for ATP) and anabolic (intermediates feed gluconeogenesis via OAA, heme synthesis via succinyl-CoA, amino acid and fatty acid synthesis via α-KG/citrate).

**6.** ETC components: Complex I (NADH dehydrogenase) → Coenzyme Q → Complex II (succinate dehydrogenase, feeds electrons from FADH₂) → Complex III (cytochrome bc₁) → cytochrome c → Complex IV (cytochrome oxidase, reduces O₂ to H₂O). **Chemiosmotic theory**: Complexes I, III, IV pump H⁺ from matrix to intermembrane space building a proton-motive force; protons re-enter the matrix through ATP synthase (Complex V), driving ATP synthesis from ADP + Pi.

**7. β-oxidation**: fatty acid → fatty acyl-CoA (acyl-CoA synthetase) → shuttled into mitochondria via carnitine (CPT-I/CPT-II) → repeated cycles of oxidation (FADH₂), hydration, oxidation (NADH), thiolytic cleavage releasing acetyl-CoA. Palmitic acid (C16) undergoes 7 cycles yielding 8 acetyl-CoA, 7 FADH₂, 7 NADH. **Fate of acetyl-CoA**: enters TCA cycle for ATP generation, or diverted to ketogenesis in liver when in excess.

8a. **Fat-soluble vitamins**: A, D, E, K — absorbed with dietary fat via micelles, transported in chylomicrons, stored in liver/adipose tissue.

8b. **Regulation of cholesterol biosynthesis** — rate-limiting enzyme HMG-CoA reductase is regulated by: feedback inhibition by cholesterol, insulin/glucagon (dephosphorylation activates, phosphorylation inhibits), and transcriptional control via SREBP (low cellular cholesterol activates SREBP, upregulating the enzyme); statins competitively inhibit this enzyme.

8c. **Free oxygen radicals** — reactive species (superoxide O₂•⁻, hydroxyl OH•, H₂O₂) generated from ETC leakage, phagocyte respiratory burst, radiation; cause lipid peroxidation, protein/DNA damage; neutralised by SOD, catalase, glutathione peroxidase, and vitamins C/E.

## August/September 2021

**1.** Functional classification of proteins: structural (collagen), enzymatic (amylase), transport (haemoglobin), defensive (immunoglobulins), hormonal (insulin), contractile (actin/myosin). **Secondary structure**: local regular folding — α-helix (intra-chain H-bonds between C=O of residue *n* and N-H of *n+4*) and β-pleated sheet (inter-strand H-bonds), stabilised by hydrogen bonding of the peptide backbone.

**2.** **Enzyme** — a protein biocatalyst lowering activation energy. **Coenzyme** — small organic cofactor (often vitamin-derived, e.g. NAD⁺, FAD, TPP) required for catalysis. **Isoenzyme** — different molecular forms of an enzyme catalysing the same reaction but differing in kinetic properties/tissue distribution (e.g., LDH1–5). Factors affecting activity: substrate concentration, enzyme concentration, temperature, pH, presence of activators/inhibitors, cofactors.

**3.** **Neurotransmitter** — a chemical messenger released by a neuron to signal a target cell. Criteria: synthesised in the neuron, stored in vesicles, released on Ca²⁺-dependent depolarisation, acts on a specific postsynaptic receptor, has a defined inactivation mechanism, exogenous application mimics the endogenous effect. Classification: cholinergic (acetylcholine), monoamines (dopamine, noradrenaline, adrenaline, serotonin), amino acids (glutamate, GABA, glycine), neuropeptides.

4a. Glycolysis reactions with rate-limiting enzymes: hexokinase (step 1), phosphofructokinase-1 (step 3, main regulatory point, inhibited by ATP/citrate, activated by AMP/F2,6BP), pyruvate kinase (step 10, inhibited by ATP/alanine).

4b. **Thalassemia** — reduced or absent synthesis of α- or β-globin chains due to gene deletions/mutations, causing imbalanced chain production, ineffective erythropoiesis, and microcytic hypochromic haemolytic anaemia.

4c. **Myasthenia gravis** — autoimmune disease where antibodies destroy nicotinic acetylcholine receptors at the neuromuscular junction, impairing neuromuscular transmission and causing fatigable muscle weakness.

**5.** (See TCA cycle steps, Oct/Nov 2021 Q5.) Amphibolic role: catabolic oxidation of acetyl-CoA plus anabolic supply of intermediates for gluconeogenesis, heme synthesis, and amino acid/fatty acid synthesis.

**6.** Ketone body synthesis is increased in: prolonged starvation, uncontrolled/poorly controlled diabetes mellitus (diabetic ketoacidosis), high-fat/very-low-carbohydrate diets, and chronic alcoholism — all conditions of high fatty acid mobilisation and limited oxaloacetate availability for TCA entry of acetyl-CoA.

**7.** (See Oct/Nov 2021 Q6 for ETC/chemiosmotic theory.)

8a. **HDL metabolism** — synthesised as nascent discoid particles by liver/intestine; picks up free cholesterol from peripheral tissues; LCAT esterifies this cholesterol; cholesteryl esters are transferred to VLDL/LDL via CETP or delivered directly to the liver via SR-B1 receptors (reverse cholesterol transport).

8b. **Regulation of fatty acid synthesis** — rate-limiting enzyme acetyl-CoA carboxylase, activated by citrate and insulin (dephosphorylation), inhibited by palmitoyl-CoA (end-product) and glucagon/epinephrine (phosphorylation via AMPK).

8c. **Eicosanoids** (from arachidonic acid): prostaglandins (inflammation, pain, gastric protection), thromboxane A₂ (platelet aggregation, vasoconstriction), prostacyclin (vasodilation, inhibits platelet aggregation), leukotrienes (bronchoconstriction, chemotaxis in inflammation).

## March/April 2021

**1.** Carbohydrates — polyhydroxy aldehydes/ketones; classified as mono-, di-, oligo-, polysaccharides; function: energy source, structural component. Lipids — hydrophobic biomolecules (fatty acids, triglycerides, phospholipids, steroids); function: energy storage, membrane structure, signalling. Amino acids — building blocks of proteins containing amino and carboxyl groups; function: protein synthesis, neurotransmitter precursors, nitrogen balance.

**2.** Competitive inhibitor resembles the substrate and binds reversibly at the active site — raises apparent Km, Vmax unchanged, overcome by excess substrate. Non-competitive inhibitor binds elsewhere on the enzyme — lowers Vmax, Km unchanged. Therapeutic uses of competitive inhibition: methotrexate (inhibits dihydrofolate reductase, used in cancer chemotherapy), statins (HMG-CoA reductase inhibition for lowering cholesterol).

**3.** **Neurotransmitters** defined as in Q3, Aug/Sep 2021. Acetylcholine synthesis: choline + acetyl-CoA → acetylcholine (choline acetyltransferase). Degradation: acetylcholine → choline + acetate (acetylcholinesterase at the synaptic cleft).

4a. Regulation of heme synthesis — ALA synthase (rate-limiting enzyme) is feedback-inhibited by heme (negative feedback on both its activity and gene transcription); heme also represses transport of ALA synthase into mitochondria.

4b. **Homopolysaccharides** — polysaccharides made of a single repeating monosaccharide unit, e.g., starch and glycogen (glucose units, storage), cellulose (glucose, structural in plants).

4c. See Oct/Nov 2021, Q4b (glycolysis in RBC).

**5.** Ketone body pathway: 2 Acetyl-CoA → Acetoacetyl-CoA → HMG-CoA (HMG-CoA synthase) → Acetoacetate (HMG-CoA lyase) → reduced to β-hydroxybutyrate or spontaneously decarboxylated to acetone. Utilisation in extrahepatic tissue: acetoacetate reactivated to acetoacetyl-CoA via thiophorase (absent in liver — this is why liver cannot use the ketones it makes), then thiolysed to 2 acetyl-CoA entering TCA cycle.

**6–7.** Oxidative phosphorylation and chemiosmotic theory — as in Oct/Nov 2021 Q6. Biological oxidation broadly refers to all oxidation-reduction reactions in metabolism that release energy captured as ATP; chemiosmotic theory explains how the proton gradient generated by these reactions is converted to ATP via ATP synthase.

8a. Antioxidant systems: enzymatic — superoxide dismutase (O₂•⁻ → H₂O₂), catalase (H₂O₂ → H₂O + O₂), glutathione peroxidase; non-enzymatic — vitamin E, vitamin C, glutathione, β-carotene, uric acid.

8b. **Chylomicrons** — largest, least dense lipoproteins, formed in intestinal mucosa carrying dietary triglycerides (ApoB-48); ApoC-II activates lipoprotein lipase in capillaries, releasing FFA to tissues; remnants cleared by the liver via ApoE receptors.

8c. **Cardiac markers** — Troponin I/T (most specific, rises 3–6h post-MI), CK-MB (rises 4–6h, useful for reinfarction), myoglobin (earliest, non-specific), LDH (rises late, historical marker).

## February/March 2020

**1.** Carbohydrate classification: monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, galactose), disaccharides (sucrose, lactose, maltose), oligosaccharides, polysaccharides (homo- and heteropolysaccharides).

**2.** Protein defined as a polymer of amino acids linked by peptide bonds. Structural organisation: primary (amino acid sequence) → secondary (α-helix, β-sheet via backbone H-bonds) → tertiary (3D folding via hydrophobic/ionic/H-bond/disulfide interactions) → quaternary (assembly of multiple subunits, e.g., haemoglobin).

**3.** Enzyme — protein catalyst lowering activation energy of a specific reaction. Enzyme inhibition: reversible (competitive, non-competitive, uncompetitive) and irreversible (covalent modification of active site, e.g., organophosphates on acetylcholinesterase).

4a. **Porphyria** — group of disorders from enzyme deficiencies in the heme biosynthetic pathway, causing accumulation/excretion of porphyrin precursors; classified as hepatic (e.g., acute intermittent porphyria) or erythropoietic (e.g., congenital erythropoietic porphyria).

4b. **Essential fatty acids** — polyunsaturated fatty acids the body cannot synthesise (linoleic acid, α-linolenic acid), must be obtained from diet; precursors of arachidonic acid and eicosanoids.

4c. See Feb/Mar 2020 Q2 for hierarchy of protein structure.

**5.** **Apo-lipoproteins** — protein components of lipoproteins that solubilise lipids, act as enzyme cofactors (ApoC-II activates LPL), and serve as receptor ligands (ApoB-100, ApoE). Lipoprotein classes: chylomicrons, VLDL, IDL, LDL, HDL — differing in density/lipid-protein ratio. Chylomicron cycle: formed in intestine → LPL-mediated triglyceride hydrolysis in capillaries → chylomicron remnants cleared by hepatic ApoE receptors.

**6.** Ketone bodies — acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate, acetone; formed as in Mar/Apr 2021 Q5 from excess hepatic acetyl-CoA when fatty acid oxidation exceeds TCA cycle capacity (e.g., low oxaloacetate during fasting).

**7.** Citric acid cycle — diagrammatic representation as in Oct/Nov 2021 Q5 (8 sequential reactions from acetyl-CoA + OAA back to OAA).

8a. Antioxidants and role in biological systems — as in Mar/Apr 2021 Q8a; protect cells from oxidative damage implicated in ageing, atherosclerosis, cancer, and inflammatory disease.

8b. Cardiac markers and role in MI diagnosis — as in Mar/Apr 2021 Q8c; troponin preferred for its high specificity and long diagnostic window.

8c. Chemiosmotic theory of oxidative phosphorylation — as in Oct/Nov 2021 Q6.

## 2015

1a. Competitive inhibition mechanism — reversible binding of an inhibitor resembling substrate at the active site, raising apparent Km without changing Vmax; overcome by increasing substrate concentration.

1b. **Isozymes** — structurally distinct forms of an enzyme catalysing the same reaction, differing in tissue distribution and kinetic properties; example: LDH has 5 isoenzymes (LDH1–LDH5) with different subunit (H/M) compositions distinguishable by electrophoresis.

**2.** Sickle cell genetic defect and sickling mechanism — as in Oct/Nov 2021 Q4c.

**3.** Vitamin B12 biochemical function — cofactor for methionine synthase (homocysteine → methionine) and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase; deficiency impairs DNA synthesis in rapidly dividing cells (bone marrow), causing megaloblastic/pernicious anaemia.

4a. **Scurvy** — Vitamin C deficiency; vitamin C is required for prolyl/lysyl hydroxylase activity in collagen synthesis; deficiency causes defective collagen, leading to bleeding gums, poor wound healing, perifollicular haemorrhages.

4b. Organophosphorus poisoning — as in Dec 2017/Jan 2018 Q4a.

4c. Phospholipids — as in Jul/Aug 2018 Q4b.

**5.** Chemiosmotic theory and uncouplers — as in Oct/Nov 2021 Q6 and Jul/Aug 2018 Q8a.

**6.** Creatine phosphate in the heart — acts as an immediate energy buffer, regenerating ATP via creatine kinase (creatine phosphate + ADP → creatine + ATP) during high demand. Calcium-induced calcium release — depolarisation opens L-type Ca²⁺ channels in T-tubules; the resulting small Ca²⁺ influx triggers larger Ca²⁺ release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum via ryanodine receptors. Role of calcium in contraction: Ca²⁺ binds troponin C, shifting tropomyosin to expose the myosin-binding site on actin, enabling cross-bridge cycling.

**7.** LDL and atherosclerotic plaque — as in Dec 2017/Jan 2018 Q8b. Two ways to lower blood cholesterol: statins (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors) and dietary/lifestyle modification (reduced saturated fat intake, increased soluble fibre, regular exercise).

8a. Reactive oxygen species — as in Oct/Nov 2021 Q8c.

8b. **Respiratory burst** — in neutrophils/macrophages during phagocytosis, NADPH oxidase generates superoxide from O₂, converted to H₂O₂ and further to hypochlorous acid (via myeloperoxidase) for microbial killing.

8c. Cardiac markers — as in Mar/Apr 2021 Q8c.
