## March 10, 2022 (50 Marks)

### Section B

**1.** **Four immunoglobulins:** IgG, IgA, IgM, IgE (IgD is the 5th class).

**Structure of IgG:**
- Basic Y-shaped unit (monomer): 4 polypeptide chains — 2 **heavy chains (γ)** and 2 **light chains (κ or λ)**, held by disulfide bonds.
- **Fab region** (2 arms): Contains variable domains (VH + VL) → antigen-binding site; each IgG has 2 antigen-binding sites (**bivalent**).
- **Fc region** (tail, constant domain of heavy chains): Binds Fc receptors on phagocytes, activates complement (classical pathway).
- **Hinge region:** Between Fab and Fc; flexible; contains interchain disulfide bonds; cleaved by papain (→ 2 Fab + 1 Fc) or pepsin (→ 1 F(ab')₂ + pFc').

**Functions of IgG:**
- Most abundant Ig (~75% of serum); longest half-life (~23 days).
- **Opsonisation** — coats pathogens for phagocytosis.
- **Neutralisation** — blocks toxins and viral binding.
- **Complement activation** via classical pathway.
- **ADCC** (antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity).
- Only Ig to **cross the placenta** → passive neonatal immunity (via FcRn receptor).
- **Secondary immune response** predominant antibody.

**2.** **Differences between Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria:**

| Feature | Gram-Positive | Gram-Negative |
|---------|-------------|--------------|
| Cell wall thickness | Thick (20–80 nm peptidoglycan) | Thin (2–7 nm peptidoglycan) |
| Outer membrane | Absent | Present (LPS) |
| Teichoic acids | Present | Absent |
| Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) | Absent | Present (endotoxin) |
| Periplasmic space | Narrow | Wide |
| Staining result | Retains crystal violet — **purple** | Decolorised, takes safranin — **pink/red** |
| Toxin type | Mainly exotoxins | Endotoxin (LPS, lipid A) |
| Antibiotic sensitivity | Generally more sensitive to penicillin | Generally more resistant (outer membrane barrier) |
| Examples | *Staphylococcus, Streptococcus, Bacillus, Clostridium* | *E. coli, Pseudomonas, Salmonella, Klebsiella* |

**3.** **Life cycle of *Leishmania donovani*:**

*L. donovani* is the causative agent of **visceral leishmaniasis (kala-azar)**. Two forms:
- **Promastigote** (flagellate, extracellular): Present in sandfly gut; transmitted via sandfly bite into human skin.
- **Amastigote** (non-flagellate, intracellular): Multiplies within macrophages (reticuloendothelial system).

**Cycle in human host:** Sandfly bite → promastigotes injected → phagocytosed by macrophages → transform into amastigotes in phagolysosomes → multiply by binary fission → macrophage ruptures → amastigotes infect more macrophages → spread to liver, spleen, bone marrow.

**Cycle in vector (sandfly *Phlebotomus* spp.):** Sandfly feeds on infected host → amastigotes ingested → transform into promastigotes in gut → multiply, migrate to proboscis → transmitted on next bite.

**Laboratory diagnosis of blood parasites:**
- Peripheral blood smear (Giemsa-stained thick and thin films).
- Buffy coat examination.
- Serology: DAT, rK39 rapid antigen test (for leishmaniasis).
- PCR (molecular diagnosis).
- Culture (NNN medium for Leishmania; BACTEC for bacteraemia).
- Antigen detection (RDTs for malaria — HRP2 for *P. falciparum*).

**4. Short notes:**

**4a. Morphological classification of fungi**

Fungi classified morphologically as:
1. **Yeasts:** Unicellular; reproduce by budding; circular/oval; e.g., *Candida albicans*, *Cryptococcus neoformans*.
2. **Moulds (filamentous fungi):** Multicellular; grow as hyphae (septate or aseptate); form mycelium; e.g., *Aspergillus*, *Rhizopus*.
3. **Dimorphic fungi:** Both forms depending on temperature; yeast at 37°C (body temperature), mould at 25°C (room temperature) — "hot = yeast"; e.g., *Histoplasma capsulatum*, *Blastomyces dermatitidis*, *Coccidioides immitis*, *Sporothrix schenckii*.
4. **Yeasts with pseudohyphae:** e.g., *Candida* (forms pseudohyphae and true hyphae/germ tubes).

**4b. Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV)**

EBV (Human Herpesvirus 4, dsDNA, enveloped herpesvirus) infects B lymphocytes via CD21 (complement receptor 2). Causes:
- **Infectious mononucleosis** ("kissing disease," glandular fever): Fever, exudative pharyngitis, lymphadenopathy (posterior cervical), splenomegaly, atypical lymphocytes (reactive T cells), hepatitis; monospot test (heterophile antibody) positive.
- **Burkitt's lymphoma** (EBV + malaria → c-myc translocation, jaw tumour in Africa).
- **Nasopharyngeal carcinoma** (undifferentiated, common in SE Asia).
- **Hodgkin's lymphoma** (Reed-Sternberg cells EBV-positive in 50%).
- **Post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder** (PTLD) in immunosuppressed.

**Lab diagnosis:** Monospot test (heterophile antibody agglutination of horse RBCs), Paul-Bunnell test, EBV VCA-IgM and IgG (specific antibodies), PCR.

**4c. Exotoxin vs. Endotoxin:**

| Feature | Exotoxin | Endotoxin |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Source | Gram-positive AND Gram-negative bacteria | Only Gram-negative bacteria |
| Chemical nature | Proteins | Lipopolysaccharide (LPS, specifically Lipid A) |
| Heat stability | Mostly heat-labile (destroyed at 60°C) | Heat-stable (withstands 250°C) |
| Toxicity | Very high (nanogram doses lethal) | Lower; requires larger doses |
| Specificity | Highly specific (e.g., binds specific receptors) | Non-specific; general effects |
| Antigenicity | Highly antigenic; form toxoids | Weakly antigenic |
| Toxoid formation | Yes (diphtheria, tetanus toxoids) | No |
| Mode of action | Specific mechanisms (ADP-ribosylation, etc.) | Activates macrophages → cytokines → septic shock |
| Examples | Diphtheria toxin, tetanus toxin, cholera toxin, TSST-1 | LPS of E. coli, Salmonella, Pseudomonas |

### Section C

**5.** **Pathogens causing pneumonia:**
- *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (commonest community-acquired)
- *Haemophilus influenzae*
- *Staphylococcus aureus*
- *Legionella pneumophila* (atypical)
- *Mycoplasma pneumoniae* (atypical)
- *Klebsiella pneumoniae* (aspiration, alcoholics)
- *Aspergillus* spp. (immunocompromised)
- Respiratory viruses (influenza, SARS-CoV-2)

**Pathogenesis of pulmonary aspergillosis:**
*Aspergillus fumigatus* (commonest) spores are inhaled → most are killed by mucociliary clearance and alveolar macrophages. In **immunocompromised** (neutropenic) patients: conidia germinate into hyphae → invade blood vessels → angioinvasion → haematogenous spread → haemorrhagic infarcts with the characteristic "halo sign" on CT (ground-glass halo around nodule) → "air crescent sign" as lung tissue necroses.

**Laboratory diagnosis of pulmonary aspergillosis:**
- **Microscopy:** BAL or sputum — KOH/calcofluor white stain; septate hyphae branching at acute angles (45°).
- **Culture:** Czapek-Dox agar or SDA; *A. fumigatus* grows as blue-green colonies.
- **Histopathology:** GMS (Gomori methenamine silver) or PAS staining of tissue.
- **Antigen detection:** Serum/BAL **galactomannan** (ELISA — sensitivity >70% in invasive aspergillosis).
- **Beta-D-glucan assay** (pan-fungal marker).
- **CT chest:** Halo sign early, air-crescent sign late.

**6.** **Causative agents of infective endocarditis (IE):**
- **Native valve:** *Streptococcus viridans* (most common, dental procedures), *Staphylococcus aureus* (acute, aggressive), Enterococci.
- **Prosthetic valve (early < 2 months):** *S. aureus*, *S. epidermidis*, Gram-negative organisms, fungi.
- **IVDU:** *S. aureus* (most common, tricuspid valve predominantly).
- **Culture-negative:** HACEK organisms (*Haemophilus*, *Actinobacillus*, *Cardiobacterium*, *Eikenella*, *Kingella*), *Bartonella*, *Coxiella burnetii* (Q fever).

**Pathogenesis:** Underlying valve damage (rheumatic, congenital) or turbulent flow → endothelial damage → platelet-fibrin thrombus (non-bacterial thrombotic endocarditis/NBTE) → transient bacteraemia → bacteria colonise NBTE → **vegetation** (fibrin-platelet-bacteria matrix) grows on valve leaflets → valve destruction, emboli (septic emboli to brain, kidney, spleen), immune complex deposition (Osler nodes, Janeway lesions, Roth spots, glomerulonephritis).

**7.** **Pathogenesis of Streptococcal sore throat (*S. pyogenes* / Group A Streptococcus):**

**Virulence factors:** M protein (antiphagocytic, anti-complement), hyaluronidase, streptolysin O and S, streptokinase, DNase, SPE (streptococcal pyrogenic exotoxins → scarlet fever).

**Pathogenesis:** *S. pyogenes* aerosol transmission → adherence to pharyngeal epithelium (via LTA + M protein to fibronectin) → local inflammation (pharyngitis, tonsillitis) → M protein prevents phagocytosis → spreads to lymph nodes → exotoxins cause systemic effects. Anti-M-protein antibodies cross-react with cardiac antigens (molecular mimicry) → **rheumatic fever** (if untreated).

**Laboratory diagnosis:**
- **Throat swab:** Inoculated onto **blood agar** → large, translucent colonies with wide zone of **β-haemolysis** → Gram stain (Gram-positive cocci in chains) → catalase-negative (distinguishes from Staph) → **bacitracin sensitivity** (S. pyogenes is sensitive, other beta-haemolytic Strep resistant) → **lancefield grouping** (anti-A serum — latex agglutination).
- **Rapid antigen test (RADT):** Streptococcal antigen from swab; high specificity but lower sensitivity than culture.
- **ASO titre (anti-streptolysin O):** Rises 1–3 weeks post-infection; elevated in rheumatic fever.

**8. Short notes:**

**8a. Pathogenesis of *Mycobacterium tuberculosis***

*M. tuberculosis* (acid-fast bacillus) spread by airborne droplet nuclei → alveoli → phagocytosed by alveolar macrophages → survives intracellularly by: (i) preventing phagolysosome fusion (cord factor — trehalose dimycolate), (ii) producing catalase-peroxidase (katG) to neutralise ROS.

Cell-mediated immunity (CD4⁺ Th1 cells → IFN-γ) activates macrophages → granuloma formation: central **caseous necrosis** + epithelioid macrophages + Langerhans giant cells + lymphocytes. In primary infection, lesion heals → calcified Ghon focus + calcified hilar nodes (Ghon complex). Reactivation (immunosuppression) → cavitation, caseation, spread via bronchi, blood (miliary TB).

**8b. Antigenic variation in influenza virus**

Influenza A virus surface has **haemagglutinin (HA)** and **neuraminidase (NA)** antigens.
- **Antigenic drift:** Small point mutations in HA/NA genes due to RNA polymerase error-prone replication → gradual antigenic change → seasonal epidemics; overcome by annual influenza vaccine update.
- **Antigenic shift:** Reassortment of gene segments between different influenza A strains (from different species — human, avian, swine) in a cell doubly infected with two different influenza A subtypes → entirely new HA and/or NA subtype to which no population immunity exists → **pandemic** influenza (e.g., 1918 Spanish flu H1N1, 2009 swine flu H1N1, avian flu H5N1).

**8c. Laboratory diagnosis of Rheumatic Heart Disease**

RHD is diagnosed clinically (Jones criteria) but lab confirms preceding streptococcal infection:
- **Throat swab culture:** May or may not be positive (disease starts 2–3 weeks after infection).
- **Rapid antigen test (RADT)** for Group A Strep.
- **ASO titre (anti-streptolysin O):** Most used; >200 Todd units in adults, >333 in children — elevated in 80–85%.
- **Anti-DNase B titre:** More sensitive for skin infections.
- **Anti-hyaluronidase titre.**
- **CRP, ESR:** Elevated (acute phase reactants during active carditis).
- **Echocardiography:** For cardiac involvement (not microbiological).

**2.** **Laboratory diagnosis of malaria:**

**Direct methods:**
- **Peripheral blood smear** (gold standard): Thick film (sensitive, screening) + thin film (speciation). Giemsa-stained. Identify: ring stage trophozoites, schizonts (not seen in *P. falciparum* peripheral blood), gametocytes. *P. falciparum*: multiple rings/cell, banana-shaped gametocytes, Maurer's clefts. *P. vivax*: Schüffner's stippling, amoeboid trophozoites, enlarged RBCs.
- **Quantitative Buffy Coat (QBC):** Acridine orange staining + fluorescence microscopy; detects parasitaemia.

**Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs):**
- Immunochromatographic strip detecting *P. falciparum* HRP-2 antigen or pan-malarial aldolase; results in 15–30 minutes.

**Molecular methods:**
- PCR: High sensitivity/specificity; detects low parasitaemia; speciation; detects drug-resistant mutations.

**Serology:** Not useful for acute diagnosis (antibodies persist after cure).

**3.** **Antigen-antibody tests for infectious diseases:**
Antigen tests: RDT (HRP-2 for malaria), HBsAg (hepatitis B), HIV p24 antigen, galactomannan (aspergillosis).
Antibody tests: ELISA (HIV, hepatitis), ASO titre (streptococcal), RPR/VDRL (syphilis), Widal test (typhoid), monospot (EBV).

**ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay):**
1. Antigen coated on well → 2. Patient serum added (antibodies bind) → 3. Wash → 4. Enzyme-linked secondary antibody added → 5. Wash → 6. Substrate added → enzyme converts substrate to **coloured product** → absorbance read by spectrophotometer. **Indirect ELISA** (detects antibodies); **Sandwich ELISA** (detects antigen). Highly sensitive and specific; suitable for mass screening (HIV, HBsAg).

**4. Short notes:**

**4a. Antimicrobial susceptibility test**
Methods: **Disk diffusion (Kirby-Bauer)** — antibiotic-impregnated disks placed on inoculated agar; zone of inhibition measured and compared to CLSI/EUCAST breakpoints → Sensitive (S), Intermediate (I), Resistant (R). **MIC (Minimum Inhibitory Concentration)** — broth dilution or E-test strip; lowest concentration inhibiting visible growth. **Automated systems** (VITEK, MicroScan). Guides antibiotic choice; detects resistance patterns (e.g., MRSA, ESBL).

**4b. Contribution of Louis Pasteur**
French chemist/microbiologist: (i) **Disproved spontaneous generation** — swan-neck flask experiment (1859). (ii) **Germ theory of disease** — microorganisms cause fermentation and disease. (iii) **Pasteurisation** — partial sterilisation by heating to kill pathogens without altering product. (iv) **Developed vaccines** — rabies vaccine (attenuated), anthrax vaccine (attenuated). (v) Identified *Streptococcus* in puerperal fever. (vi) Discovered anaerobic bacteria.

### Section C

**5.** **Classification of Streptococci:**
- **Haemolytic classification:** β-haemolytic (complete clearing of RBCs — Group A: *S. pyogenes*, Group B: *S. agalactiae*, Group C, Group G), α-haemolytic (partial, greening — *S. pneumoniae*, viridans group), γ-haemolytic (non-haemolytic — Enterococcus).
- **Lancefield grouping** (carbohydrate C antigen in cell wall): Groups A–H, K–V.

**Pathogenesis of rheumatic heart disease:**
*S. pyogenes* pharyngitis (untreated) → immune response against M-protein epitopes → **molecular mimicry** — anti-M-protein antibodies cross-react with cardiac proteins (cardiac myosin, laminin, vimentin) → **autoimmune carditis** → pancarditis (endo-, myo-, and pericarditis) → vegetation on mitral/aortic valves → valve leaflet thickening and scarring → stenosis or regurgitation.

**6.** **Virulence factors of *Streptococcus pneumoniae*:**
- **Capsule** (most important — polysaccharide; antiphagocytic; basis of vaccine development — 23 serotypes in PPSV23, 13 in PCV13).
- **Pneumolysin** (cholesterol-binding cytotoxin — destroys ciliated epithelium, inhibits complement, activates macrophages).
- **Neuraminidase** (promotes adherence and spread).
- **Autolysin** (LytA — releases cell wall components that activate complement; causes lysis and inflammation).
- **Phosphorylcholine** (choline on cell wall binds platelet-activating factor receptor on epithelium — facilitates adherence and invasion).
- **Surface proteins** (PspA, PspC) — inhibit complement.
- **IgA protease** — destroys secretory IgA.

**Laboratory diagnosis of *S. pneumoniae*:**
- **Gram stain of sputum:** Gram-positive diplococci (lancet-shaped pairs).
- **Culture:** Chocolate agar or blood agar; α-haemolytic (viridans-like) green colonies.
- **Bile solubility test:** *S. pneumoniae* colonies dissolve in 10% bile (bile-soluble — positive) → distinguishes from viridans Strep (bile-insoluble).
- **Optochin sensitivity:** Inhibited by optochin disk (zone >14 mm) — viridans Strep resistant.
- **Quellung reaction (Neufeld):** Capsular swelling in type-specific antisera — typing.
- **Urine antigen test:** Immunochromatographic assay for pneumococcal polysaccharide antigen.

**7.** **Pathogenesis and laboratory diagnosis of *Bordetella pertussis* (whooping cough):**

**Pathogenesis:** Respiratory droplet transmission → adherence to ciliated respiratory epithelium (via filamentous haemagglutinin, pertactin, fimbriae) → local invasion → **pertussis toxin (PT)**: ADP-ribosylates Gi protein → ↑ cAMP → impairs phagocyte function, promotes lymphocytosis, insulin secretion. **Tracheal cytotoxin** and **adenylate cyclase toxin** destroy ciliated cells → loss of mucociliary clearance → mucus accumulation → paroxysmal cough. Three stages: Catarrhal (1–2 weeks, most infectious), **Paroxysmal** (2–4 weeks, characteristic "whoop" on inspiration after coughing fits), Convalescent.

**Laboratory diagnosis:**
- **Nasopharyngeal swab** (Dacron swab — Calcium alginate/wooden swabs inhibit growth).
- **Culture:** Bordet-Gengou agar (potato-blood-glycerol) or Regan-Lowe medium; pearly, mercury drop colonies; takes 3–7 days.
- **PCR** (most sensitive, especially after antibiotic treatment).
- **Serology:** Anti-PT IgA (useful in later stages/convalescent phase).
- **Direct fluorescence antibody (DFA):** Rapid but low sensitivity.
- **CBC:** Marked lymphocytosis (absolute lymphocyte count often >15,000/μL).

**8. Short notes:**

**8a. Diphtheria (*Corynebacterium diphtheriae*)**
Gram-positive club-shaped bacillus (pleomorphic); **metachromatic granules** (Babes-Ernst granules, stained with Albert's stain). **Toxin:** Diphtheria toxin (phage-encoded, tox gene): A-B toxin; B fragment binds cell receptors (HB-EGF receptor); A subunit ADP-ribosylates **EF-2** (elongation factor 2) → inhibits protein synthesis → cell death. **Clinical:** Exudative pharyngitis with **grey-white pseudomembrane** (fibrin-WBC-bacteria) on tonsils/pharynx — bleeds on removal; bull neck (cervical lymphadenopathy); myocarditis (most common cause of death); peripheral neuropathy; palatal palsy. **Lab:** Throat swab → Loeffler's serum slope/Tellurite agar → Albert's stain → Elek immunoprecipitation test (toxigenicity). **Prevention:** DTaP/Td vaccine.

**8b. Infective Endocarditis**
(See March 10, 2022 Q6 above — pathogenesis and causative agents.)

**8c. Laboratory diagnosis of pulmonary tuberculosis**
- **Sputum collection:** 3 specimens (spot-early morning-spot); ideal 5–10 mL.
- **Smear microscopy:** ZN stain (carbol fuchsin heated, acid-alcohol decolorisaiton, methylene blue counterstain) — red AFBs on blue background. **Fluorescence microscopy** (auramine-rhodamine stain): More sensitive, faster.
- **Culture (gold standard):** LJ (Lowenstein-Jensen) solid medium — slow (6–8 weeks), characteristic buff-coloured rough colonies. **BACTEC MGIT 960** (liquid medium) — 2 weeks; automated, sensitive.
- **Molecular:** **Xpert MTB/RIF** (GeneXpert PCR) — detects *M. tuberculosis* DNA and rifampicin resistance in 2 hours; WHO-endorsed.
- **Drug sensitivity testing (DST):** Solid or liquid media; phenotypic or molecular (line probe assay).
- **Tuberculin skin test (Mantoux):** Detects prior sensitisation, not active disease.
- **IGRA (Interferon-Gamma Release Assay):** QuantiFERON-TB Gold — detects Th1 response to ESAT-6/CFP-10; not confounded by BCG.

### Section B

**1.** **Sterilization:** Complete destruction or removal of all microorganisms including spores from an object.

**Moist heat methods:**
1. **Autoclaving (Steam under pressure):** 121°C at 15 psi for 15–20 minutes (or 134°C for 3 min). Kills all vegetative organisms AND spores. Mechanism: Protein denaturation and coagulation. Uses: Surgical instruments, culture media, dressings, IV fluids. **Most reliable method of sterilization.**
2. **Boiling (100°C, 30 min):** Kills vegetative organisms and some viruses; does NOT kill spores. Disinfection only.
3. **Pasteurisation (63°C for 30 min/72°C for 15 sec):** Kills pathogens in milk/beverages; not sterilization.
4. **Inspissation/Tyndallisation (fractional sterilization):** 56–80°C on 3 consecutive days; kills vegetative forms each day while spores germinate → killed next day. For heat-sensitive media (serum, egg-based — e.g., Lowenstein-Jensen).

**2.** **Types of immunity:**
- **Innate immunity** (non-specific, first line): Physical barriers (skin, mucous membranes), natural killer cells, complement (alternative pathway), phagocytes, acute-phase proteins, inflammation. No memory. Present from birth.
- **Adaptive immunity** (specific, acquired): Humoral (B cells, antibodies — protects against extracellular pathogens) and Cellular (T cells — protects against intracellular pathogens, cancer). Has **immunological memory**.
- **Natural immunity:** Innate + natural exposure-induced adaptive immunity.
- **Artificial immunity:** Active (vaccination) or Passive (administration of immunoglobulins/antiserum). Active immunity provides long-lasting protection; passive is immediate but short-lived.

(See March 10, 2022 Q3 above — peripheral smear, QBC, RDT, PCR.)

**4. Short notes:**

**4a. Bacterial growth curve**

When bacteria are inoculated into a suitable liquid medium (batch culture), growth follows four phases:
1. **Lag phase:** Bacteria adapting to new environment; synthesising enzymes, RNA; no increase in cell number; duration depends on inoculum size and media.
2. **Log (exponential) phase:** Rapid, exponential cell division at maximum rate; generation time is constant; metabolically most active; most susceptible to antibiotics.
3. **Stationary phase:** Growth rate = death rate; nutrients depleted, toxic metabolites accumulate; total number constant.
4. **Decline (death) phase:** Death rate exceeds growth; loss of viability; exponential decrease in cell count.

*(Draw: sigmoidal bacterial growth curve with four labelled phases.)*

**4b. *P. falciparum* vs. *P. vivax*:**

| Feature | *P. falciparum* | *P. vivax* |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| Fever pattern | Irregular or tertian (48h) | Benign tertian (48h) |
| RBC infected | Any age; multiple rings/cell | Young/reticulocytes only; one ring/cell |
| RBC size | Normal | Enlarged |
| Schüffner's dots | Absent (Maurer's clefts) | Present |
| Gametocytes | Banana-shaped (crescent) | Round |
| Peripheral blood | Only early ring forms | All stages seen |
| Dormant liver stage | No hypnozoites | **Hypnozoites** (relapses) |
| Severity | Most severe (cerebral malaria, blackwater fever, ARDS) | Benign |
| Drug resistance | Chloroquine-resistant common | Mostly chloroquine-sensitive |
| Specific treatment | Artemisinin-based combination | Chloroquine + Primaquine (for hypnozoites) |

**4c. Methods of anaerobiasis**
Techniques to maintain anaerobic conditions for culture of anaerobes:
1. **Anaerobic jar (McIntosh-Fildes jar):** Filled with N₂/CO₂/H₂ mixture; H₂ reacts with residual O₂ over a palladium catalyst → water; methylene blue indicator becomes colourless (anaerobic).
2. **Anaerobic chamber (glove box):** Maintains constant anaerobic atmosphere; best for obligate anaerobes.
3. **GasPak system:** Disposable envelope releases H₂ + CO₂ when water added; removes O₂ via palladium catalyst.
4. **Candle jar (Thayer-Martin):** Creates 5–10% CO₂ environment by burning a candle (for microaerophiles like *N. gonorrhoeae*; not true anaerobic).
5. **Layering with liquid paraffin or mineral oil:** Seals liquid culture tubes from O₂.
6. **Reducing agents** (thioglycollate broth, cysteine) in media.

## January 9, 2019 (December 2018/January 2019 Session, 50 Marks)

### Section B

**1.** **Antibiotics:** Chemical substances produced by microorganisms (or synthetically) that inhibit or destroy other microorganisms at low concentrations.

**Mechanisms of action of antimicrobial agents:**
1. **Inhibition of cell wall synthesis:** β-lactams (penicillins, cephalosporins — inhibit transpeptidase/PBPs), vancomycin (inhibits transglycosylation by binding D-Ala-D-Ala).
2. **Disruption of cell membrane:** Polymyxins (disrupt Gram-negative outer membrane + cytoplasmic membrane), amphotericin B (binds ergosterol in fungal membrane).
3. **Inhibition of protein synthesis:** 30S: aminoglycosides (irreversible — misreading), tetracyclines (reversible — block tRNA binding). 50S: macrolides, chloramphenicol (inhibit peptidyl transferase), clindamycin, linezolid.
4. **Inhibition of nucleic acid synthesis:** Fluoroquinolones (inhibit DNA gyrase/topoisomerase IV), rifampicin (inhibits RNA polymerase), metronidazole (damages DNA in anaerobes).
5. **Inhibition of metabolic pathways (antimetabolites):** Sulfonamides + trimethoprim (sequential block of folate synthesis pathway).

**2.** **Antisepsis** (use of chemical agents on living tissue to inhibit microorganisms without necessarily killing them) vs. **Disinfection** (chemical/physical process that eliminates most but not necessarily all microorganisms on inanimate objects; does not kill spores).

**Chemical disinfectants:**
- **Alcohols** (70% ethanol, isopropanol): Protein denaturation; skin antiseptic, hand rubs.
- **Aldehydes** (glutaraldehyde 2%, formaldehyde): Alkylate proteins/nucleic acids; high-level disinfection/sterilization of endoscopes.
- **Halogens** (bleach/NaOCl, iodine/iodophors): Oxidising agents; used in water purification, wound antisepsis.
- **Phenolics** (e.g., Lysol): Disrupt cell membrane; surface disinfection.
- **Quaternary ammonium compounds (QACs):** Disrupt membrane; low-level disinfectants; effective against Gram-positive bacteria.
- **Chlorhexidine:** Disrupts membrane; surgical scrub, wound antiseptic; active against Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria.

**3.** Immunoglobulins defined and classified: (See March 10, 2022 Q1 — IgG structure; plus:)

| Class | Structure | Half-life | Key Features |
|-------|---------|----------|-------------|
| IgG | Monomer | 23 days | Most abundant; crosses placenta; complement activation; 4 subclasses |
| IgA | Dimer (secretory) or monomer | 6 days | Mucosal immunity; breast milk; does NOT activate complement |
| IgM | Pentamer | 5 days | First produced; strongest complement activator; agglutination; natural ABO antibodies |
| IgE | Monomer | 2 days | Allergy (Type I hypersensitivity — binds FcεRI on mast cells/basophils); anti-helminthic |
| IgD | Monomer | 3 days | B-cell surface receptor; function largely unknown |

**4. Short notes:**

**4a. Type 4 hypersensitivity (Delayed-type hypersensitivity, DTH)**
Cell-mediated immune reaction; **not mediated by antibodies**; T cells and macrophages are effectors. Onset: 24–72 hours after antigen exposure (hence "delayed").

Types:
- **Contact dermatitis:** Sensitised T cells (CD4⁺ Th1) recognise hapten (e.g., nickel, poison ivy, rubber) presented on skin DCs → cytokine release → inflammation.
- **Tuberculin reaction (Mantoux test):** PPD injection → existing memory T cells → local swelling and induration at 48–72 h.
- **Granulomatous hypersensitivity:** Persistent antigen (TB, leprosy, schistosomiasis) → sustained Th1 response → granuloma formation (macrophage → epithelioid cells → Langerhans giant cells).
- **Contact hypersensitivity to metals (Ni, Co, Cr).**

**4b. Kala-azar (Visceral Leishmaniasis)**
Caused by *Leishmania donovani* (Indian subcontinent, East Africa) transmitted by the bite of female sandfly (*Phlebotomus* spp.). Parasitises macrophages of the reticuloendothelial system (liver, spleen, bone marrow, lymph nodes). Clinical features: Gradual onset fever (double daily spike — "duple fever"), progressive **hepatosplenomegaly** (massive), wasting, anaemia (bone marrow infiltration), pancytopenia, hypergammaglobulinaemia, **darkening of skin** ("kala-azar" = black fever in Hindi). Fatal if untreated. Lab: amastigotes in bone marrow/splenic aspirate, DAT (direct agglutination test), rK39 strip test. Treatment: liposomal amphotericin B (drug of choice), miltefosine.

**4c. Bacteraemia**
Presence of viable bacteria in the bloodstream, usually transient. Caused by: dental procedures, GI/GU manipulation, IV line insertion, surgical procedures. Most are cleared rapidly by the reticuloendothelial system. Distinguished from **septicaemia** (bacteria multiplying in blood, causing systemic signs/sepsis) and **septic shock** (sepsis + refractory hypotension). Bacteraemia can seed remote sites (heart valve → endocarditis, bone → osteomyelitis, joint → septic arthritis).

## January 6, 2017 (December 2016/January 2017 Session, 50 Marks)

### Section B

**1.** **Robert Koch's contributions:**
1. Established the **germ theory of infectious disease**.
2. **Koch's postulates** (criteria for establishing causation): (i) The organism must be found in all cases of the disease. (ii) The organism must be isolated in pure culture. (iii) The isolated organism must produce the disease when inoculated into healthy host. (iv) The organism must be re-isolated from the experimentally diseased host.
3. Discovered the causative agents of: Tuberculosis (*M. tuberculosis*, 1882), Cholera (*Vibrio cholerae*, 1883), Anthrax (*Bacillus anthracis*).
4. Developed solid culture media (agar plates), pure culture techniques.
5. Developed **Koch-Henle postulates**, staining techniques.

**2.** **Antigen:** Any substance (usually protein or polysaccharide) that can induce a specific immune response and react with the products of that response (antibodies, sensitised T cells).

**Conditions of antigenicity (immunogenicity):**
1. **Foreignness:** Non-self; self-antigens are normally tolerised.
2. **Molecular weight:** >10,000 Da; smaller molecules (haptens) are antigenic only when coupled to a carrier.
3. **Chemical complexity:** Aromatic amino acids, branched chains increase antigenicity.
4. **Biodegradability:** Must be processed by APCs (if indigestible, poor antigen).
5. **Route of administration:** Subcutaneous/intradermal → best response.
6. **Dose:** Too little or too much → tolerance; optimal dose → strong response.
7. **Adjuvants:** Substances that enhance immunogenicity (e.g., alum, Freund's adjuvant).

**3.** Life cycle and laboratory diagnosis of *Plasmodium falciparum*:

**Life cycle in human:** (i) Sporozoites injected by female *Anopheles* mosquito → liver (hepatocytes) — **exo-erythrocytic schizogony** → tissue schizonts → merozoites released → invade RBCs. (ii) In RBC — **erythrocytic schizogony**: ring trophozoite → mature trophozoite → schizont (16–24 merozoites for *P. falciparum*) → RBC rupture (48h for *P. falciparum*) → fever paroxysm. Some merozoites → **gametocytes** (banana/crescent-shaped for *P. falciparum*). **No hypnozoites** in *P. falciparum* (no relapse).

**Life cycle in mosquito:** Gametocytes ingested with blood meal → form gametes → fertilisation → **ookinete** → penetrates midgut wall → **oocyst** → **sporozoites** released → migrate to salivary glands → injected at next blood meal.

### Key answers (topics not covered above):

**Sterilization — Hot Air Oven:** Dry heat method. **160°C for 60 minutes** or 170°C for 30 minutes. Mechanism: Protein oxidation and denaturation. Advantages: No moisture, no rust, penetrates oils/powders/glassware. Disadvantages: Slow, damages rubber/plastics. Uses: Glass Petri dishes, test tubes, syringes, metal instruments, petroleum jelly, talc powder.

**Life cycle and lab diagnosis of *Wuchereria bancrofti*:** Filarial nematode causing lymphatic filariasis. Infective stage (L3 larvae) transmitted by *Culex* mosquito bite → lymphatics → adult worms (in lymphatics, lymph nodes) → microfilariae (in blood, nocturnal periodicity: peak 10pm–2am). Clinical: Lymphoedema, elephantiasis. Lab: Nocturnal blood smear (Giemsa) for microfilariae; antigen detection (filarial card test — circulating filarial antigen, no periodicity required); eosinophilia.

**Vaccines:**
- **Live attenuated:** BCG (TB), MMR, varicella, yellow fever — strong, long-lasting immunity; contraindicated in immunocompromised.
- **Killed/inactivated:** Pertussis (wP), hepatitis A, IPV, influenza (injected) — safer, multiple doses needed.
- **Toxoid:** Diphtheria, tetanus — inactivated toxin; excellent immunity.
- **Subunit/conjugate:** Hepatitis B, acellular pertussis (aP), Hib, pneumococcal, HPV — specific antigens.
- **mRNA vaccines:** COVID-19 (Pfizer-BioNTech, Moderna) — encode spike protein.

**Histoplasma capsulatum:** Dimorphic fungus (mould in soil, yeast at 37°C). Transmitted by inhalation of spores from bat/bird droppings-contaminated soil (endemic in Ohio/Mississippi river valley, parts of Asia). Clinical: Pulmonary (self-limiting like flu in immunocompetent; progressive in immunocompromised — AIDS-defining illness). Lab: Culture on SDA; microscopy (macroconidia 8–14 µm, tuberculate); in tissue — small intracellular yeast within macrophages (GMS stain); urine/serum antigen (Histoplasma polysaccharide antigen, most sensitive); complement fixation serology; skin test.

**Cryptococcus neoformans:** Encapsulated yeast; AIDS-defining opportunistic infection. Lab: India ink preparation (clear halo/capsule around yeast); CSF for meningitis (India ink + cryptococcal antigen). Culture on SDA or bird-seed agar (brown colonies via laccase).

## September 16, 2015 — New Course (50 Marks)

**Gram stain mechanism:** Crystal violet applied → iodine (mordant) forms crystal violet-iodine complex → decolorised with alcohol/acetone → Gram-positive (thick peptidoglycan wall retains CVI complex → stays purple); Gram-negative (thin peptidoglycan + outer membrane dissolved by alcohol → decolorised → takes safranin/fuchsin counterstain → pink).

*(Diagram: Gram-positive — thick peptidoglycan, no outer membrane. Gram-negative — thin peptidoglycan, periplasmic space, outer membrane with LPS.)*

**Genetic material transfer in bacteria:**
1. **Transformation:** Uptake of naked DNA from environment by naturally competent bacteria (e.g., *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*).
2. **Conjugation:** Direct cell-to-cell contact via sex pilus (F factor/plasmid-mediated). (See Jul 13, 2018 Q4a above.)
3. **Transduction:** Bacteriophage (phage) carries bacterial DNA from donor to recipient. Generalised (any bacterial gene — headful packaging) vs. Specialised (specific host genes adjacent to phage integration site — e.g., diphtheria toxin gene by beta-phage).
4. **Transposition:** Movement of transposons (jumping genes) within or between DNA molecules.

**Properties of oncogenic viruses:**
Oncogenic (tumourigenic) viruses — cause cancer by: (i) Integration of viral genes into host genome → insertion mutagenesis or continuous proliferation signals. (ii) Viral oncogenes (v-onc) or transactivation of cellular proto-oncogenes (c-onc). (iii) Inactivation of tumour-suppressor genes (p53, Rb). Examples: HPV (cervical cancer — E6 inhibits p53, E7 inhibits Rb), EBV (Burkitt's lymphoma, NPC), HBV/HCV (hepatocellular carcinoma), HTLV-1 (adult T-cell leukaemia), HHV-8 (Kaposi's sarcoma).

**Lab diagnosis of filariasis:** Nocturnal blood smear (10pm–2am for *W. bancrofti*) for sheathed microfilariae; thick Giemsa film or Knott's concentration technique; antigen detection (filarial card test — circulating filarial antigen) by day; PCR for species-specific diagnosis.
